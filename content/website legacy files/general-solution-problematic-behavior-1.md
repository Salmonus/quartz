# A General Solution for Human Problematic Behavior (1): Focusing on the Case of 19-year-old Kim Yeon-woo

**By:** Kim Yeon-woo

**Platform:** Brunch

**Note:** *Independent Post*

### I. The Lighthouse of the Spirit

"Love" is a very specific emotion. If you are experiencing it, you know it instantly. Yet, the entire canon of French literature was created to express that one specific feeling. Even now, writers continue to churn out books. Why do I mention this? It is to convey my own current state. What I want to communicate in this text is a very specific structure of thought.

What is love? It might be the desire to be near someone, or the wish to be kind to them. Actually, that explanation is wrong. Those are characteristics of love, not love itself. Writers rely on such secondary descriptions because that is the work of artists. Like "Love," if I had to compress the aspect of the human spirit I am trying to convey into a single word, it would be **"Rationality."** This is the 28th entry in this archive, and the theme I declared in the very first post was also "Rationality." This means I have been struggling for four months to convey the weight of that one word. Is it not finished yet? No, it is not. When will literature about love end? Likely only on the day humanity ends. In my view, Rationality is a vaster subject than Love. While love is a part of emotion, Rationality is the lighthouse in the ocean of the human spirit, encompassing both reason and sensibility.

### II. Figure, Ground, and the Mental Optician

It is 4:00 PM. It is still bright outside, and I am sitting in a corner of a Starbucks. Why am I here? As I write, this starts to feel like literature. Perhaps that is because literature is a medium that allows us to easily pull out difficult subjects that the human mind struggles to capture in a purely sane state. But I will stop writing "like literature." That doesn't mean this is non-fiction. This is just a text.

In Douglas Hofstadter’s first book, the concepts of **Figure** and **Ground** appear. You have likely heard of convergent and divergent thinking. I came to Starbucks to engage in **divergent thinking**. Why? Because divergent thinking is a mental activity that treats the *Self* as the Figure and everything else as the Ground. **Convergent thinking** is the opposite: it treats the *Other* as the Figure and the *Self* as the Ground.

These terms—Figure and Ground—were not invented by Hofstadter, nor Kant, Descartes, or Plato. Of course, they all called them by different names in their own languages. They were the **Opticians** of their eras. An optician is someone who adjusts the focus for people whose eyes have failed. Vision usually breaks in three ways: **Myopia** (near-sightedness), **Hyperopia** (far-sightedness), and **Astigmatism**.

So, what does divergent thinking have to do with Starbucks? It doesn’t have to be Starbucks. It just needs to be an environment with many people and blurred surroundings, like a park or a plaza. In a high-noise environment, it is easier to treat the surroundings as the **Ground**. My mind is already trained; the moment I enter such a place, it automatically processes the environment this way. Then, I can simply sit down and start writing.

Conversely, when I need to focus on something other than myself, a study hall is better. Such places are structured to demand focus on the "Ground," making it hard to treat surroundings as a mere backdrop. A mind that cannot focus on two things at once naturally excludes the Self as the Figure, allowing for total focus on the task. This is why I never study for the CSAT in a cafe, and I never write in a study hall. I like toggling between these two focal points.

Is the ability to freely adjust the focus of things not the core skill of an optician? Indeed. A philosopher is nothing other than a **Mental Optician**. They criticize those who try to see only what is too close, and those who look only too far. Ultimately, the goal is to see what is right in front of you clearly and live well. But even that depends on context. The ideal vision for someone living on the Mongolian steppe is naturally different from someone in the middle of Manhattan. This metaphor implies that opticians must change according to the context of the era.

### III. The Habit of Thought

Readers of my last three paragraphs will easily notice my speech habits. *Therefore, so, it is, because, the reason is.* This is how I think. Most people try to hide these transitions when they write, and most essay academies teach them to do so. In fact, most literati would judge my writing as "bad." I claim to write exactly as my keyboard dictates, I do not proofread, and I express my emotions with excessive honesty. It didn’t even take 20 minutes to get this far. Shouldn’t I try harder? Spend more time?

I think that’s all nonsense. First, one must live well. The spirit of a person who has lived well is rational—whether they were born with "good vision" or created the right lens through tireless effort. Such people don't even need to write. As I stated in Part 8, writing is a self-editing technology for thought; those whose thoughts are already straight have no need to "correct" them.

Then why do I write? First, I am an adolescent; I must be clear that my lenses are not yet finished. Second, I am an optician. Opticians, rarely among those who live "straight" lives, write for the public. Such writings are usually contradictory. "The Public" is a non-existent concept, and "popular philosophy books" are like kits containing as many lenses as possible. Foolish people take out the one lens that lets them see what they *want* to see and then dismiss the entire kit, complaining, "I can't see X with this lens!" Such fools only prove the book's fame. Eventually, the author is labeled a "Master of X-ism." People use the book to reinforce their own biases and then wrap themselves in the label of having read "X-ism." This is why Sartre hated the "Self-Taught Man." This is why he fought against Humanism! He didn't write non-fiction, or literature, or Existentialist literature—he just wrote. He simply wanted people to read it. That is the highest praise a text can receive.

### IV. The Case of 19-Year-Old Kim Yeon-woo

Let’s return to the three types of broken vision: Myopia, Hyperopia, and Astigmatism. By now, everyone knows this is a metaphor for Rationality. I am not the first to use it; the community *LessWrong* saw a post with a similar title in 2006. Even then, that person wasn't the first. The oldest I know is Kant. I am simply expressing the world as I see it, at the level of a Korean teenager.

* **Myopia (Near-sightedness):** A mental rigidity that stays in one "layer" of thought for too long.
* **Hyperopia (Far-sightedness):** A spirit with too much flexibility, taking thought layers too far away from the self.
* **Astigmatism:** A distractibility that cannot anchor itself to any layer, constantly fluctuating.

All three states prevent one from seeing the world correctly and, consequently, from living well. They are illnesses of the mind. When these illnesses fester, they lead to "problematic behavior."

I will now propose a general solution for human problematic behavior, centered on the case of 19-year-old Kim Yeon-woo.

Recently, Kim Yeon-woo exhibited several problematic behaviors. First, he threatened his own parents with a knife, and eventually, he fled school and attempted to cut the artery in his wrist. Like a dog that turns on its owner due to a lack of walks, the problem surfaced. Why?

To state the conclusion: **He had Hyperopia.** Over the past few months, he became addicted to meta-cognition and tried to look only at the far distance.

Why do I use the term "problematic behavior"? That is a word usually reserved for pets, not humans. I use it because, just as almost all problematic behavior in dogs is solved by more walks, Kim Yeon-woo’s behavior stemmed from a **lack of physical activity.** Physical activity—exercise—is one of the most effective methods of **Grounding**. When the layers of thought become too high and you drift away from yourself, the way to return instantly is to do something that makes you breathless and makes your muscles tremble. It is running until the body heats up and lifting heavy weights. It is eating something after a cold shower.

Why? Radical behavior is usually caused by depression. And depression is a **learned helplessness** where a person feels cornered. A person might actually be cornered—in extreme situations like espionage or war. But if a person in ordinary circumstances falls into severe depression, we must suspect a hypersensitivity of the nervous system. The nervous system is ill. It is not independent of the body; it relates to the world *through* the body.

A nervous system dwelling in a sick body is like food kept at room temperature in the summer. It is destined to spoil. Taking antidepressants (like preservatives) is not the fundamental solution. The real solution is to make the "room" where the food is kept as cool and fresh as a refrigerator. It means tidying up the room.

* **Eating** is like bringing items into the room.
* **Exercise** is like organizing those items.

This is why no philosopher underestimates the value of proper diet and exercise. Ultimately, one must move. Through exercise, one anchors the far-fixed vision to the near distance. Only then can one proceed with activities of the same grain—like studying for the CSAT or conducting research that requires convergent thinking.

---

I have only dealt with the solution for **Hyperopia** so far. This was because solving my own problem was most urgent. In reality, for society as a whole, the solution for **Myopia** needs to be found much faster. **Astigmatism** cannot be ignored either.

First, I will make my own body and mind healthy again. Then, I will address the others.
