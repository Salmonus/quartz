### I. The Shining People Within the Food Chain

While leaving the school gates for my last outing before the exam-period break, I spotted Teacher Shin Seung-ho taking a stroll. He was dressed in plain clothes, no different from what Year 3 students wear when they’ve abandoned their uniforms, and since I only saw his back from a distance, it wasn't easy to recognize him immediately.

Once, after a quick trip to the convenience store, I was returning to the V-Hall classroom via the 4th-floor stairs. A man in a black cardigan was waiting for the elevator. Teacher Shin Seung-ho is not particularly tall, and his build is similar to that of a slightly chubby high schooler. Yet, just by seeing his back, I instinctively found myself bowing in respect. I could feel an **Aura** even as he stood there silently checking his phone. It was as if his body were glowing. Regardless of age, he is a superior. His gait, his manner of speaking, the nuances of his movements, and his expressions—everything that represented him externally was, paradoxically, an unreserved manifestation of his internal depth.

Food chains don't just exist in the safari or the jungle. The "law of the jungle"—the relationship between predator and prey—is most perfectly realized here, within the human food chain. Because he is a teacher at HAFS, he will protect and help me rather than harm me, but I couldn't help but shudder, recalling the memory of facing such a strong individual as an adversary.

I’ve been binge-watching Kim Seung-ri's lectures. He is currently the undisputed #1 instructor for CSAT Korean in Korea. In his signature lecture series, *All Of KICE* (All-O-Ka), the most frequently appearing words are undoubtedly **"Predator"** and **"Shining Person."** In Latin, *Aura* means a "gentle breeze," but as it originates from *Aurum* (Gold), it also carries the connotation of "shining." In fact, if the uncountable neuter noun *Aurum* were to be made plural, it would be *Aura*. Kim Seung-ri says, "The bodies of those who do what they must do, when they must do it, begin to glow." Of course, it’s not as if human bodies have suddenly developed bioluminescent organs like fireflies to emit visible light. What shines is the person's **attitude**. The demeanor of a person who has been thoroughly refined despite countless obstacles is bold, meticulous, and simultaneously possesses a child-like purity.

In the Middle Ages, painters depicting Jesus would invariably use a **Halo** to signify that the subject was not an ordinary person but a Saint (*Sanctus*). Similarly, the Buddha is often depicted through golden sculptures or headbands—elements associated with "light"—to express his mastery over the laws of the universe. At this point, it is no exception. Regardless of physical attractiveness, "shining people" are usually positioned at the apex of the human food chain. If they are high schoolers, they are the top of their class; if they are academy instructors, they are the #1; if they are children who love coding, they likely appeared on TV as child prodigies.

I once had dinner with children from the KAIST IP Gifted Education Center and Professor Mi-young Cha from the KAIST School of Computing. As a middle schooler passionate about chatbots, I thought it was a great opportunity and sat next to the professor, mentioning that I was studying *Deep Learning for Everyone*. I don't know. If you asked me to recall that day three years later, someone else's memory remains more vivid.

> "I suddenly looked at Demian. He was sitting quietly, and his face was so calm and serene. Suddenly, he looked like a dead person to me. No, not dead, but... ageless, as if he had always existed."
> — *From Demian by Hermann Hesse*

I thought he was an older student, perhaps in the 11th grade. It wasn't that his face looked old. Rather, it was a very clean, well-defined, and handsome face. Yet, I felt an extraordinary nature that made it impossible to call him a "younger peer." There was power in his eyes! He spoke sparingly and only answered when asked. His eyes were focused on something that was neither the professor nor me. It is only now, as a 12th grader five years older than the him of 2022, that I realize he was looking at **himself**.
I called my mother that day and told her I had dinner with "two professors."

Experiences of this kind occur daily now. From 6 PM to 11 PM, V-Hall Study Room 204 is a rather special space in the Korean entrance exam world. It is the single space that will produce the highest number of Seoul National University (SNU) admissions across both early and regular admission tracks. At HAFS, one or two entire classes get accepted into SNU every year. As someone who is severely lacking, I somehow obtained the opportunity to study in the same space at the same time.

When I look around, it truly shines. The students who are exceptionally high achievers even exude an overwhelming presence similar to what one feels from Teacher Hong Guk-jin or Teacher Shin Seung-ho. They are calm, composed, and passionate. These kids, who haven't even taken off their school uniforms, spend five hours after sunset completely forgetting themselves and immersing themselves in the discipline of the mind and body. My roommate is the same. It is truly magnificent. They are the most shining people within this fierce food chain.

### II. The Matter of Money & Multiple Food Chains

The biggest reason Darwin was able to travel all the way to the Galapagos Islands to research the traits of finches was not because he was brilliant, but because his family was wealthy enough to purchase the HMS Beagle. The reason I can come to Daechi-dong and share the perspectives of Kang Ki-won, Hyun Jung-hoon, and Kang Jun-ho is also not because I am exceptional, but because my parents are investing the money they worked hard to earn for me. This is not a reason to belittle Darwin or Kim Yeon-woo. However, I must admit that the opportunities I have are purely due to the **Birth Lottery**. I will care for the weak, constantly contemplate what is right, and never act superior or boastful.

There is an attitude even more important than what I wrote above: recognizing that there is more than one ecosystem, beyond just grade-based competition. The biggest reason Korea has not yet made the leap into being a truly advanced nation is that it unifies competition and suppresses diversity. This is mostly tied to the matter of money. Because it's a problem of survival if one doesn't work immediately, the culture has placed the highest value on technology and driven those who didn't master it as quickly as possible into being "losers."

Out of fear, mothers push their children into advanced learning from as young as one year old and forbid any study not related to college entrance. They mock children who read healthily and play outside instead of doing six rounds of Calculus. I, too, have memories of being hurt—moving from one academy to another, taking level tests, and being ignored or discriminated against based on my grades. Since I am a child with a strong sense of self and a clear worldview, I succeeded in intentionally refusing advanced learning from a young age and establishing my own perspective. Most children are not like that. I acknowledge the problem of money. However, the food chain is not just a single one—being good at studying—as the majority thinks.
