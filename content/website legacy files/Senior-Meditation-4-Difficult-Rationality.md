### I. The Illusion of Permanence

The most frequent and fatal bias humans possess is the **"Permanence Bias"**—the belief that the current state will last forever.

There is something my mother has told me since I was young.
When she was pregnant, people would ask, *"Your stomach is so big, why do you eat so little?"*
After she gave birth, people would ask, *"You’re so thin, why do you eat so much?"*
She simply ate what she felt like eating at each moment, but people only believed what they saw and what they wanted to believe in that specific snapshot of time.

She told me something else regarding my birth.
Giving birth damages the body; the bones spread apart.
A wound is like an empty space: if you fill it with bad things, it leads to destruction, but if you fill it with good things, it leads to recovery.
While many mothers order instant food under the guise of postpartum care, my mother ate seaweed soup with extra seaweed.
Now nearly 50, she looks so young that when we go to a department store together, clerks often mistake her for my (18-year-old) older sister.

### II. The Prefrontal Cortex and the "Later"

The part of the brain that makes a human truly human is the **prefrontal cortex**. Among all brain regions, it is the only one that allows us to plan for "later."

* If I invest all my resources here right now, will this become my entire identity?
* Will my current strengths and weaknesses remain eternal?
* If I don't do it now, will I never be able to do it?

What **evidence** is that belief based on? What is its **credibility**? What is the **prior probability**?

A person who overcomes the bias of believing the current state is eternal has essentially passed almost every other rationality training. Conversely, those who fail to overcome it fall into a **self-fulfilling prophecy**, plunging themselves into eternal suffering.
The moment you create a space for "later" in your mind, you can finally "plan" your beliefs and "update" them based on prior probabilities. You become able to suppress the ego.
This is the most difficult rationality training, yet it is the most powerful magic a human can wield.

### III. LessWrong: The Basic Physicality of the Spirit

What I believe is not who I am. My current resources are not everything I have.
This is because the most precious resource is **time**, and as long as you are alive, you have not exhausted it.
If you can do this for yourself, you are already living "The Sequences." Effectively, you have developed the "basic physicality" to be **"Less Wrong."**

It is difficult. But it is what makes a human, human.
There are many types of training in the world, but only one for becoming a human: **Rationality Training.**

> "Just admit it and win next time. That is the privilege of an adult."
> — **Full Frontal (*Mobile Suit Gundam UC*)**

The more things you want to do, the more important this ability becomes. Doing one thing doesn't mean you can't do others; rather, by doing one thing, you enable yourself to do the next.
After spending an entire day on exam preparation, if you feel, *"Is this all I am?"* or *"Will I never get to do what I truly want?"*, simply look back at this:

**How many percent true is that thought? Based on prior probability, how reliable is it?**

Even if reason answers "No," it takes time for emotion and instinct to settle down. Until then, fiercely shout "No." You can do it because you are human. You can do it because you are an **aligned being**. You just need to keep moving. Even if the thought that "the current state is eternal" brushes past you, do not let go of the hands that are doing the necessary work.
That is how we become better—by being **Less Wrong**, one step at a time.

> "You fight like a younger man, nothing held back. Admirable… but mistaken."
> — **Bane (*The Dark Knight Rises*, 2012)**

Will you fight like a young man? Or like a seasoned warrior?
Will you rush in without hesitation, looking only at the present, or will you perceive your errors and plan the "Big Picture"?

**Batman:** *"You've made a serious mistake."* **Bane:** *"Not as serious as yours, I fear."*

Before it becomes an irreversible mistake, do what must be done.
