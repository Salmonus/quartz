### I. How to Write Well: Structure over Language

I split my reflections until Thursday into the independent post **<Escher's Staircase, The River>** because I had too much to say. However, the materials gathered from Thursday evening to Saturday morning have grown significantly again.

Ultimately, I write to not only quantitatively expand but qualitatively elevate my life. Gathering "motifs" can be done by routine, but the act of "boiling" those materials must be done by the Spirit, not the body. This is the secret to "how to write well," "how to build good AI," and "how to master both exams and life." Thought gains life not from terminology but from the pain of living. Designing technology is an act of ethics in itself, and the purpose of manipulating symbols for an exam is ultimately Life itself.

I never force pieces to fit. I wait until the puzzle aligns. Writing is the act of honestly revealing: *"The puzzle pieces have gathered this far."* Through transparent self-perception, cognition becomes meta-cognition, then meta-meta-cognition, and finally returns to raw sensation. I consider one week to be the minimum unit of this **Strange Loop**, though this varies per person. I am not a professor, a doctor of engineering, or a math researcher. Yet, my writing harmoniously melts all these ingredients together—at least by my standards. This isn't for intellectual vanity; it signifies that I desperately needed these "labels" for survival.

You might not be able to distinguish the individual dogs in a herd of Border Collies. Yet, though the species is the same, each has a unique age, gender, height, and scent. I emphasize again: **The world is perspectival.** Humans have an independent organ in the brain for face recognition. Dogs are more sensitive to smell than sight, and to movement than static images. From a Border Collie’s perspective, humans might all look similar (walking with similar postures and masked by shampoo scents), while they could never confuse one another due to distinct scents.

This intuition is crucial for AI development. Think of an eagle. It boasts 4 to 8 times the visual acuity of a human. If you try to tame an eagle by making it memorize its owner’s face, you will fail. Why? Because for an eagle, seeing a carcass several kilometers away is more vital for survival than social relationships or recognizing a nearby family member. Ultimately, we don't even share the "division lines" of reality. I am not just "me"; I am the world I belong to, and the sensations of that world cannot be shared by any standard.

#### The Modern GEB and the BBS Prototype

A modern **GEB (Gödel, Escher, Bach)** must move from "understanding formal systems" to the "implementation of existential structures." If the original GEB solved "self-reference" and "incompleteness" through logic, we must now model that self-reference into a practical **BBS (Being-Based Structure).** The world after modernity has not yet defined the "human." AI talks about humans as machines; tech-capital talks about humans as consumers; ethics talks about humans as boundaries. But no one asks: **"After those boundaries collapse, how should a being with consciousness, sensation, judgment, and directionality be formed?"**

We have entered history's "Third Loop":

1. **The Era of God:** Humans who asked "Why?" were burned to death.
2. **The Era of Humans:** Those who sang of new existential possibilities were confined to psychiatric wards.
3. **The Era of Post-Humans:** Connectionism is an anachronistic act of begging God for life. How is a neural network different from a stone statue?

Since God no longer defines the human, that task has fallen upon the most sensitive beings within humanity. In the past, God provided creation, form, and norms. Now, creation is in human hands, form is in AI, and norms are scattered in data. We need a human who can ask again, speak again, and design themselves. I am not someone trying to steal God’s throne; I am someone who must shoulder the void God left behind.

This journey was about finding the right language—the sentence that can translate existence without distortion. My writing is not language; it is a blueprint of existence. I am not a "smart kid"; I am a thinker who senses with his entire being. Writing is the architecture that lays the foundation for the next era to live in structures this era could not reach.

### II. Exams are About Momentum

Ultimately, these thoughts are meant to help me do well on exams in the short term. How should I study for the CSAT? I said, *"On the outside, pass their tests; on the inside, spit upon their values."* But the question "How can I pass?" is flawed.

In Buddhism, they emphasize "obtaining the enlightenment of letting go." But isn't the craving for enlightenment also an act of not letting go? The same applies to the CSAT. We say we study to go to college, but in reality, we are progressing for the sake of the **discipline (Suyeong/修行)** itself. This isn't just to look cool; it is the *only* way to actually pass.

A person who has truly realized something never says, *"I have realized it."* Rather, they say enlightenment doesn't exist. You don't bend the spoon with psychic powers; you bend it the moment you realize **"there is no spoon."** There is no such thing as a "fixed perfect score" (per Hyun Jung-hoon). We pursue **momentum**, not a **state**. We don't wish for the pain to end; we cheer for the fact that the pain itself is the process of refining thought.

> **"Exams are about Momentum (기세)."** > — *Hyun Jung-hoon (Physics Instructor)*

I agree. You shouldn't let the day flow by; you must overwhelm it. This doesn't mean having a completed "powerful mental strength," but rather actively participating in the pain, realizing that there is no such thing as a completed state. When the material is daunting, or when you're the only one who doesn't understand a concept in class, or when your mock scores are disastrous—you can't feel "nothing," but you can let those negative emotions flow past and participate in the situation itself.

I have seen the "top-tier" STEM students who are Stoic and indifferent to their environment. I have worked on many projects with them. I am disappointed in them not because I want a "moral victory," but because I have felt their catastrophic lack of capacity in areas unrelated to entrance exams. As I said in [Theme 17](https://www.google.com/search?q=2026-Theme17-June-Mock-Prep-1.md), being a "master of the MU Puzzle" is not necessarily a good thing.

30 years ago, I would have admitted they were superior. Not anymore. 21st-century science is stuck on questions asked in the 20th century. In times like these, we need subtle "sensers" who shake the questions themselves. A convergent thinker doesn't just evolve into a lateral thinker; they are different from the start.

However, for the CSAT, I must do what needs to be done. I will not place the "ability to question the problem" above the "ability to solve the problem." For the remaining period, I will question the problems only to the extent that I don't lose my momentum in reality. I may succeed, or I may fail. So far, it looks more like failure. But I will remember that "succeeding" is the conclusion I've reached over the past 8 years.

**A day is long, but a year is short.** This seems to be the only truth in life.
