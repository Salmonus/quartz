### Reflection and the Lack of "Intensity"

I have gained much regarding the CSAT over the past week. I've made balanced progress across Math I, Physics, Korean, and Calculus. However, much is still lacking. I lack the sheer "vicious intensity" I observe in the highest-tier students. If I continue like this, I can see only the path of a re-taker (Jaesusaeng).

Like any other student, I have dreams and ambitions. Whether my capabilities can support them remains to be seen, but I have held onto my 7th-grade dreams until this very moment as I approach adulthood. I cannot afford to study "moderately." I must burn with passion as if starting something entirely new. I must not merely endure; I must conquer and overwhelm. This afternoon, after Physics class, I re-evaluated everything about myself from square one.

And yet, no matter how much I think about it, the source of my initial resolve and passion seems to resemble this existence.

### The Spirit of the Spear

> "My son, take my spear. It is too heavy for my arm."

These are said to be the words the great scholar **Theodor Mommsen** left to a 23-year-old **Max Weber** before his death. What is this "spear"? I don't fully know its meaning yet. What matters is that the essence of the "spear" and the "pioneering spirit" are the same: **to pierce through.**

Piercing through something as a pioneer is different from walking a set path. It is far from glory. It means not even picking up a mechanical pencil for a CSAT problem without a sense of purpose. It means choosing Neurology—an unpopular department—instead of the highly coveted Plastic Surgery. It means studying forgotten languages that no humanities student can read anymore while running at the edge of the STEM track. It is about trusting only the direction, regardless of the magnitude of the vector, and piercing through—discarding the ego to devote oneself to the plan.

I have heard too many words from too many people. This post, too, is left for the future, not the present. But even if I am not loved by everyone, even if I am "different," there is something I cannot give up: **the flow of the times.** Even if I were removed, the flow of the times would not change; someone else would carry out all the tasks mentioned above. If I cannot do it, the next generation will inherit it. Then why must I strive? To join this fascinating flow and enjoy it to the fullest.

Many who view study not as a journey to answer questions but as a transaction with society study far more "viciously" than I do. I believe many problems in Korean society stem from this. The starting point of all their passion is a longing for a comfortable life, not a fundamental question.

Simply studying that way might yield some results for the CSAT. I might make balanced progress in Math, Korean, and Science. However, much is still lacking. I lack the "intensity" required to pierce through ahead of the top-tier students. At this rate, no path is visible. To achieve at least the easiest Step 1, what I must do right now is clear.

I want to achieve a level of growth never seen before by internalizing every possible case, pattern, and relationship between concepts in the CSAT. I want to become a monster that thinks of nothing but the CSAT—to become the CSAT itself. I want to introduce myself that way, because I am Kim Yeon-woo. Even though I am opening a CSAT workbook, in my heart, I am back in my happy middle school days, wearing my Unjung Middle School uniform with nothing but a single declension table in front of me. Because the me of today is the "best" that the me of back then dreamed of—and even more.
