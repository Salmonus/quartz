### I. The Gap Between Motivation, Will, and Ability

I have been very ill since the day I wrote the Week 2 post. I wrote that post promising myself I would get better, even though I wasn't sure of my condition. Since I hadn't been severely ill for more than a day since 2020, I trusted my youth and didn't go to the hospital. I also believed there wasn't much a hospital could do for a common cold. As a result, I spent four days—Saturday, Sunday, Tuesday, and Wednesday—lying in bed. I achieved nothing.

Last weekend, lying in bed, I thought, "I must go to the study hall on weekdays." I did go on Monday morning. But once there, a wave of drowsiness hit me. It wasn't just lack of sleep; holding my upper body upright required desperate resistance. Even while trying to make a plan, my mind felt so distant that I constantly worried about wasting the entire morning. I drank an energy drink—a beverage that grants immediate focus at the cost of being unable to guarantee the body's condition the next day. Even then, I couldn't even touch Science. Instead, I finished the Math I special lecture assignments I missed over the weekend. By the time I finished, it was time for dinner and my Math I study group. Because of the study group, I solved even more Math I problems. Monday effectively became a day of only studying Math I.

When I returned at 9 PM after the study group, a fever began to rise. I knew how sick I would be the next day. Between the energy drink keeping me awake, the pain in my body, the non-stop coughing, and trying to sleep alone in a room with poor heating—it was not easy. I didn't even tell my family I was sick.

I believe everyone who studies has something they do when they "can't sleep but must." For me, it's high-intensity exercise, which I couldn't do because I was ill. I’ve had a bad habit since early in my 11th grade. As a "Plan B" for sleep, I kept female hormone medication with me. Drastic hormonal changes in the body lead to mood shifts. In my case, it manifests as crying for no reason. I used this to exhaust my body through crying to force sleep. Falling asleep that way weakens the body and mind, and above all, "feminizes" the spirit.

The next day, I was diagnosed with the flu, not a cold, and suffered for two more days until Wednesday. There were many problems. My body was permanently damaged by the wrong medication. Without *Guron-san*, energy drinks, or high-caffeine coffee (over 200mg), I feel dizzy all day and can't study—I can't even function as a normal patient. My muscle mass decreased, and my appearance changed enough for my family to notice. Due to the medication, I found myself craving directionless affection. All of this prevents me from focusing on what I must do. This was a problem I was aware of even before the break, and restoring my masculinity was a task as important as studying. Even now, my mind is hazy, making it hard to write.

I must recognize the difference between **Motivation**, **Will**, and **Ability**.

* **Motivation** fluctuates based on health.
* **Will** is a fixed constant of an individual's output based on life experience and values, regardless of health.
* **Ability** is the lower bound of scores one can achieve when motivation is at its lowest.
* 
* 

My will is objectively strong. But if my growth is unsatisfactory, it's because my motivation has plummeted. If you don't feel like doing something, you might actually lack the physical power to do it. You must maintain a healthy body through diet, exercise, and habits to sustain motivation. Masculine hormones, which spark the spirit of challenge, are part of that motivation. I believe this is why male students often stand out in high-stakes entrance exams. I need to know how to manage my motivation. The mind cannot be separated from the body. I must manage my motivation rather than just trusting my will, and check my ability with a longer-term perspective. And will is also reinforced by successful experiences. Honestly, I don't know what I'll do if I get sick again. The loss this week is too great. Please, let's stop consuming strange things.

### II. Why "Stabilizing Science" Keeps Failing

It takes this much effort just to analyze a single constant acceleration problem—not even a "killer type" or "Question 20." Is it easy to realize on your own why some problems are hard to analyze when drawing two  graphs together, while others are easier to solve by identifying geometric properties? I approached the  graph with more information first, fixed one, determined the rest through the slope, and found the answer using time conditions. But looking back at the explanation, it was a problem solvable entirely with the average velocity formula. Hyun Jung-hoon tells us to solve every constant acceleration type using both formulas and graphs, but is that actually easy to practice?

The phrase "Stabilizing Science" hides a psychological urge to "hurry up and finish Science so I can do other subjects." The pressure of Korean and Math, which feel never-ending, combined with rationalizations common in the exam world to ignore electives, makes Science seem small. Everyone *wants* to "stabilize" it. But not every Physics student can immediately think of two ways to solve a problem when given a past paper. That is the dilemma of Science electives. You want to finish quickly, but it never ends quickly. It seems okay because patterns repeat and complex math isn't involved, but you have to beat the "fixed 1st grade" students who display god-like intuition in desperately short time limits to get that 1st grade, which is synonymous with a perfect score.

Yet, I also know. I know that moment when you reach a level in Chemistry where you can freely convert mass, moles, volume, and density—the answer appears as soon as you see the data. That sudden moment of realization. I know that if I confidently write '1' on key data, convert everything to my relative scale, and then switch back to the actual values provided, I can reach a solution more ideal than the official explanation. Physics can be the same if I think properly. That is my definition of "Stabilizing Science."

Scientific thinking is different from mathematical thinking. Unlike Math, which highly values the attitude of approaching new problems with the same framework by possessing an unshakeable system, I know that valid science education means sharpening facts about the natural world into intuition using obvious formulas and logic. To reach that, one must practice obsessively in a way so complex that no one else would do it. That is my specialty. I can do it, and I must do it.

What I experienced while being ill is that Korean and Math skills are hard to raise but also hard to lose. Even if I don't finish Kang Ki-won’s assignments, I get above-average scores. Even when I feel like dying from the flu, I get 6 out of 8 right in the Math I special lecture. One of those was just a calculation error.

Since Korean and Math remain stable even if I only do Science for a week, let’s try to crush Science once and for all. If I push through and finish one *Zaisstory* book in my own way, I’ll get a feel for the subject. It is ideal to reach a state where I can approach every problem somehow, then add Hyun Jung-hoon's "color" to it and solve *Stimpack*. I must also finish reviewing all the missed *Hwajaljuice* problems, and *FAST TRACK* must become a paper I can solve in 20 minutes without hesitation. I’m okay with putting Math aside completely and structure my day to go "all-in" on Science. My current understanding of electives is non-existent to the point that I *must* do this. I hope this week is one where I truly immerse myself in Physics and Chemistry.

### IV. The Sufficient Level of Thinking

Ultimately, all of this was created for high school students to achieve. Until now, when I solved CSAT problems, it was "advance study." Now, studying every scope is "current study." From a developmental standpoint, there is no problem I cannot solve. If I can't, the immaturity of my brain or physical organs can never be the reason. Now, it is entirely a matter of practice. And I can feel it. All the elements of study that I once thought were monumental and distant are coming together to become "things I can do." Students who were always good at studying or who did intense advance study from a young age won't know this feeling. They won't even want to know. I believe that's where the difference begins after university. I will challenge myself constantly until every task becomes like this. And when my understanding matures, that’s when my true value will show. I believe in this.
