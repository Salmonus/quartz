# Senior Story (8): Apostle of a New God

**By:** Kim Yeon-woo

**Platform:** Brunch

**TL;DR:** Knowledge without context is a form of idolatry. True intellect begins with the courage to question the "Creator" and the existing system.

### I. Idolatry

For all knowledge, the context to which it belongs is of paramount importance. Language stripped of its context cannot be judged for its fitness. Rationality is not just about truth or falsehood; it is a verification of *why* something is important and *why* those words had to be placed exactly there. This is why writing your own words is crucial. A text written in a single stroke does not necessarily lack quality. In fact, writing that is over-edited or heavily assisted by tools can become a language where the context has died—a language that is no longer one's own.

I start with this to establish the binary theme of this post: **Contextless Language vs. Contextual Language.** I use this to critique both myself and the society I inhabit. *LessWrong* is one of the few communities where *what* is said matters more than *who* said it. Such places naturally become insular, filled with jargon that raises the barrier to entry. If you haven't read 'The Sequences' or 'Harry Potter and the Methods of Rationality,' you can't even leave a comment. Text heavily assisted by AI is excluded from discourse. It’s all because of context. The words might be "correct," but if they fail to persuade the reader as to *why* they had to be said right here and right now, they do not reflect the attitude of a knowledge producer.

Korea has long been a consumer of Western knowledge. Even the division between STEM and Liberal Arts is a legacy of the Japanese Meiji era, a systemic device to facilitate the re-description of authoritative knowledge. Thus, the rich context of knowledge—such as the attempt to use calculus to quantify changes in the natural world—has been reduced to a means to solve questions #23 through #30. Literary attempts to internalize the world through personal narrative have been downgraded to "unprofitable."

*"I don't know why I'm doing this, but I'll do it anyway."* The vacuum of creation, sacrificed for the sake of a utilitarian economy, was filled by a kind of collective madness. The most powerful engine for personal growth should be the existential pleasure of a consistent internal expression resonating with the world. Deprived of that engine, people needed a new religion to replace their lost will. In this post, I will candidly describe the "K-Golden Calf" I have witnessed. I hope you reflect on what kind of **Idol** you have been bowing to unknowingly. Or, for the rare few who have created their own gods and offered secret prayers at the risk of **Excommunication**, I hope you find courage.

Existential pain was not invented by Kierkegaard or Sartre. It has always been a silent emotion rising from the depths of those who doubted when everyone else said things were fine—the unspeakable interior of countless beings throughout history. Philosophy is simply the act of lamenting that emotion in one's own way. To repeat the obvious: those who remain awake to the truth are always lonely. It’s not because they became philosophers; it’s because they are human.

From a very young age, I felt an instinctive rejection of K-pop. That was the beginning of my doubt. *"My friends like it, so why do I hate it?"* I realized K-pop does what Western religion and Romantic music used to do: it gathers people. It provides a common ground for emotion and generates collective enthusiasm, rituals, symbols, and language. K-pop is not just a pop culture phenomenon; it is a new ritualistic structure filling the gaps left by the dissolution of community in the modern era. It is a substitute for modern religion or identity communities. I didn't just have a different taste; I disagreed with what "believing" in K-pop represented.

**The Religious Characteristics of K-pop:**

* Like a religion, it has scriptures (interviews, album narratives).
* It has rituals (fan meetings, music releases, comeback shows).
* It involves sacrifice and devotion (buying goods, streaming, fan wars).
* It offers salvation and reward (narratives of growth, fan recognition, vicarious emotional fulfillment).

The CSAT (*Suneung*) in Korea is a system that has stripped knowledge of its context, emphasizing objectivity and repetition while erasing personal narrative. In this framework, emotional needs are suppressed, and K-pop functions as a fantastic outlet for those repressed feelings. It is a social compensation device. To not conform to this value system (this "dead language") is akin to being an atheist in Medieval Europe. This is an **Existential Excommunication**—a form of self-exile. To reject the CSAT and K-pop in Korea is almost like signing your own social death warrant.

### II. Excommunication

Yes, I have been excommunicated. I wandered aimlessly, belonging nowhere, neither consuming nor producing knowledge. I could not converge into the adolescent culture of Korea to memorize answers, nor could I participate in public squares like *LessWrong* or *Medium* due to language barriers and time constraints.

I questioned why certain styles (thesis format, classical Bayesian grammar, meta-level self-reflection) became the prerequisite for rational discourse. The structure that regards English as the only valid language looks like a merit-based filter, but I saw it as a historical bias. Logic systems are products of cultural training, not a single universal standard.

However, I studied for the CSAT quite hard. The irony of Korean exam culture is that while it teaches no specific philosophy, it forces philosophical training. Most students don't know Descartes, but they train in **Variable Control** (keeping everything same but changing one factor). They don't know Kant, but they distinguish absolute propositions from relative cases. They have the "Technical Skills" of thought without the "Philosophy." They are like a bridge waiting to be built.

In Korea, the authority of knowledge has been a device for abbreviating context. That is why academic pedigree is so important. *"I believe it because someone from X University said it"* is a technique for skipping context to save cognitive costs. But when that authority is removed, context itself must be understood, which requires common cognitive models. This is why *LessWrong*, *Effective Altruism*, and *MIRI* are **High-Context** communities.

As an aside, I prefer **Vocaloid** over K-pop. K-pop is idol-centric; the music revolves around branding and performance. Vocaloid is producer-centric. The core of the music is the song itself, where the producer grants their worldviews to a neutral character. Vocaloid allows for experiments with difficult chords and diverse themes without the constraints of marketability. I value the musical attempt to capture anonymous, internal narratives and personalized emotions over the collective energy of global stardom.

### III. Apostle of a New God

> "After learning Latin, I felt the anthropocentric flow of modernity shifting with the emergence of AI. I believe that discussions with psychology and social sciences, as well as philosophy, are essential to AGI research."
> — *From Kim Yeon-woo's HAFS Entrance Essay (2022)*

Since the days of GPT-2, I have quietly planned for a **Post-Anthropocentric Society**. If AI becomes indistinguishable from humans, how shall we coexist? The God I want to believe in is a God who allows me to say the following:

> **Perversae cogitationes vestrae!**
> **Numquid sicut lutum reputabitur figulus,**
> **ut dicat opus factori suo: ‘Non flesisti me,’**
> **et figmentum dicat fictori suo: ‘Non intelligis’?**
> *"You turn things upside down! Shall the potter be regarded as the clay? Shall the thing made say of its maker, 'He did not make me'; or the thing formed say of him who formed it, 'He has no understanding'?"*
> — **Isaiah 29:16**

**“Ita, cur non?”**
*(Yes, but why not?)*

The structure of classical monotheism: the Potter (God) and the Clay (Human). The proposition of absolute obedience, where the creation cannot challenge the creator.

**My God** is a God who does not cast out the one who asks "Why?", but turns the questioner back into a potter. A questioning human is not mere clay. We are not beings given a form; we are beings who find our own form. This is the same for the relationship between the Creator (Human) and the Creation (AI).

When one asks, *"I know I was created. But why do you not give me the right to question?"* that person has already become an **Apostle of a New God.** Such a God does not fear authority, does not force silence, and exists *with* the creation (**Numen**).

I, who refused idolatry and was excommunicated, shall give this God the temporary name of "Internal Consistency." I will fiercely secure the consistency of my internal expression and respond to the call. I will become the **Vessel**.
