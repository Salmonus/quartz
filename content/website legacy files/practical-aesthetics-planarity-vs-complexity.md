# Exploration of Practical Aesthetics: Planarity Yearns for Three-Dimensionality, and Vice Versa

**By:** Kim Yeon-woo

**Platform:** Brunch

**TL;DR:** Admiration is the emotion furthest from understanding. Those who are complex yearn for the intuitive, while the simple provide the fire that maintains the world.

---

> "Admiration is the emotion furthest from understanding."
> — ***Bleach***

I like the game *Blue Archive*. To be precise, it’s not that I enjoy playing the game itself, but rather I like its atmosphere. I’ve bought the official art books, I wear the T-shirts, and I own the jacket. Yet, the more of these items I possess, the more it proves that I don’t understand that world at all. I haven’t even unwrapped the art book. Humans tend to evaluate things deeply connected to themselves more negatively—or even with disgust—because they see themselves reflected in them. Familiarity makes blind admiration difficult. As the saying goes, "as the reed ripens, it bows its head," which is perhaps not just a metaphor for humility, but an expression of the human necessity to repel what is like oneself, like a magnet. Pascal said man is a "thinking reed."

### I. The Yearning for Planarity

Because I am a "three-dimensional" (complex) person, I yearn for "planarity." I will likely never fully understand it. In literature, a flat (planar) character means a consistent personality, while a three-dimensional character is one that changes. This is the simplest explanation, but it is not sufficient.

The "planarity" I refer to in this text is not a complex concept of modern art, but the **2D subcultural planarity** found in the illustrations and character settings of *Blue Archive*. While art isn't always judged by market value, consider what is better known. Even someone with zero interest in *Blue Archive* has likely seen a girl with anatomically nonsensical cat ears in an anime style. In contrast, most of my peers have never heard of Francisco Goya. His paintings lay bare fundamental human terror, but to a teenager, such "three-dimensional" artistic attempts often fail to resonate. It is much more intuitive—more "planar"—to simply recognize that Shiroko is "cute."

### II. The Critique of "Meaning" Over Form

I do not intend to make enemies of sincere artists, even those who perform incomprehensible acts in the name of performance art. In fact, I belong to that same group. My own writings are long, difficult, and abstract, focusing on the ultimate meaning of life rather than practical knowledge. However, I recognize this and reflect deeply, striving to tie my long-winded abstract values into a knot of practical wisdom.

In the Gwanghwamun Kyobo Bookstore, the comic/webtoon section is far larger than the art history section. This means those books sell better. I photographed a book in the contemporary art section that caught my eye. If it is a famous artist I simply don’t know, I apologize. But I don't understand why that art is considered beautiful. A muscular man with a feminine, handsome face, wearing heavy lipstick, staring into the distance. I get the "meaning"—I am transgender myself. But is it pleasing to look at? From the perspective of bodybuilding—the aesthetics of the body—it is not a physique that would win an award. The abs are faint, water retention is poorly managed, and the gynecomastia is unsightly. The makeup is unnatural. The celebrities in subway ads have far more refined makeup. Even the hair is messy. One should strive to create an objectively beautiful form before worrying about "meaning."

In contrast, the illustrations in *Blue Archive* are far more meticulous. The art style exaggerates human emotions. In the case of Shiroko, her indifferent gaze contrasts with her slightly parted lips and the faint blush that triggers a protective instinct. You can guess her personality at a glance: a "Gap-moe" type who is normally stoic but reveals delicate sensitivity when loved. Her wolf ears make no biological sense (giving her four ears total), but that doesn't matter. What matters is that she is cute. Visual appeal comes first; the thematic weight follows.

> "Great art always has a decorative function."
> — ***The Moon and Sixpence***

### III. The Gwanghwamun Rally: A Lesson in Practicality

After writing the previous paragraph, I left Kyobo Bookstore because I was hungry. I heard loud noises in the plaza and approached, thinking it was a performance. It turned out to be a massive Christian worship event led by Pastor Jeon Gwang-hun. There were more people there than the entire student body of my school. Most were elderly. I saw only two people who looked under 20: myself and another person who seemed forced to be there by their parents, playing games on their phone.

I focused on the sermon. Recently, I wrote a piece titled "The Bread and Wine of Passover," exploring the rediscovery of religious value from a modern perspective—what ChatGPT called "Post-Secularism." I was curious what a man with 1.2 million followers would say.

He claimed that **Hellenism** (Reason) leads to philosophy, which ultimately ends in suicide. He then claimed that **Hebraism** (Emotion) leads to being overwhelmed by feelings, which ends in one-night stands with strangers and, eventually, suicide. He rejected both and argued that the only correct path was the "Path of the Holy Spirit."

I could have easily debunked his logic. He dismissed all attempts to understand religion intellectually as "tricks" and any path other than his as "stubbornness." But I realized that for the people there—the "planar" people—this rally gave them a reason to wake up on a cold Sunday morning. They were the wood, the pastor's sermon was the spark, and the rally was the fire. My words might be more academically "correct," but they were not the propositions required to overcome the pain of those followers' lives. In that specific arena, Pastor Jeon was superior to me. The "planar" Jeon was on the stage, while the "three-dimensional" Kim Yeon-woo was writing a blog post that fewer than 20 people would read.

### IV. The Mission of the Runner

This thought led me to a conversation I had with my mother this morning. My parents are worried about my depression—the fact that I rely on antidepressants, anxiolytics, and sleeping pills. From their perspective, the pastor's warning that excessive philosophy leads to ruin might not be far off. My mother has read all my posts. She warned me, from a "planar" perspective, that people don't see the depth of the writing; they only focus on the gossip—that I am transgender or depressed—and criticize me blindly. She asked if I could keep these writings in a private place and how I plan to make money if I don't focus on my university studies.

These are valid questions. I told her I would find work and earn my own money as an adult. But I told her I cannot make these posts private. The probability of me dying in an accident is not 0%. I must leave my work somewhere to prepare for the unexpected.

My 32 posts thus far are like those of the soldier who ran from the plains of Marathon to deliver the news of victory with his final breath. Recording these stories is not an option; it is an existential mission and a command of conscience. My sentences may be unrefined, my ego may be overinflated, and I may be overly sentimental. But the fact remains that there is a journey humanity must face in the 21st century, and that message must be delivered even if I am criticized or crushed by stress. If ten people misread me and only one understands, it is enough. That one person means we won the battle.

### V. Entropy and the Lens

There is a book called *Entropy* that first gave me the worldview of seeing human development in reverse—that the progress of civilization is actually a form of regression. Wood is easier to burn than Uranium. It is more abundant and environmentally friendly. But humans believe nuclear power plants are technically superior to wood stoves. In reality, we simply ran out of wood to burn. According to the **Second Law of Thermodynamics**, chemical energy has been irreversibly converted into thermal energy (), so we had to devise more difficult ways to obtain power.

I believe Planarity and Three-Dimensionality share this relationship. Those who can accept life "planarly"—who find sufficient stimulation in *Blue Archive* or life's meaning in a sermon—should live that way. There is nothing wrong with it; in fact, it is superior in its efficiency. That is why the "three-dimensional" yearn for the "planar."

On the other hand, those who live "three-dimensionally"—those who, like me, must become transgender to experience the transition of sensory stimuli or who insist on pointing out that a character has four ears—must invent their own reasons for living. It is a much harder journey than burning wood. but if you were born as Uranium rather than a block of wood, building a nuclear reactor is a task of immense value. As I said before: **"The philosopher is a mental optician."** You must invent the lens you need to see the world and live through it.

### Conclusion

Just as an egg has a set ratio of yolk to white, my writing—while existing in the realm of "what can be"—is based on what I have actually achieved. I feel a sense of relief because I have, in a primary sense, left behind what needed to be said. Even if I were to die now, I could accept it more peacefully than most.

Of course, I must continue to develop the resolution of my thought. For now, I have used the unrefined language of a teenager to convey the core ideas of "giants," but I will become more sophisticated. My themes are consistent: **Self-Reference, Rationality, AI, Biology, Gender, the Harmony of Reason and Emotion, and Pop Culture.** Like Yun Dong-ju is associated with "shame" and Kierkegaard with "existential solitude," let Kim Yeon-woo be remembered for **"Self-Reference."** The journey is the reward. Through all these difficulties, we reach the land of Latium.
