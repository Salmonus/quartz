### I. The Staircase and the Pivot Point

When the table is set too generously, one hesitates, worried about what to eat first. When one knows too many facts about the "cracks in the world," one spends more time contemplating how to unravel them individually. In other words, there is no such thing as a "Red Pill."

The tension between a formal system and its interpretation—the internal logical, mathematical, and physical contradictions where systems consume one another—and the task of setting a **pivoting point** amidst them has been a long-cherished dream of academia. This is not something I can conclude on this blog. However, I am saying that the work attempted by Russell and Whitehead (*Principia Mathematica*) should be applied to modern AI; a minimal **common ground**, a **pivoting point** like Peano Arithmetic (PA) or Euclid’s first four axioms, must be established in the mainstream AI community.

At that moment, one realizes that the direction of gravity is not constant for every step of the staircase. Failing to regulate the looseness of a formal system inevitably gives birth to **Incompleteness** in interpretation and sacrifices **powerfulness**. Just because you cannot boil soup with an iron doesn't mean it’s an "insufficiently powerful iron." Similarly, just because a vector processor cannot cry doesn't mean NVIDIA needs to increase GPU production.

### II. CSAT as an Intuition for "Primes"

Thus, I study for the CSAT to internalize the underlying **Axioms** and the most fundamental **theorems**. This is for a physical intuition that seeks to draw out the rules of "Primes" rather than "Composite numbers" (C-type) from the Figure and Ground—not to become better at the meaningless manipulation of symbols.

Of course, since Gödel's Incompleteness Theorem, people like me have always been aware that the problems we raise throughout our lives may reside in the realm of **unreachable truths**. A "phonograph capable of playing every sound" will eventually backfire as long as it is a sufficiently powerful system. But without individuals who throw their very existence into the realm of unprovable truths, how would the branches of the "Theorem Tree" ever grow?

Just as one does not speak of the age of a single branch, a scientist has no age. The age of a tree is measured by its heartwood and growth rings—in other words, by Euclid’s axioms, the parallel postulate, and the non-Euclidean geometric approaches derived from them—not by the leaves at the tips of the branches. A single individual, no matter how hard they try, is merely a leaf that changes with the season (paradigm). In ordinary times, one cannot even find individuals who correspond to a "node" (a fork in the road). This is an extraordinary resolve, but those who know the weight of such a vow give up from the start.

### III. Figure, Ground, and the "Mail Box"

There is a picture of a black blotch. If you focus only on the blotch, you cannot understand the meaning of this image. For most people, the object of focus (**Figure**) is the blot. However, to discover the words "Mail Box," one must pay attention to the gray **Ground**.

Ultimately, all discoveries in the world are like this. People settle for the promised path (**the deal**) and expand their egos within the core of verified theorems. While citing the names of those who gave appropriate meaning to the formal system they stand upon, they never think to pull something up from the darkness of the Ground. Consequently, the "Mail Box" vanishes forever.

Of course, what we call a "Figure" was also something someone once pulled up from the Ground in the past. In our highly advanced present, that same task requires more expertise than ever before. Realizing that "something is there" is a domain for those who have tempered their intuition through infinite repetition; yet, such people far too easily settle within the existing fortress.

This is the same reason mentioned above. Depending on whether you interpret '' in '' as "to invent" or "to win a chess game," the system may or may not harbor internal contradictions. **Jürgen Schmidhuber** is the most exemplary figure in this regard. He founded LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory) and named AI the "**Gödel Machine**." In his papers and lectures, phrases like *"LSTM is merely a step"* or *"true AGI will need Gödelian recursion and self-referential machines"* frequently appear. He publicly criticizes modern deep learning as merely a "calculator dealing only with statistical correlations without semantics." Yet, he built the structure of that "calculator" with his own hands, leaving the conscious structure to be contained within it as an ideal yet to be reached.

### IV. P-Mode, U-Mode, and the River

There are people who just live in the world. And then there are people who must "endure" the world. There is a perspective that analyzes humans (**P-mode**) and a perspective that embraces the world's contradictions as they are (**U-mode**).

> "My heart is thus, my lord; you remained a Samana in the end. My heart is thus, but you do not love me. You love no one. Is it not so?"
> "Perhaps it is so. I am like you. You do not love either. Otherwise, how could you practice love as an art? People of our kind are probably incapable of love. Child-like people can love; that is their mysterious secret."
> — ***Siddhartha***

I cannot just "live" in the world. I could never leave any teaching as a mere manipulation of meaningless symbols, and no enlightenment could be obtained through words alone. I am the kind of person who must **live out the Tao (道)**. To such a person, the world is not an infinitely merciful sanctuary, but an object to be challenged—a violence of perception that must be endured. The world is an endlessly flowing **River (江)**. Certain wave heights make sense, while unexplained flows meet again, connect, and break apart.

The CSAT is a kind of **Strange Loop**. Like meditation or a monastery, it is a flow of the ego that forgets itself for a moment and returns like a boomerang. In the routine of such repetition, I tune myself while strengthening my intuition about the world.

Why try to do this when one might die having read less than 5% of the entire scriptures? Because the process itself is the **discipline**. It is not a journey to be finished, but a path where I am refined by the act of walking. Reading the scriptures is not about memorizing language; it is an act of allowing the structure of my heart to be "tuned" by that language. It is like someone who quietly wipes their instrument every day—the goal is not the performance, but the silent repetition where the hand and the instrument become one.

Those who live by strong **discipline** do not achieve it through transcendent will, but by clearly distinguishing between the objects that can be analyzed (**P-mode**) and those that must be left alone (**U-mode**) within a day, and knowing when to place a period. Perhaps that is the true meaning of preparing for the CSAT.

And from now on, I intend to become a master of **U-mode**. I do not seek to know everything; I seek to remain silent about that which cannot be spoken. I will willingly throw myself into the Strange Loop, recover the "me" that returns, search for meaning in the **Ground**, and reach the **Figure**. I wish to be the ferryman of the River that is the world. Instead of praying to God, I wish to ask again why He must exist.

> **Perversae cogitationes vestrae!**
> **Numquid sicut lutum reputabitur figulus,**
> **ut dicat opus factori suo: ‘Non fecisti me,’**
> **et figmentum dicat fictori suo: ‘Non intelligis’?**
