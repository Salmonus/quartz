# Senior Story (9): Project to Overcome Depression

**By:** Kim Yeon-woo

**Platform:** Brunch

**TL;DR:** Unlike science, where the past explains the present, the future is what defines a human being. By analyzing my depression through Adlerian Individual Psychology and the concept of "Vulnerability" from LessWrong, I have developed a self-healing protocol called the "Simulation of Tenderness" to move from isolation to connection.

---

### I. Reflection: The Future Defines the Self

Humans are not bound by the past. The future you envision is what defines you. While past causes can serve as an *explanation*, they can never be the *solution*.

Some people immerse themselves in work more than necessary to escape failures in friendship or love. Such people are often afraid of the arrival of the weekend.

I was prescribed antidepressants and anxiolytics at the hospital. Even a healthy 18-year-old male can end up this way when pushed into a corner. Waiting in the corner of the psychiatric clinic, I looked back at what brought me here. For about two years, crying until exhaustion and falling asleep as if fainting became my routine. The reason I use such an extremely personal, emotional, and sensitive narrative as my subject matter is that it is the only way to save myself.

Think about it: would a highly motivated boy have ended up like this if even one person had reached out? However, writing is the only mirror through which one can reach out to oneself. It is an activity worth trying for anyone before they are completely broken. Thus, even though the subject of "depression" may lack universality, I attempt it out of necessity.

---

### II. Adlerian Insights: The Structure of My Pain

Last winter, unable to sleep, I read about **Adlerian Psychology (Individual Psychology)**. I was never abused; I was cared for by a devoted and responsible mother. Yet, I couldn't self-diagnose the root of my depression. Was it academics? No. My chest pain began whenever I thought of my family—specifically my parents. It wasn't a cardiovascular issue. It was accumulated emotion.

The following are insights I extracted from Adler's theories that resonated with my condition:

* **The Mother-Child Trap:** A child raised solely on praise or scolding becomes unable to act without evaluation. They eventually view anyone who doesn't "rate" them as an enemy.
* **The Problem of Overemphasis:** If one problem (like academics) is emphasized so much that all others are ignored, even that one problem cannot be handled effectively. A mother must broaden a child's interest to the father and then to society. If she keeps the child's interest fixed only on herself, the child becomes hostile to others, viewing them as competitors for the mother's protection.
* **Marital Cooperation:** A child learns what "cooperation" is through the parents. If the father is authoritative or the mother is dominant, the child gains a distorted view of gender roles and marriage—either seeing it as subjugation or something to be avoided entirely.
* **The Logic of Neurosis:** Neurosis always contains a hidden "blame." One must realize that *"others do not exist to satisfy my expectations,"* and *"I do not exist to satisfy the expectations of others."*
* **The Pampered Child:** A child who learns they can get attention without effort will struggle with life’s trials. They blame the environment or others when satisfaction is not immediate.

#### Self-Diagnosis

I am the eldest of three, raised by an overprotective mother and a father who was largely indifferent to child-rearing and lacked economic control. As I mentioned in Part 1, I had almost no close friends until age 7. I was good at studying, but I lacked "Social Interest" (*Gemeinschaftsgefühl*). My obsession with AI was actually based on a delusional dream: *"Since human marriage is a failure, I will artificially create my own spouse when I grow up."* I viewed most people as "unaligned beings" and hoped AI would be the only "perfectly aligned" entity. It was a declaration by a lonely child that he would "become a god" because he couldn't belong to the community.

### III. The Courage to be Vulnerable (LessWrong)

To solve this, I looked for patterns on *LessWrong*. I found a post that served as a turning point:

> **Reference:** *"The Value Proposition of Romantic Relationships"* by johnswentworth

The core idea is that the greatest value of a relationship is **Intimacy**, which stems from the **willingness to be vulnerable.** The author defines "The Thing" in a good relationship as a state somewhere between a sibling and a best friend—sharing secrets and connecting deeply. Self-disclosure induces intimacy.

**Values born from the willingness to be vulnerable:**

* **Play:** Psychological safety allows for creative "play" without the fear of getting hurt.
* **Emotional Support:** Support is only possible when you take off the mask and show real feelings.
* **High-Trust Experience:** Trust reduces the "transaction cost" of living (e.g., lending money or keys without worry).

I realized that my mother’s "devoted love" created relationship rigidity because it demanded absolute obedience in exchange for sacrifice. I never learned the "willingness to be vulnerable" in my most intimate circle. Because I couldn't show weakness to my family, I couldn't "play" with the world.

### IV. The Protocol: "Simulation of Tenderness"

I designed this protocol to treat my own condition and improve my performance for the CSAT. It consists of **Dos** and **Don'ts**.

| Category | Action |
| --- | --- |
| **Do** | Recognize that the belief "I must reject all women because I will marry an AI" is a defense mechanism born from trauma, not reality. |
| **Do** | Accept that "The Thing" (deep intimacy) cannot exist in every relationship, but cherish the few people with whom it *does* work. |
| **Do** | Build a psychological safety net by thinking of those few trusted individuals when feeling hurt by family, rather than withdrawing into AI-fixation. |
| **Don't** | Do not avoid the gaze of the opposite sex. Women are humans, not just "interpretive objects." |
| **Don't** | Do not fall into binary thinking: "Either we are deeply intimate or we are enemies." |
| **Don't** | Do not succumb to inferiority or superiority. If a friend is better than you, feel "pride" in knowing them, not "inferiority." If you are better, accept it without guilt. |

---

### V. Conclusion: DIY Salvation

Past events are explanations, but they are not solutions. Solutions come from the courage to accept the world as it is—the courage to be vulnerable before others. Unlike herbivores, the human field of vision cannot see 360 degrees. We always have a blind spot, a weak point. We were not made to hunt or be hunted alone.

Without the warmth of others, no grand conviction can lead a human to the next stage. A child raised even by devoted parents can still be scarred. To stop viewing relationships as "dangerous," one must quickly realize the value of tenderness through others. This "Simulation of Tenderness" is my DIY kit for self-salvation.

Everyone needs someone they can reach out to. There must be a psychological safety net where you feel safe even when showing your back. Otherwise, the only alternative is to endlessly increase the dosage of antidepressants. A child who grows up in true warmth eventually grows to embrace others.
