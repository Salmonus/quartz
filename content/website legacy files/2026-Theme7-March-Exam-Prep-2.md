### I. March Mock Exam Prep Status

When preparing for school GPA exams (Naeshin), you can often gauge your own standing just by how much of the surrounding conversations you can comprehend.

* **In Korean:** Hearing a fragment about a character relationship or an interpretation of a poem and immediately visualizing the entire passage's structure.
* **In English:** Hearing just 3 or 4 words from a corner of a text while passing the cafeteria and being able to recite the sentences before and after it.
* **In Math:** Seeing a "fairly good" student struggling with question  in the study hall and immediately coming up with three different ways to solve it.

This is even more true for rote-heavy subjects like Korean History or Integrated Science. For History, I would often have half the material memorized just by listening to my peers talk, without even opening my own prints. It's not that I'm particularly brilliant; that’s just the nature of school exams. Like a martial artist sensing defeat just by looking at an opponent's shoulders, most exams I’ve seen are a power struggle between the creator and the taker. Two weeks before the exam, I could know my level just by walking down the hallway. That’s why the last two years were so painful.

**The CSAT is different.**

The Korea Institute for Curriculum and Evaluation (KICE) is a public research institution with an annual budget of approximately 5 billion KRW. Its selected group of question-setters and reviewers are not entities a mere high schooler can "power-play" against. It is not an exam you can prepare for in two weeks. What a so-called "no-base" student can analyze by opening a past paper workbook alone is limited. It cannot be overcome with mere grit or stubbornness. Going to an academy doesn't change this fact. Even the most brilliant academy instructor is likely to be overwhelmed in both depth of knowledge and understanding of the curriculum by even a junior research professor at KICE. It’s not even a "fair game."

Many people who don't know better complain like this. I have complained many times too, because I am one of those "who don't know better," never having actually taken the real CSAT. However, I am not diving into the regular admission (Jeongsi) track because it's a reckless act. Rather, I believe I am preparing quite advantageously. The CSAT is different from school exams. Its principle is to award a "99-point stability score" to the most ordinary solution that anyone could use, rather than a "99-point artistry score" for the most brilliant one. I study with the firm belief that every subject is designed this way.

The evaluation criteria are likely: "Can you do the basics?", "Do you actually think in your daily life?", and "Have you sat at a desk regularly for the past 12 years?" I already know that many children crossed the wall of basic thinking required by the CSAT at a much younger age than I. Such children will likely view all my blog posts as mere whining. And they would be right. I am not yet certain that I will do well on the CSAT. However, I believe that if one cannot even do well on the CSAT after trying, they lack the basic foundation to earn an "elite medal" in this country and are unqualified for the next stages of life. Ultimately, the CSAT is a floor, not a ceiling, for everyone interested in academic pursuits.

Given this premise, the phrase "March Mock Prep" loses its weight. The "CSAT" has a unique meaning that school evaluations (Hakpyeong) merely mimic. In the end, my saying "I'll prepare for the March Mock" simply translates to: "I'll try to study a bit more intensely until March 26th under that pretext." That’s the reality.

Nevertheless, organizing my regrets and solutions for each subject will at least serve as a direction for the next month. It's better than summarizing nothing after a week of doing nothing but eating and studying. No matter how much I write "The CSAT is a great exam and I'm great for doing it," to someone who studies only a little, I'll just look like an arrogant person with low intelligence. I’m not trying to self-deprecate either. It’s about not repeating thoughts, not falling into baseless arrogance, and moving forward while praising what deserves praise.

For future researchers, I hope these posts serve as informative guides; for fellow travelers, as comfort; and for those who have already passed this road, as a nostalgic reflection. To achieve this, I will be honest, and that honesty will lead me back to my books.

* **Korean:** I haven't been studying it. I need to force myself to do it in the morning to keep my practical senses sharp, but waking up early is hard. When school starts in March, everyone will live in "Senior Mode," so I'll use that stimulation to join in. I have some "bathroom issues" caused by tension. I need to figure out exactly how much to eat for breakfast, when to sleep the night before, and build the grit to finish the paper no matter what warning signals my body sends. "Protocols" come later; waking up on time and concentrating from start to finish is the first hurdle.
* **Math:** I will place more weight on the *end* of the solution than the *start*, focusing on reducing the number of missed items. With a "researcher's attitude," I want to avoid situations where I might be right or wrong after solving. This means each problem takes longer, but since the loss from "solving and getting it wrong" is the same as "not attempting at all," it’s better to use an extra 3–4 minutes to get it right if I've already committed. If I get stuck, it’s highly likely that 90% of other students are feeling the same struggle, so it’s better to research and collect the elements that make that problem difficult. Like Olympic archery where a 9-point shot is great but not enough if you need a 10, in Math, being off by 1 is still wrong. There are no "close calls." My inability is the issue, and real skill is increasing speed with gravity and persistence.
* **Science (Gwatam):** I will become friends with full-length mock exams. Honestly, at this point, there are almost no problems I can't solve if given enough time. I will obsess over time to an almost excessive degree and consider any solution reliant purely on long equations as a failure. I will practice like a madman to reach the finish line within 30 minutes. If you don't recognize the structure and meaning of a problem at first sight, you end up guessing. I will practice until not a single problem relies solely on "brute-force physicality," while internalizing methods to at least put up a fight in a real situation. Also, in Science, you can often "snipe" the answer by matching specific numbers or forms. This can raise your score by over 10 points. I won't just skip unknown problems in mocks; I'll practice that "strategic guessing" along with the timer. Knowing  but not ... yet if there's a unique number like , then  might just be the answer. When someone else got a 42 on Kang Jun-ho’s Mock Vol. 1 like that, I shouldn't just be annoyed; I must practice until that becomes a part of my own arsenal by any means necessary.

The best way to study is to let it seep in naturally while in peak condition. I'm approaching completion when I feel a sense of pride in filling my day completely—recalling what I've learned and minding the details while eating, moving, or even in the bathroom. Any human gets excited when they think, "What I am doing is good, it is a difficult thing that not everyone can do, and I have reached its final stage." However, unlike school exams, it’s not easy to feel that "completion" every time with the CSAT. If I didn't have that feeling at the start of the day, I just need to create it by the end. That’s all. The CSAT is one of the easier projects among the many I could undertake at my age.

### II. People who Curse Me and People who are Moved by Me

There are people who curse me. One step below them are those who pretend to ignore me. Even hating is a form of interest. They show interest because they think I’m "unusual." They hate me because they see something in me that resembles a part of themselves they want to fix. They live hiding that part of themselves, but because I don't hide it, they can't stand it. The first reaction of hatred is pretending to ignore; if cornered or escalated, they curse me; and finally, if our worlds overlap, they try to destroy my world.

There are also people who simply cannot see me. These people are different. While those who curse me at least share some resemblance to me, these people have a categorization of "human types" and "worldviews" in their self-formation that simply doesn't include someone like me. Regardless of good or evil, these people don't even know I exist, even if I am standing right in front of them. Surrounded by such people, I am more miserable than in a society with haters. Fighting is better than being a ghost.

I went to school recently for the final confirmation of my Student Record (Saeng-gi-bu), and Teacher Dale was incredibly glad to see me. As soon as we met, they said I was "the student they are most looking forward to among graduates" (even though I haven't graduated yet) and told me to "earn a lot of money and remember them later." They asked if my CSAT prep was going well and even said, "A genius will figure it out on their own." No matter how glad they were to see me, it’s hard to say that to every student.

I was certain that Teacher Dale was "moved" by the person I am. Yet, I have nothing to show off—neither looks, grades, popularity, nor wealth. I’m in the position of "Student 3" if I were in a textbook. There are many people smarter than me at school, and there are no clear factors to expect that I will earn a lot of money.

With about 8 blog posts related to the CSAT accumulated, I've read a few similar reactions of praise and encouragement. When I graduated middle school, I heard similar things from a small number of people. Those who were moved by me, despite not being close to me, commonlly sought me out directly. It wasn't superficial praise; they confessed that their world had truly deeply resonated with mine, and consequently, they had achieved growth that wouldn't have been possible without me. I know exactly what that is. But if I blur out "I know exactly what you mean!" the moment we meet, it’s awkward. After reading *Demian* in the 6th grade, I learned the sophisticated expression: "sharing the mark." I tell myself that we share the mark, our paths overlapped for a moment, and I sincerely hope they are closer to themselves when we meet next. And then we go our separate ways.

Those who curse me do so because they have experienced my "type" before and are trying to drag me down out of fear. Those who cannot see me do so because they have never experienced anyone like me. All of them are the ones I must embrace—the ones who will benefit the most from my future actions.

It is a blessing to meet even once in a lifetime an individual who is not stained by secular things, who is completely immersed in contemplation of themselves and the world, constantly throwing out interesting questions and meaningful messages, whose life trajectory aligns with the movement of the stars. Even if you meet such a person, it is hard to recognize them if they are still in an immature, minor state. Regardless, I do what I must do.
