### I. Reflections on the "Deformed Child" (畸形兒)

I originally thought the *Gi* in *Gi-hyeong-ah* (Deformed child) was the character for "strange" or "eccentric" (**奇**). This character depicts a soldier on horseback; in ancient China, where horses were rare, such a sight must have been considered strange. This is why the character for "to ride a horse" (**騎**) includes the character for "strange" (**奇**). It is common for a root character to lose its original meaning and evolve to denote a more abstract concept.

However, upon looking it up, I discovered that the *Gi* in *Gi-hyeong-ah* is actually the character for "crippled" or "deformed" (**畸**). While being "strange" (**奇**) can be negative, it can also be positive—it simply means being outside the norm. "Deformed" (**畸**), however, explicitly implies a disability.

In the thought process of an adult, countless learned factors intervene. I want to set those aside and speak of those "strange people" (**奇**) who were born (**兒**) with such a form (**形**) from the very beginning—in other words, the "eccentric children" (**奇形兒**). These are people whose innate way of thinking falls outside the normal category and who are treated as peculiar, yet possess no disability significant enough to hinder their lives.

I have discovered three types of people among my peers in South Korea, and I have come to understand that their thoughts flow in different directions.

[Image Placeholder: A diagram categorizing three types of people]

These are extreme typifications of character traits; in reality, I believe there are degrees of overlap. Generally, people fall into Types 2 and 3. In fact, 99% are Type 3. Most of Type 3 correspond to the "people who curse me" or the "people who cannot see me" mentioned in **Theme 7**. It is unfortunate, but just as they cannot know me, I cannot know them. We are too far apart.

The remaining 1% are Type 2. These individuals are either the victors of the Korean educational system or are already carving out unique life trajectories by digging deep into a specific field.

Usually, when people speak of "geniuses" or "prodigies," they refer to Type 2. They show early talent in solving practical problems and are specialized in seeing details purely, without any specific purpose or reason.

In middle school, I met such people: a younger student who told me that "computer science is a subcategory of discrete mathematics and all related fields are connected," or someone who sat next to me on a bus and suddenly handed me a notebook saying, "Let’s prove Ptolemy’s Theorem together," or someone who spent 10 minutes preaching nonsense before the dormitory check-in only to shout, "The conclusion is, 'We cannot know!'" and revel in it alone.

If Type 2 and Type 3 make up 100%, then who exactly is **Type 1**?
In my diagram, Type 1 has the most text. The fact that it has the most explanation means it is the person I encounter most and know best.

**Type 1 is a description of myself.**

Throughout my life, I discovered that my thoughts differ from others. I spend most of my time performing eccentric actions that most people don't do and having thoughts that fall outside the normal range. Had this led to an immersion in mathematics or science, I too would have followed the typical path of Type 2. The fact that I am a high school senior worrying about the CSAT means I did not walk that path. I did not pour much interest into the logical thinking or STEM problem-solving skills required for the "details." What on earth was I distracted by? Am I a *Gi-hyeong-ah* (**畸形兒**)—a useless, disabled person adapted only to my own mental prison? If so, I would need protection and treatment. But that is not the case.

1. **Direction, Purpose, Value**
2. **Relevance to the ultimate goal**
3. **The logical validity of "me" doing that work**

I obsessively spend 90% of my time and mental energy defining and explaining these three points. I use the actual execution phase merely to discover the details necessary to prevent my thoughts from becoming "contentless." I remember being this way since birth. I am a person with a "Liberal Arts" (Humanities) thinking structure, which is extremely difficult to bring to fruition in South Korea. After passing through steps 1–3, the medium for expressing the result varies. Because I am impatient, I write as soon as I finish defining the world, but others create music or paint. The methods for explaining "Value" can be diverse.

As a byproduct of rapid economic growth, children with STEM-oriented thinking structures receive public attention and succeed in institutional education. I am not saying I am at a disadvantage in institutional education. In fact, seeing as I chose "Speech & Writing" (Un-mae), "Calculus," "Physics," and "Chemistry," no one would superficially call me a Liberal Arts student. However, I am essentially more interested in the *humans* surrounding the natural world than the principles of nature themselves. Therefore, **I am a Liberal Arts student.**

I believe the CSAT is an exam that evaluates whether one can see the world as it is, using the names of various disciplines as a pretext. CSAT Physics I is not the study of "Physics," and Chemistry I is even further from the study of "Chemistry." Some criticize this as a distorted education that encourages private tutoring, but they misunderstand the CSAT. I believe the difficulty of all elective subjects should be adjusted to be identical. The same goes for the electives in Mathematics. Since one doesn't study Physics to do Philosophy, I believe it would be ideal if the Physics II and East Asian History exam papers shared the same level of calculation and inference difficulty. *(Most would think I'm crazy for saying this.)*

Due to the nature of the CSAT, which evaluates **convergent thinking skills**, Type 2 students are at an overwhelming advantage. However, since this isn't a celebrity audition, there is a cutoff line. Failing to meet even that provides no excuse, regardless of what thinking structure one was born with. I wouldn't even have the right to speak such words because it would mean I hadn't used my time properly. To lead as a general, one must at least be able to do the work of a single soldier. Simply put, one must remember the metaphor of **Kant and Schiller**.

### II. My Plan for the CSAT

> **A strategy to defeat the 90% using only 10% of the brain.**

If it weren't for the CSAT, my interest in "details" might have vanished entirely. As Kant said—*thoughts without content are empty*—every piece of writing or philosophy I express might have become "something I read somewhere, attempting to look profound." Therefore, it is desirable to first record my efforts for the CSAT and then record my thoughts. I must always prioritize **"what is actually the case."** "How things should be" is the next problem. As I heard at an academy during my HAFS admission process, it can be summarized as the **"clash between Truth and Logic."** In the Humanities, the *Logic* that persuades people overwhelms the *Truth* that explains facts; in the Natural Sciences, a single line like  receives more spotlight than grand rhetoric. Both are important entities responsible for the progress of human society. While it is the Liberal Arts minds who actually produce information and create systems, their actions are all based on scientific facts.

I will remember this fact and consciously strive to keep the ratio of **Truth and Logic** in my heart at . Setting aside my innate, eccentric thinking structure, I will approach the CSAT like a Type 2 student. Rather than saying, "I can't help it because this is who I am," I must realize that **roleplaying as a genius of a different type** is the condition for my awakening. I must consciously rectify my thoughts to become that person. This is because the *Zeitgeist* I have grasped is still technologically immature. Even if I had been born a bit later into a technologically mature era, I would have had to study as intensely as that technology was researched. I believe my plan for the CSAT, based on an analysis of being a "Deformed Child" (*Gi-hyeong-ah*), must be formulated this way.

As one gets older, time seems to pass faster because the brain adapts. My brain has already adapted mostly to a Liberal Arts way of thinking through infancy and adolescence. For that very reason, I can introduce a common vision that a typical "Math Prodigy" cannot, uniting Type 2 people and becoming a leader for them. However, I do not want to remain a failed idealist who only cries out for empty thoughts. I have seen too many such people, both in history and around me. Even Steve Jobs was able to debate the technical problems of Java programming with engineers. The part I must pray for before solving Kang Ki-won’s *Ass* or Hyun Jung-hoon and Kang Jun-ho’s mocks is exactly that.

AI—the technology that mimics technology or humans—seems to be a field that fits my aptitude perfectly.
