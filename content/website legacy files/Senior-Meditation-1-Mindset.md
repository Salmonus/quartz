# Senior Meditation (1): Mindset for the Examinee

**By:** Kim Yeon-woo

**Platform:** Brunch

**Note:** *This series focuses on lighter content not covered in "Senior Story."*

### I. Continuity and Discontinuity

All meaningful things occur continuously, but they conclude discontinuously.

* The moment you first learn to ride a two-wheeled bicycle  The state of finally riding alone without training wheels / Or not. **(O/X)**
* The moment you challenge a new language and memorize basic words  The state of speaking fluently / Or stumbling. **(O/X)**
* The moment you first buy and solve a CSAT past paper workbook  Entrance exam success / Or failure. **(O/X)**
* The moment you work out for the fifth day at the gym to lose weight  Diet success / Or failure. **(O/X)**

### II. The Purpose of the Game

When we play a game, we think we do it "to win," but in fact, we do it "to enjoy."
If someone told a beginner collecting items to level up, "I'll boost you to level 100 instantly," it might sound tempting at first. But soon, you’d realize something is wrong.
You didn't buy the game to "see the end"; you bought it because the process of "heading toward the end" is enjoyable.

Of course, it can be hard. There may be moments of resignation where you think, "I shouldn't have bought this," or "I just wasted my time and money."
However, just as we don't live simply to die, that game waits for you in its place all day long for the sake of the agony in the process and the catharsis of achievement.

### III. To Impress Oneself

When we study, we think, "I must work hard to get into college," but in reality, we do it "to be able to accept the results."

Despite solving dozens of math problems all day, the skepticism: *"Will even one of these show up on the exam?"*
Despite barely overcoming drowsiness to sit down in the morning, the doubt: *"Will I even be able to wake up on the actual morning?"*
Despite pouring all of lunch and night-study time into the science electives, the anxiety: *"Can I display my true skills within 30 minutes?"*

Despite accumulating all that continuous "experience points," there lies the discontinuous truth of Grade 1 or Grade 2.
Moments of self-torture may arrive: *"I have no talent," "I wish I hadn't started,"* or *"Some friend got a perfect score in just one month."*
When the question *"Can I get into my dream college?"*—a question that is an obvious "Yes" to yourself but a "No" to most—tightens your chest, think about this:

> "If you receive an acceptance text, can you nod with genuine, deep joy?"
> "If you receive a rejection text, can you accept it with the same sincerity as if it were an acceptance?"

If the answer to both is "No," then for whom is your study—which has failed to even move your own heart—intended?

### IV. The Secret of Success

All meaningful things occur continuously, but they conclude discontinuously.
I know all the grueling "intermediate processes," but the world will try to define me as either "one who arrived" or "one who did not."
Only a few can apply that same standard to themselves and derive motivation from it.

Think of it as a game. Are you studying "to go to college"? Are you living "to die"?
Or are you studying "to impress yourself and accept the results," to "enjoy the moment exactly as it is"?

You grumble about studying math for three hours,
but that workbook waited for 21 hours beneath the drawer for your three hours.
You waste a day feeling gloomy because you ruined an exam,
but that exam paper has been gauging your skills and refining its errors since a year ago.

The universe does not endure the remaining 24 hours without you to test you, but to realize your will.
So, come now—do not blame the past, do not worry about the future, and face what is before you at this very moment.
Eat your meals well, get enough sleep, and live out your day by greeting those around you warmly.

No sentence can comfort one who is enduring; only that which they have endured can provide comfort. And that comfort is found not at the beginning or the end of the journey, but in this very moment. That is the only secret to success.
