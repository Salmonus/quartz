### The Evolution of My Mindset

The significance of switching my academic track was a call for a fundamental shift in my mentality:

* **Pre-September Mock Exam:** "I must do what needs to be done."
* **Post-September Mock Exam:** "I must do what needs to be done *properly*."
* **Now (Midterms):** "**What needs to be done is already set.**"

I cannot attempt things the old way. I must complete the "executive brain" by enhancing my agility, management skills, and emotional regulation. Even in team efforts, these individual capabilities remain valid. They are based on direct and indirect experiences and are, in fact, more important than Calculus. There is a word for this: **Fortitude (Guts).**

### The Threshold of the Physical and Mental

When running on a treadmill, I feel like I’m going to die by the 3rd minute. Yet, curiously, I can maintain that "dying" state for 10 more minutes. By focusing on each individual step, I found myself alive—more intensely alive than ever.

I thought I couldn't lift 120kg. But the moment I straightened my knees using instant core strength, the rest of my thigh muscles activated, and I finished 10 reps with ease. It was always the beginning that was difficult.

They say you have to quit smoking all at once. A paradigm shifts overnight. The boiling point isn't a transition from 90 to 110 degrees; it is **100 degrees** itself. It is a *boiling point*, not a *boiling range*. If I had tried to lift that 120kg squat slowly and tentatively, I would have been crushed by the weight, never even daring to try.

### On Maturity and Limits

Thinking back, the phrase "must do" shouldn't exist. Those who will do it, do it; those who won't, never will. There is no need for "must." Perhaps a "formed/mature person" (成人) cannot truly change. If you can't do it, you simply can't.

The Hanja character **成** (to achieve/mature) carries this somewhat negative connotation. It implies a state of having achieved growth, but conversely, having **no more room for change.** If a sculpture is ugly, trying to reshape the plaster after it has hardened will only cause it to shatter. This is why a "completed" (完成) work must be respected as it is.

"What needs to be done is already set." If one could simply leap over their limits, why would the word "limit" even exist? If I, as a semi-adult, see a version of myself that is incapable, it means I have been that way for the past 18 years. That is a sufficient "average rate of change" to predict the next 40 years of my life.

**You will likely never be able to do these things in your lifetime:**

1. Agility
2. Balanced Management
3. Tension Regulation
4. Flexibility & Resilience
5. Executive Brain

If I don't have them, I don't have them. If I do, it's a cause for celebration.

### The Peace of Non-Contention

I think I finally understand the tranquility of someone who refuses to place a bet. That is what it means to have a "peaceful mind"—to celebrate achievements within one's limits. A person with pure intellect can achieve amazing things in a much shorter time than an experienced veteran. I used to think I wasn't that "style." But that "style" is necessary.

I need an attitude of compromise and acceptance to narrow the gap between the ideal and reality. This does not mean growth stops. My ambition shows no sign of shrinking; therefore, reality will have to improve rapidly to catch up. There is no distinction between "before and after the CSAT" or "before and after age 20." **Only the present exists.** It has always been this way, and it always will be.

"Completion" is not the same as "fruition." I hope to enjoy the Midterms—the October festival that acknowledges and celebrates completion. One must never forget that the journey itself is the reward.

To show off my full strength at the "Grand Festival" next year, I’d better start warming up now, in this same season.
