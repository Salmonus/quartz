### I. Dotted Lines and Solid Lines

I’ve taken a screenshot of my table of contents before starting. This is to prove that I determine the structure before writing the body. Just as one decides the ending before writing a novel, I write with my intended message already fixed. To borrow Instructor Kang Ki-won’s words: *"No matter how complex the calculation, if you can finish explaining 'how' to do it in words, you won't get it wrong."* My method of solving math and my method of writing are one and the same. My way of living is no different. The conclusion is pre-determined. Therefore, time only advances the journey; there is no such thing as a "wandering" where essential meaning fades. This connects to the main theme of this post: **"Dotted Lines" and "Solid Lines."**

I’m already in a good mood because I know that since Thursday, high-density, fresh thoughts have gathered like honey. I have already completed this post in my mind during the gaps in Hyun Jung-hoon’s morning lecture. That was the **Dotted Line**. When solving problems involving point charges or the periodicity of trigonometric functions, having confidence in an abstract concept like "symmetry" before diving into the calculation is the process of turning a dotted line into a **Solid Line**. Everything exists on this spectrum. Scholars play in the gap between the "unspeakable feeling" and the "speakable reality." If the main medium is the unspeakable feeling, you are an artist; if it is the speakable reality, you are a scientist. But even a scientist uses the "feeling" of symmetry as a motive for a paper, and even an artist builds scales and paints on the foundation of harmonics and perspective. There is no such thing as doing only one. All education should start by acknowledging this.

#### The Tiella and Tigris of Education

In the 6th grade, I was obsessed with the novel series ***Children of the Rune***. In that world, there are two legendary swordsmanship styles from the ancient kingdom of Ganapoly: **Tiella** and **Tigris**.

* **Tiella:** Dual-wielding, focusing on speed. Growth is incredibly fast in the beginning, but one hits a ceiling easily. Most practitioners remain second-rate.
* **Tigris:** Greatsword-wielding, focusing on weight and gravity. Early growth is so slow that a practitioner can't even face a Tiella user. However, upon reaching mastery, the growth becomes unmeasurable—one just keeps training until exhaustion, and their skill stretches infinitely.

In the story, the protagonist Boris realizes he is the heir to Tigris, while his love interest Isolet is an overwhelmingly talented Tiella practitioner. What does this mean to me? At the time, I didn't know. But fictional settings read at a young age can function like prophecies when combined with later life experiences.

I map **Tigris to "Liberal Arts (Humanities) talent"** and **Tiella to "STEM talent"** to view the educational landscape. In childhood, and even through high school, solving one math problem earns more overwhelming encouragement and envy than reading a book deeply. Initially, the literary or philosophical talent—the ability to meta-cognitively perceive emotions, feelings, and lifestyles—can never overwhelm a Math Olympiad winner. Such talent is, unfortunately, buried.

Think about it: Tiella is described as a "feeling of enduring" as growth repeats, while Tigris is a "feeling of extending." Look at Math and Science. High school math has an overwhelming amount of computation compared to middle school, and undergraduate math requires a level of abstraction completely different in texture. If you are studying math properly at this age, it should feel like "holding your head and enduring," not "singing of dreams and hopes while letting your imagination fly." (Unless you are Terence Tao). Most people who hit that wall and give up the path of a researcher to establish "Math Research Institutes" in Daechi-dong are, in my view, second-rate swordsmen of Tiella. Dual-wielding swordsmanship, which is powerful at first but eventually relies solely on raw talent and "feel," is the fate faced by those who dug *only* into pure math and science. The problem is the Korean educational system itself, which rank-orders children on a cold, reductionist relative evaluation curve and links it directly to economic rewards. I’m not saying studying math is bad; I’m asking why we must study *only* math.

#### Dotted Lines Becoming Solid

What is "Liberal Arts talent"? In simple terms, it is the **ability to draw dotted lines.** For example, we learn that the test for a local extremum is the sign change of the derivative, and the test for an inflection point is the sign change of the second derivative. Rigorous limits are defined by epsilon-delta. Does knowing this "form" help in solving CSAT math? For a Grade 1 student like me, I don't do that at all. After reviewing the conditions and holding them in my mind, I tell myself: *"This case is a contradiction because of X, this case is the answer because of Y."* I write only the single correct scenario on the paper. I solve the problem as if I am lecturing a practical solution on a blackboard, not writing a textbook. I know the formal definitions, but I never "use" them.

I consider this a "Liberal Arts" talent—the ability to logicize and articulate an "unspeakable feeling." This blog is an extension of that. If I were to paint, that too would be an extension. A child who writes well doesn't necessarily become a writer; a math prodigy doesn't necessarily become a researcher. However, it is not strange at all if a child who silently stared at the dust (**Ground**) in an elevator grows up to sweep national awards, or if a child who wanted to be a poet wins a Fields Medal, or if a student who failed and chose to re-take the exam grows up to write a novel like *The Vegetarian* and win a Nobel Prize.

**Dotted lines soon become solid lines. Dots connect to form a set.** (Connecting the Dots).

If we respect children's "dotted lines"—if we stop forcing Tiella for economic reasons and suppressing the Tigris heritage—then growth curves resembling exponential functions will exist in their lives. Initially, an exponential function is slower than a polynomial one. But as , even if the base is close to 1, the exponential function overwhelms all forms of polynomials. Just wait a little for the time axis to proceed.

### II. Linguistic Originality (Dotted) vs. Logical Consistency (Solid)

Among all the media I possess, the one that most completely expresses my thoughts is writing (language). I can write code or paint, but I am not fully "at home" with them yet, so they take too long or the result is crude. I am only 18 or 19.

I want to discuss the "Academic Rigor" I’ve realized while preparing for the CSAT. In a math solution, the standard is **Logical Consistency**. In a poem, the standard is **Linguistic Originality**. My blog has no genre. But if there are criteria for "good writing," it is these two. If I wanted academic recognition, I would focus on logical consistency. If I were entering a short story contest, I would focus on originality. This blog is for the practical purpose of self-correction over the coming week. Therefore, it must reflect my interior most honestly.

I think about **Rigor**. There are many "smart people" in the world.

After reading *GEB*, I looked up reviews on domestic communities and was convinced that almost no reader followed Hofstadter’s playful self-referential humor. I felt a significant repulsion reading the review above. *GEB* is a book about **dotted lines**. It is a joyful hope that uses the "Ant Fugue" to metaphorize new horizons AI hasn't reached; it is not an explanatory manual (**solid line**) for "scientific facts about AI." Why did the Pulitzer committee award it in the Non-fiction category? That review is a classic error of failing to distinguish dotted lines from solid lines. *GEB* is a book meant to be enjoyed and laughed with, not a paper to be evaluated and applied. If you don't smirk at the acrostics of Achilles and the Tortoise, you aren't reading it right. Evaluating dotted lines with a solid-line ruler is a clear **Non-theorem**.

This blog is a series of dotted lines. The solid line will likely be my CSAT score. Both are important. **Process overwhelms, result proves.** In any moment, I will take pride in always being a positive-definite () function.
