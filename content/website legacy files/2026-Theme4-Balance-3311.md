### I. Review of "Stabilizing Science"

> "Teacher~ I picked answer 4 just because it obviously looked like 12 and 1."
> "No, what is the point of guessing a single problem correctly?"
> — *Kang Jun-ho, during a Chemistry I weekly test explanation*

On January 2nd, I moved into the Hun-jang-hak-sa dormitory near Sidae Injae. It was a New Year greeted with the fatigue of postponed finals and a mixture of anxiety and excitement for CSAT preparation. I didn't know how things would unfold. All that was certain was that I was underprepared, but I had to start nonetheless.

In my underprepared state, my level in Science was such that I hadn't even solved basic past papers. Chemistry I, which I had gone "all-in" on along with Math I during the first semester of my 11th grade, still felt familiar in terms of "hand-sense" regarding relative values, internal division, and data conversion. I felt that if given unlimited time, I could solve most problems, or at least I had developed the eye to find the clue to the solution. That intuition proved mostly correct; I’ve had no trouble following Kang Jun-ho’s Chemistry I classes so far.

Physics was different. My only approach to basic constant acceleration problems was bombarding the page with variables without any situational judgment, performing simultaneous equations on meaningless expressions to find  or . Analyzing force or energy was impossible unless it was a problem I had seen before. Thinking about "structure" or finishing a paper within 30 minutes was unimaginable. It was common for my equations to exceed the margins of the *Zaisstory* workbook. Above all, the stress I felt whenever "Physics" was mentioned was significant. It was a task that had to be solved eventually, and while "eventually" kept approaching, my skills remained stagnant. Just like a *Treadmill*, I was applying my best effort , but since , the work  was .

My "Stabilization of Science" plan on January 6th consisted of two goals:

1. **Chemistry I:** Learning the missing concepts to make *FAST TRACK* (and other half-mocks) a meaningful practice tool, and developing a deep enough understanding of Unit 1 (Stoichiometry) to practice under timed conditions.
2. **Physics I:** Building "tenacity" by solving key past papers in early Mechanics (Acceleration, Force, Momentum, Energy) and understanding the intent and structure of Hyun Jung-hoon’s solutions. To actively internalize these as tools for new problems, ultimately reducing the mental burden of Physics to the level of Chemistry.

[Image: Hand-sized notebooks filled with blue, black, red, and green ink analyzing problems]

> "You should use a consistent solution, not a post-hoc analysis.
> Private Physics education today is plastered with post-hoc solutions."
> — *Hyun Jung-hoon, Week 5 Treadmill class*

When I don't know something well, I study by writing everything down like this. I did the same for Math, Latin, and AI. Blue for the initial intuition or core idea, black for the problem situation, and red/green for the steps. **(I really did this for Latin, too.)** This palm-sized notebook is my best tool for checking my thoughts and memorizing while on the move. For a beginner, it is the "last resort" of practical tools to take into the exam hall. Based on experience, once I fill two such books and practice, I eventually don't need to write anything. The core ideas marked in blue become subconsciously ingrained in my hands and mind the moment I see a problem.

I feel my Math has reached that level, so I stopped making notes last winter. I can solve *Ass* (Assignments) without difficulty, and even for the toughest problems, extracting the core from the solution key is easy. I feel CSAT Math is now purely a matter of practice. Physics must reach that state before the cherry blossoms bloom.

The reason I believe "Stabilizing Science" was successful is that by using this method to review *Stimpack*, I was able to approach a significant number of mechanical energy problems in this week's test, and I didn't find the class itself overly difficult. Thermodynamics, Special Relativity, Magnetic Fields, and Waves are not hard units if the concepts are clear. The fact that I can solve Mechanics *Stimpacks* (Vol 1-5) like a normal workbook means I have built the "base physical" and "tenacity" I intended. Of course, my solutions need much more refinement, and there are many problems I still can't solve. I am far from being able to finish a full-length mock exam in time. However, through a month of intensive investment, Science is no longer a "burden I must overcome someday," but a stable source of points that I can expect linear growth from, just like Korean, Math, and English.

### II. Routine Update for Korean and Math (3:3:1:1)

> "Kids, you keep misunderstanding what a 'practical solution' is.
> A practical solution isn't the one you understand easily;
> it's the one used by the people who actually got the answer right in the exam."
> — *Kang Ki-won, explaining 2017 CSAT #30*

As "Stabilizing Science" is no longer the sole theme, I must increase the mental weight and time invested in Korean and Math. To summarize the conclusion of my thoughts:

* **Q:** How should I study now?
* **A:** I’ve graduated from being a novice, so I should study like everyone else.

Next week’s theme is **"Pursuit of Balance, 3:3:1:1."** This ratio (Math 3: Korean 3: Science 1: Science 2) is the standard time allocation for most examinees. If you are extremely poor at a subject, timed practice is meaningless—you might spend 2 hours solving only 2 problems. In such cases, it’s better to go "all-in" for a burst of growth, as I did during the break. After investing almost all of January, I now feel that timed practice in Science is meaningful.

**Updated Study Goals:**

1. **Kim Dong-uk (Korean):** Understand the text clearly and strictly before solving. Don't rush; read accurately to avoid "back-and-forth" reading. It's the first thing I do in the morning, so I must train myself to read even when sleepy.
2. **Son Chang-bin (Grammar):** Solve "Grammar Practice" at least twice a week. Concepts are best learned through constant exposure. Read them at least once a day, even if it's right before bed.
3. **Kang Ki-won (Math):** Allocate 3 hours daily for *Ass* (Assignments) and review. Focus during class so no extra review time is needed. Remember the definition of **"Intensity."** Math is meant to be bored and frustrated through until you suddenly find yourself improved.
4. **Hyun Jung-hoon (Physics):** Review on the same day (Saturday). Approach problems with a "research" mindset for now. Increase the sheer volume of thought rather than just getting through problems.
5. **Kang Jun-ho (Chemistry):** No more excuses for falling behind on *Hwajaljuice*. Solve them daily. *FAST TRACK* should be solved at least twice a week (one on a weekday, one on Sunday) to keep the practical sense sharp.

The most important thing is to feel the unique joy and meaning of each subject. Unlike before, growth shouldn't be lopsided. I need the achievement of stimulating "all muscle groups" effectively. It’s like exercise—you go to the gym because of the *feeling* of getting stronger and lighter, even if you don't change instantly.

### III. Lotte Tower and <Harbin> Movie Review

I went to Lotte Tower. I make it a rule to leave Daechi-dong at least once a week. I usually go where there are many people. Observing various people who are *not* students and finding my place among them is vital. In Daechi-dong, people of the same age have the same thoughts, read the same books, and walk around with the same expressions. While this provides a focused environment, being buried only in the problem can be counterproductive.

[Image: Lotte Tower (Day), Lotte Tower (Night), Sushi eaten outside]

Walking through such a place alone, without my parents, was both exciting and scary. I realized that while my thoughts are developed in one direction, I am still at an elementary school level in others. Observing the couples at the London Bagel Museum, the Hermes store, and the aquarium, I felt a bit bittersweet. **Why can't I live an ordinary life like them?** Earning and spending money within the system, taking the CSAT as life flows, meeting a spouse according to my station, and enjoying life slowly.

This is because my life's value stems from a single question I asked on May 31, 2020:

> **“Why can’t I speak with a computer?”**

It was strange that I couldn't say a word to a computer that performs billions of calculations per millisecond. It took four years to realize the question itself was flawed and to refine my resolution:

> **“How can I design the appropriate hardware from the start?”**

I cannot do this alone. It requires interdisciplinary cooperation: medicine, philosophy, engineering, academia, capital, and government regulation. We need talent that combines engineering, biology, and will, rooted in the philosophical literacy of the humanities. If someone else with good intentions achieves this, I am glad. If not, I hope at least I can be that person.

The current "AI Agent" boom, flawed from the design stage, will soon see its bubble burst. Winter is coming.

> "The darkness will deepen and the wind will blow even more fiercely. We must light a fire. People will gather. Once they gather, we will carry our torches and walk through the darkness together."
> — *From the movie <Harbin>*

The word **Value** has two meanings: a numerical *price* and a moral *worth*. Wealth, academic background, and status are all "prices." Most people I know live and die in that world. However, if you harbor a grand question that ready-made answers cannot satisfy, you have decided to follow that question wherever it leads. This is the **Personal Legend** mentioned in *The Alchemist*.

Preparing for the CSAT feels the same. If martial power dominated the Middle Ages, thinking power is the standard now. One can increase competency as needed. A general is not just the best fighter; a CEO is not just the best engineer. A CEO is the one who most deeply feels the founding values of the company and will remain even when everyone else leaves.

I don't have many friends. When people meet an individual like me, they label me as a "loner," "socially inept misfit," or "an oddity living in his own world." Life is like a struggle to always remain stronger than society. To fall asleep at night, I have to exercise until my eyes close or take medication. A pain that cannot be expressed by the word "loneliness" weighs on my body all day. It’s because of a stubbornness I cannot abandon out of fear. When stubbornness is expressed beautifully, it is called **Conviction**.

The AI winter will come, and public interest will shrink miserably. I must found a company. Talents will gather. Once they gather, we will walk through the darkness together with our investment capital.

Even if adversity faces me, I cannot stop. If I don't get into medical school this year, I'll make another notebook next year, and the year after, for 10 or 100 years. I will not be satisfied until I have created a human by human hands.

If I gather talents, receive investment, cooperate with academia, respect philosophy, and communicate with religions, I will surely be able to answer the question I seek.

Until that day, we must not give up. We will walk through the darkness with our torches held high.
