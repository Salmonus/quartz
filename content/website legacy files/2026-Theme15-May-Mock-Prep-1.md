### I. You Cannot Connect the Dots if You Don't Mark Them First (Individual Psychology?)

Leaving the unique environment of the mountains after three weeks, I finally see where people live. I have returned to a "real reality" where the logic of power operates—a space where reality is suffocating and ideals feel distant, unlike the school where everything is tailored to my advantage and the ideal meets the real.

Home and family always remind me of my "origin point." For those who have run far to escape the pain and frustration of reality, the origin point is merely a re-confirmation of that pain. We set imaginary goals to delay the collapse of the ego, believing the real problems are too difficult to solve.

**Alfred Adler** viewed most of human psychological problems as stemming from a lack or distortion of "social interest." In other words, interpersonal relationships occupy a central position in human life, and many psychological difficulties start there. Furthermore, Adler posited that humans pursue "superiority" to overcome "inferiority complexes." While this can lead to self-development and achievement in a positive direction, it can manifest as an excessive desire for power or dominance in a negative one. He also saw "physical inferiority" as a significant factor that motivates individuals to set and achieve goals.

It could be said that the true problem of my life is a single issue of interpersonal relationships. Because I failed to resolve that psychological problem, many other difficulties arose, and physical inferiority acted as a deciding factor, providing the motivation to pursue superiority. Now that time has passed, a narrative has accumulated that allows me to escape primitive expressions, but the moment I step into the front door of my house, I realize that my beautiful and true courage to "change the world" was not the genuine starting point. Even now, on the brink of adulthood, I feel there is nothing I can do. Do you think I am not in pain? But this is a cross-section of 18-year-old Kim Yeon-woo’s life. It is an *Asura* realm, a purgatory where the logic of power and money crushes the inherent maturity of humans and the evolution of things. It is the world of the "Ma" (the demonic) itself—a cold, heartless animal kingdom.

When there is too much to say, one becomes sparing with words. Borrowing the words of others, I belong to the "blessed." I possess all the infrastructure, youth, talent, and opportunities to "live well"; if I simply plug myself into the formula for success, I would be the envy of all. This foolish child, given such a golden rope, should grab it quickly, but his mind is elsewhere. He is busy marking "dots."

There are plenty of children ready to help reproduce the elite dominance structure. My mother, grandmother, and younger sibling all hope I will quickly integrate into that world and give them 5 million KRW each per month. Yes, earning money is hard. I acknowledge that. I want to pat my mother on the back. But that is a result; it can never be the ultimate goal of a man's life. **"Seon-u-hu-rak" (先憂後樂)**—it is right to worry about the affairs of everyone first, and then worry about one's own welfare. My mother told me to remember An Jun-saeng, the son of the hero An Jung-geun, who lived like a dog. I once asked my sibling what kind of life they wanted. They replied that they hoped I would "succeed quickly" and provide them with financial support. I asked for the definition of success. Since they said it was "earning a lot of money," I asked if they knew the author Han Kang, Korea’s Nobel laureate. My sibling acted annoyed and said they didn't. I asked if the contribution she made to Korean literary history couldn't be called success, even if she earns less than a corporate executive. "Forget it, not earning money isn't success. Earning more is more success." I asked if their friends thought the same. "Of course, isn't that obvious?"

South Korean society is like a collection of "small hexagons." A "freak genius" who excels in only one corner might be a genius, but they cannot be called an "elite." Elite society prefers humans whose hexagons are tightly filled—the "regular hexagon" human. It pursues stability over innovation and reproduction over invention. In a word, it is conservative. Yet, they curse the conglomerates, criticize Samsung, and praise the "vibe" of Apple and the self-made billionaires of the world. My point is that I don't know if I should play for a team like that. If six people, each excellent in one corner, gather, they can create a "large hexagon" instead of a small one. If someone truly wants to contribute to this society—at least an individual who explores the possibilities—shouldn't they choose the most deficient corner and become an "edge-type" human to fill it?

I emphasize that this is the ability to ask **"Why?"**—the purpose, reason, and value of existence. But that is entirely contrary to the "Suneung spirit." That is the battle given to my life right now. It's true. Buyers don't choose AirPods over Galaxy Buds because the noise-canceling performance is exactly a certain number of Hz higher relative to the price. They are swept away because they feel a connection to Apple's unique vibe and pursuit—because it is beautiful. That is storytelling, and for that, you must start from the "Why." I felt that very few people do that. Conversely, I think I’m quite good at it. So, I must mark the "dots" of that world while simultaneously integrating into the South Korean elite society. As in Steve Jobs' famous speech, you cannot connect the dots looking forward. But if you don't at least "mark" the dots now, connecting them later will be even more impossible. **You cannot connect the dots if you don't mark them first.** That is my thought.

However, if that is my mindset, one might ask why I bother with the CSAT (Suneung) instead of sticking to the international track. It is because my thoughts do not reside only within South Korean society. On the contrary, I see **SNN (Spiking Neural Networks)** as the possibility that will save the world from the "AI Winter." Until middle school, I was interested in LeCun’s "World Model," but I judged that the limits of format-fixed Gradient-Based Learning are clear. That, again, is a matter belonging entirely to the fields of natural science and engineering. I judged that while marking dots, my basic math and science skills were far too insufficient to act as a researcher in academia. I examine what the world needs before what is advantageous for me. I believe that is where a sense of mission and professional vocation arises, which can trigger a true awakening of the world and the self. One must do what *must* be done, not just what one *wants* to do. It is not just wanting the gold; it is the process of evolving along with the world—the process of enjoying it. So, I must do well on the CSAT. I know what the world after the CSAT is. Even if logicizing my thoughts feels like "imitation crab meat," it is the precursor to tasting the "king crab." The narrow-mindedness of saying "I’ve tasted king crab, so how can I be satisfied with imitation?" is not allowed.

### II. Internal Forces Cannot Move the System

[Image: Physics diagram showing two blocks A and B connected by a string on an inclined plane. Force vectors for gravity, tension, and normal force are indicated.]

> Tension cannot do work on the "A+B System." Only the sum of the gravitational force acting on B and the component of gravity on the incline for A constitutes the Net Force.

How can we define a "protagonist"? Needless to say, it must be the main character who intervenes in and directly influences the narrative structure. But a narrative structure can be viewed as a "System" in literature or physics. Various mechanical interactions overlap to form a massive Net Force—the Theme.

**The problem is that internal forces cannot move the system.**

An individual *inside* the system can enjoy the elements within and be leisurely, but an individual *outside* the system who actually wants to move the system—one who does not belong to the internal forces—cannot afford to consume energy and enjoy the trivialities inside the narrative. That is where the word **"Dok-bo-jeok" (獨步的 - Unrivaled/Unmatched)** comes from. If we follow the literal meaning of the Hanja, it doesn't mean "excellent" or "superior" at all. It simply means **"walking alone."** Why? Because internal forces cannot move the system. An individual outside the system has no leisure to participate in the interactions within the system. The protagonist is thoroughly alone. Isolated from society, they engage in a true duel between the World and the Self. Such loneliness is the deepest kind of loneliness in quality—**essential loneliness.** It is not a depressing emotion felt just because no one is around, but the solitude of realizing that the "Righteous" must be alone, and that one must participate in the essential parts of life and death alone.

Why must one live to that extent? I don't know either. As Hyun Woo-jin said, I suppose I live this way because that's just how I was "built."
