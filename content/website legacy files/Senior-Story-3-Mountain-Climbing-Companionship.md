# Senior Story (3): Mountain Climbing Must Be a Companion's Journey

**By:** Kim Yeon-woo

**Platform:** Brunch

**TL;DR:** All academic pursuits are akin to mountain climbing. Furthermore, most journeys in life can be compared to the same. Therefore, the darker and more rugged the mountain, the more we must travel "together."

---

### I. The Peak

The Academic Year 2026 June Mock Evaluation (conducted on June 4, 2025) has ended.

I feel as though I’ve been struck. I looked back at how much I had let go of my studies this past May, and what my mind was desperately trying to hold onto while my body drifted away. I think I was seeking "Enlightenment"—or perhaps just motivation. Much like Master Zhaozhou’s advice to "kill the Buddha if you meet him," I thought I had internalized the teaching from Hesse’s *Siddhartha* that "Enlightenment cannot be sought through words." Yet, I committed the same childish mistake again.

It wasn't just a mistake; when I lay in bed at night, I couldn't stop crying. The practice mocks felt hollow, the weekly workbooks were walls that had grown too high, the mornings were drowsy, and the nights were exhausting. Above all, a sense of loneliness—asking *"Is this the end?"*—overwhelmed my entire body.

In competitive testing, one must use the **"Chicken-slaying knife"** and the **"Cow-slaying knife"** in their proper places. To manage time in the Science section, difficult problems require sophisticated quantitative tools, while easy problems require rapid, qualitative shortcuts. One can achieve a high Grade 2 or a low Grade 1 in Math or Science using only the "Cow-slaying knife" (brute-forcing equations with multiple variables); it just takes time. The problem was the "Chicken-slaying knife." If you approach a "chicken" problem with a "cow" tool, the path is often blocked from the start.

The intuition of a perfect score involves exhausting questions #1 through #19 with daggers, shurikens, and tacks, saving the Greatsword only for the head-on collision at Question #20. The same applies to Math questions #15, #22, and #30.

This is where I felt the void. Up until my junior year and early senior year, I felt I was following the "Orthodox Path"—swinging a heavy blade and always hitting the mark. But suddenly, I felt I wasn't improving; instead of grand solution sets, I was just collecting small, fragmented pieces. Though they say this is the path from a low Grade 1 to a perfect 100, I lost strength before even opening my books, feeling that my scores might actually drop despite studying all day. The phrase "there is no enlightenment" sounded like "all humans die." It might be true, but it lacked warmth or consideration for those actually walking the path.

Yet, I had to move forward. I forced myself to open and close books, paced the playground, watched lectures, thought of every possible word of blame and rejection in solitude, and endured sleepless nights thinking that an answer requiring self-consolation is no answer at all. This is the life of an examinee, but a sense of crisis took hold: if this "feeling of enduring" doesn't transition into a "feeling of overwhelming," the remaining five months could turn into one year and five months.

> People point their fingers / At only the high and jagged peaks
> I was alone / Climbing toward the highest peak I knew
> Have I climbed too high? / Have I traveled too far?
> There’s not much left now... / Forget it!
> Just keep climbing no matter what...
> The hardship now is nothing.

### II. Owl Hunting

My Brunch profile picture is **"Minerva's Owl."** It is taken from Hegel’s famous line: the owl of Minerva, symbolizing philosophy and wisdom, spreads its wings only with the falling of the dusk. While a practical attitude is necessary for those living in the moment, practicing philosophy arrives only after the moment has passed. *"Philosophy is too late..."* That is the lament of the philosopher.

One who possesses both the "hand of an engineer" (practical contribution) and the "tongue of a philosopher" (speculative temperament) inevitably becomes the designer of the next generation. While I poignantly feel my own insufficiency for such a position, I have never doubted that someone must do it. My dream is to be an AI engineer. Whether it is **Bottom-up Emergence** (Hinton, Bengio), **Top-down Structural Design** (LeCun), **Bio-based** (Poggio), or **Self-referential Symbolism** (Bach, Hofstadter)—the moment anyone figures it out, they gain the status of a mythical creator.

It wasn't Wittgenstein who first taught me that the meaning of words is not invariant; it was a sentiment analysis classifier on Github. I study for the CSAT, but I have never let go of my contemplations on the **"Philosophy of AI."** This generation, so to speak, is one that can only be saved if the Owl takes flight in the *morning*. Discussions on **AI Alignment** are at a level where "thinking about it later" could mean the end of humanity. I wish someone would define a "thinking machine" as Einstein did for the universe, but that can only be done by a multidisciplinary researcher who can translate *being alive* (Medicine) and *thinking* (Philosophy) into *structure* (Engineering).

Turing left us the beginning and the end: the question *"Can machines think?"* and the ultimate benchmark, the **"Turing Test."** We only have "pop quizzes" like BLEU or N-gram scores to fill that Black Box. I apologize for the rambling. However, I wanted to say that in the 21st century, **"Owl Hunting"** must be performed proactively. This blog is doing the same for the CSAT. Before achieving the actual score, asking questions like *"Why am I doing this?"*, *"What is the ethics of this action?"*, and *"Was there no other way?"* is my own small strategy for the exam.

### III. The Place I Reached was Just a Mountain Pass

> But the place I reached / Was just a mountain pass
> The path leads again to another peak
> Sitting on that broken tree stump / I saw
> The sea that flows only to the low places / And pools there
> Small boats going by, puffing smoke...
> If ever pain should pierce you
> Think of the sea then
> The sea... / That a peak is just a pass you cross...

I believe there is no "Enlightenment" in the general sense, other than the journey of pursuing it. It is like answering **"Mu" (無)** to the question "Does a dog have Buddha-nature?" It is a warning to deconstruct the question itself and a shout to look back at yourself for asking it.

However, even so, there are times when it feels like too much. When peaks lead to more peaks, and the ideal I held highest turns out to be just a pass, and pain starts to seep in—must I stay huddled up? Is it because "others are enduring it too," and thus "you alone are falling behind and weak"? That might be, but would the intellect accumulated that way ever become a **Center of Gravity** that integrates society? A center of gravity is defined by the coordinates of the vertices. Only when the coordinates of the most marginal, disrespected individuals receive equal force does it earn the title of a Center of Gravity.

Ultimately, **mountain climbing (山行) must be a companion's journey (同行).** To those who haven't climbed, only the summit is visible; but those actually climbing lose strength and feel the path is endless. You might walk a local hill alone, but the CSAT is certainly not at that level. It is a journey led by the footprints of many, but it is not a flatland where everyone reaches the summit. If even the CSAT is like this, how distant must be the mountain of Artificial Intelligence discovered by Lovelace and opened by Turing?

Of course, we must move forward. We will clash, and at the end of that clash, a new path will open. The place we reach might treat even the name "AI" as an antique. If you are currently burning through your life, I hope you turn your eyes to the **"person next to you"** instead of a perfect score or a specific salary. Even if there are no peaks, the fact that we are all headed toward one is an undeniable truth. And that fact is much warmer and more bearable than the command that "there is no enlightenment."

From the "Chicken-slaying knives" of the CSAT to the details of programming, the strokes of Hanja, the unfamiliar grammar of Latin, and the rigor of mathematics—the road to completion is long and winding. Rather than seeking absolute completion, it is better to first **complete the day.** Because a day is long, and a year is short. That is how we make our way to Latium.

> **Per varios casus, per tot discrimina rerum tendimus in Latium;**
> *The suffix **'-mus'** is the first-person plural marker.*
