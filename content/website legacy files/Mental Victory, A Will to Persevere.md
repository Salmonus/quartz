**Date:** July 10, 2024, 01:44

**Status:** Private Post

### The Breakthrough

"Wait... I see it! I can keep up with the rhythm!"

I’m not entirely sure if I’ll be able to keep the promises I hold dear, but I’ve put up a good fight against a formidable opponent. I can now see the structure of the exam papers; specifically, I can feel that Mathematics and Chemistry have become "doable." Things that I wouldn't have dared to touch before are starting to fall within the realm of challenge. At least for the subjects I prepared for, there are no longer any "throwaway questions," and I’ve become capable of managing the entire exam paper.

### Looking Back at the "Humble" Beginnings

Until last November, my Grade 11 June mock exam scores were still in the 60s. I even had to search the internet because I didn't know the formula for the sum of an arithmetic sequence. When the second-semester finals ended, I was so hopelessly stuck on *Gojangi Mathematics I* that I frequently went to a classmate in the International Track to ask for help—so often that they looked at me with disbelief.

During the winter break, I was shaken by self-loathing. Unable to bear the loneliness, I would go to a crowded cafe only to open *Maple Textbook Mathematics II*—a basic concept book. I felt pathetic, but that only fueled the fire within. I had to prove that my passion wouldn't be extinguished by something like this.

**My Mathematical Progression:**

* **Math I:** 87 (1st Exam)  79 (2nd Exam)
* **Math II:** 58 (1st Exam)  76 (2nd Exam)
* **Math Mock Exam:** 76 (March)  **96 (June)**

---

### Confirmation of Possibility, Lack of Internalization

Being able to see through the structure of the problems is something to celebrate, but because I lacked the time to truly "internalize" it, it remained merely a "confirmation of possibility."

To guard against complacency, I set aside one hour during every Night Study 1 session to solve Grade 12 (Year 3) mock exams for practical sense. During Night Study 2, I focused on Science Electives (mainly Chemistry). Anyone who regularly saw me out for a walk or a snack would know this.

However, the school finals—with their trap-laden, "final-esque" structures so different from the clean patterns of the Evaluation Institute papers—constantly caught me in their snares under the 50-minute time limit. Just the day before the exam, I was full of confidence: thinking I could cover Math I with explosive calculation speed and that I could grasp the situation and draw the graph for any Math II problem. But this final exam remained just a confirmation of potential, not a realization of it.

### The Philosophy of "Deliberate Practice"

Should I say I did well despite the mental distractions during the exam period? No. If I’m still at this level, it means I haven't solved the problems with enough certainty. I shouldn't do this just to show others. I must strive consciously, without sparing my body, using every ounce of my being.

I must use my plans to ensure that I do neither too little nor too much, regardless of whether my heart is racing or if it’s a task I loathe. No matter how much I hate a problem, I must check every case until the words **"That’s easy~"** spontaneously pop out of my mouth before moving to the next one. Even if the surroundings are noisy, even if people belittle, "correct," or try to cut me down—no matter what anyone says—I will give it my all. In fact, I know that being tired and distracted provides the best environment for **deliberate practice.**

---

### The Treadmill of Life and the CSAT

Ultimately, what separates the top from the rest is how one confronts the same fatigue, the same immature heart, and the same lack of will and mental strength that everyone faces. Beyond the excitement I feel when seeing a problem, I’ve realized that I must understand my own personality and habits in the act of problem-solving. Only then can I be free from the pressure of the time limit.

> When I'm on the treadmill, I want to get off immediately. Yet, once I'm off, I lament, "Why didn't I run harder?"
> Life is the same. There are moments when I want to drop everything—including my AP Latin score of 2. But eventually, I will miss the thrill of surviving amidst this uncertainty, ambiguity, loneliness, and physical pain. I will miss the realization of the value of life’s fininitude.

I am already addicted. I will crave this sense of existential growth for the rest of my life. I love this magnificent treadmill called the **CSAT (Suneung)**, and I am profoundly grateful for it. It’s okay if people around me don't call my name yet. Is there anyone else who loves the CSAT as much as I do?

---

### Conclusion

I will not forget that the journey is the reward. Whether or not I keep my promises, I will run at full speed again starting tomorrow.

Regardless of the outcome, I have someone by my side who supports me and believes that my effort alone is remarkable. To me, this is more precious than talent. Even if that person is no longer by my side, as long as my heart doesn't change, I can prove myself at the next opportunity. Since they said they see and hear what they want to see and hear from me anyway, I’ll just think in a way that suits me. Just wait a little longer...
