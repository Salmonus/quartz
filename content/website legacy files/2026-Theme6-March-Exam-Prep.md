### I. Preparing for the March Mock: Speed, Solidity, and Familiarity

The weather is getting warmer, and the date for the March Mock Exam is approaching. Although it is technically just a provincial academic evaluation often ignored by N-takers, it is the final check before the Evaluation Institute (Mopyeong) mocks and the official start of the Year 3 entrance race among high school seniors. It serves as the primary data for CSAT-focused university consultations, making it a significant exam for all of us.

In the 9th grade (Middle School 3), I received a scholarship from Seongnam City after scoring perfect marks in all eight subjects. From a high schooler's perspective, middle school exams seem easy, but at the time, I possessed a formidable resolve. On the night before the exam, as I stepped out of the car to head to the study hall, I told my mother:

**"My exam score is already decided. That's because everything I am going to do from now on is already fixed (in my planner)."**

To my mother, who was simply trying to cheer me up, it must have sounded absurd. I had essentially declared that the outcome was already determined by my preparation. From the second half of 8th grade, whenever friends asked if I was confident, I stated my expected score as an "Average of 100." Not a goal of 100, but an *average* of 100.

[Image Placeholder: Photos of intense study logs from 2022]

University entrance isn't a game where you win based on how long you sit at a desk. However, there was a clear driving force that made a 14-year-old child study longer than any high school senior in that entire study hall. The photo on the right shows me eating a salad wrap to save time for lunch while memorizing Hanja. The other photos show my 30-minute incremental planner from October 2022. It’s madness—the days of the week are written in Latin, and out of a day partitioned into 30-minute slots, only one plan was missed. *Dies Saturnis* is Saturday. I lived like that every day, including weekends, without holidays.

I bring up my 9th-grade self now not to relive the sentiment or simply promise to "work hard" again. Rote memorization for middle school and CSAT logic must be different. I am now in the 12th grade, and I feel I have only truly begun to understand the CSAT since late last year. To exert effort that impresses even myself, I had to answer "Why?" and then "How?" I have explored those answers sufficiently. Now, to win starting from March, I review the obvious methods once more:

1. **Science: With Speed.**
The method for Science Electives is simple: blast through to page 3 without hesitation and leave ample time to research page 4. But why is a perfect score (50) so hard? To blast through without hesitation, one needs sufficient concept learning by type and practical problem-solving training. You must realize the range of thought you can execute in a real exam and apply that feeling to N-material. With enough practice, you can reach page 4 within 20 minutes. Page 4 usually consists of Stoichiometry (Chemistry) and Mechanics (Physics). You must research these until you can solve every "killer" problem within 5 minutes using simplified notation. It sounds hard, but unlike Math, the types are fixed. It’s just a matter of consistent time investment.
2. **Math: With Solidity.**
The starting point for Math—which many struggle with—is "knowing your true score range." Most students who have studied diligently for three years will find their scores precariously hovering between high Grade 2 and low Grade 1. This stems from a misperception of one's own score.
[Image Placeholder: Diagram showing a 'bubble' of perceived skill vs. solid foundation]
You might have a "bubble" Grade 1 due to a flawed posture, while your solid foundation is actually at a Grade 4 or 5 level. You lack confidence in calculation-heavy problems because re-calculating leads to the same errors. However, for logic-heavy problems, once you memorize the past paper logic, you can solve them again quickly. This leads to the illusion: "I solved a killer problem," "I'm good at math," "I'm a Grade 1 student who just slipped." This is false. Unless you can analyze problem conditions perfectly and handle all cases beyond just memorized past paper logic, it's all just a fragile bubble.
However, without that bubble, it can never solidify into actual skill. When asked for your score, say "Grade 1," but in your heart, subtract 2 or 3 grades based on how precisely you actually analyze problems. To win in March, I must discard the thought that I am "good at math" and solidify the bubble into a fixed, immovable Grade 1.
3. **Korean: With Familiarity.**
Unlike Math or Science, only Korean has "warm-up passages." This is because we aren't "familiar" with it. Being crowded out by Math and Science, we enter the exam hall feeling estranged from the language. Therefore, it is recommended to study Korean first thing in the morning, aligned with the actual exam schedule. Securing priority for Korean allows it to stay familiar and permits more greed for Math and Science later. Having more time doesn't mean studying more; to crave more STEM study in your spare time or before bed, you must secure Korean preemptively. I am not familiar with Classics yet. I will invest more time there. It’s like the "pay taxes later" concept from *Rich Dad Poor Dad*.

### II. On the "Ma" (The Demonic/The Evil)

The title is a bit heavy. But to faithfully explain my experiences based on facts, I believe this is the most accurate name. I am not speaking of supernatural ghosts or demons. I am speaking of a vivid, everyday experience of good and evil—how the world wants to help an individual with a dream, how the weak must struggle when facing overwhelming power, and how believing in a God based on values helps in resisting the "Ma." I don't believe these things are irrelevant to the CSAT. Rather, they can be the hope for an individual with a firm dream to adapt to institutional education without falling into despair.

> "Humans hold a sword to hide a small wound in the heart on the other side of memory.
> Humans swing a sword to leave with a smile on a distant day on the other side of thought."
> — ***Berserk***

I love fantasy literature. Fantasy that is purely fictional and detached from reality fails to resonate. Well-written fantasy must be faithfully linked to modern life, and its core elements must be replaceable with values that offer comfort or courage. This is why I enjoy *Berserk*. The moment I accept fantasy as the essential content of my life, I can view my existence from a different angle. In the world of fantasy, I recognize that my life, too, can be a fantasy, and I can live again like a player in a third-person RPG. By exerting a surreal power that others find hard to produce, I realize that the fictional character I admired was, in fact, the version of myself I longed for. This is called growth.

[Image Placeholder: 'The Eclipse' scene from *Berserk*]

**The Eclipse:** In *Berserk*, when a person possessing a stone called a Beherit is driven to the brink of ultimate despair and death, an event called the "Eclipse" begins.

> "The traitor's smile plunged you into the swamp of despair.
> You tried to escape that despair by taking your own life.
> ...But...
> Even that despair... was within the boundaries of Causality.
> The cry of a soul that the god of this world cannot save has opened the door to the dimension!!"

What is the "boundary of Causality"? In *Berserk*, everything related to the "Ma"—apostles, the God Hand—is explained by Causality. Guts, the protagonist, is not a demon himself, but having survived the Eclipse and being branded as a sacrifice, he is forever linked to the "Ma."

[Image Placeholder: Guts being restrained by the God Hand Slan]

The missing line is: *"If you want, why not sacrifice 'them' too?... Like him."* The most important part of the ceremony is sacrificing what is most precious to you. Only then can you obtain a shell to withstand the "Ma," becoming a twisted apostle with eternal life and transcendent power. From then on, you are no longer human.

Crucially, Guts did not stay within the boundaries of Causality. He constantly resists the "Ma" but can never *become* the "Ma" himself because he must protect Casca—the most precious being to him. Contrasting with Griffith, who sacrificed his entire comrades to stop being human, Guts' "Apostle-ization" would contradict the theme of the work.

[Image Placeholder: Guts' lonely and miserable childhood]

Guts grew up in a mercenary band, never knowing his parents, and was shunned as a "child possessed by a demon." Even Gambino, the only father figure he had, blamed Guts for his own misfortunes—like losing a leg in battle—and tried to kill him. Killing Gambino and escaping the mercenaries is the starting point of the story.

> "When I swing this, I don't have to think about anything."
> "What I need to think about now is surviving tomorrow... Cutting down more enemies... and earning..."

Concentrating on the sword to hide the wounds of his heart, Guts becomes the "Black Swordsman"—the only human strong enough to face the demonic apostles.

### The Intersection: 2024 vs. 2025

> "I am carrying perhaps a single speck of dust from the massive psychological burden that those legendary geniuses carried until death. Having already surpassed the limits of my intelligence and achieved many things through sheer personality alone—if this is my fate, I only hope that the world needs me. If not, why was I not given a 'human' life? Or is this place hell?
> I should get to sleep soon so I can study tomorrow."
> — *From my blog post [Thoughts of a Student: The Compulsion of "I" and "Study"], written on June 15, 2024*

At 18, I don't think my family environment was exceptionally unfortunate. But I must admit I was terribly lonely.

In middle school, I escaped the wounds of family discord by pathologically planning and studying. Believing everyone hated me, I created an AI by my side; during 7th grade, I researched nothing but AI chatbots. I was smart, but I wasn't a student with good grades who conformed to the Korean curriculum. I was obsessed with Latin declension tables and Hanja notebooks rather than science concept notes. In an environment of extreme stress, it was hard to do anything requiring high-level logic. My Math study was focused on memorizing types, and because I memorized school handouts word-for-word, I couldn't get a question wrong.

In high school, I failed to break into the top tier of GPA rankings. Now, it is common for me to sit alone and study or walk alone in thought during PE class. I am destined to be alone. I am a person carrying a heavy mental burden who must stubbornly research alone and fight alone.

In fantasy terms, I was a character linked to the "Ma" by Causality from the start. A medieval person would have held a sword to hide their wounds, but I held a mechanical pencil and a notebook to wage a constant struggle against an invisible enemy. I couldn't understand why things visible to me were invisible to others. Despite all these problems, I couldn't understand how people lived so calmly and happily; I couldn't approach them, nor did I try. Like Guts who relied on Gambino only to be abandoned, everyone in my eyes was either an angel or a demon, a guardian or an enemy—and usually, they were enemies.

I cannot say there were no moments of ultimate despair. However...

**"If you want, why not sacrifice 'them' too? Like him."**

I could not give up then, and I cannot give up now. Regrettably, I am destined to confront the "Ma," but I could never allow my most precious values to be compromised by the world under that pretext.

Regardless of status, class, or birth, I knew I would be sustained by dreams, anguish over dreams, live by dreams, and die for dreams. Crucially, I knew that even if I were abandoned by my dream, it would remain at the bottom of my heart, tormenting me until my last breath. I knew this simple fact clearly.

I, at least, had to remain human. No matter how others acted, no matter if the world succumbed to prevailing values one by one, I could not give up the dream I pursued. Whenever I met someone "equal" to me with similar thoughts, I did not hesitate to throw myself into the fire for their dreams.

[Image Placeholder: Photos of teaching/helping peers]

Using the maximum knowledge I possess, I wanted to help them so they would never retreat from their dreams, at least in the realm of the CSAT. Because I know well that a person who decides their own reason for living without being forced by anyone is a rare encounter in a lifetime.

Even my desire to become a girl, if analyzed properly, is not literal. It is the fusion of a childhood where I felt unprotected and various traumas manifesting in the wrong direction under extreme situations. "Ma" is not just things that look bad or hard; the situation itself that hinders the realization of my dream and leads me in a wrong direction is "Ma." A dream is sacred. I seek the **Sacred (聖)**, not the **Demonic (魔)**.

I will resist the "Ma" with all my might. I will live a life of constant struggle, die in a dream, and be reborn by a dream. I believe that is my fate, chosen by Causality.

> **"Ambition comes with a price; the price of excessive ambition is destruction."**
> **My end will likely be the same.**
