# Senior Story (10): The Reason Why People Let Me Live

**By:** Kim Yeon-woo

**Platform:** Brunch

**TL;DR:** Survival in modern society is a probationary period granted by excess resources. To survive beyond this period, one must sharpen "human claws"—the systemic tools of collaboration, language, and rules—by balancing existential depth (Dostoevsky Mode) with structural analysis (Turing Mode).

### I. Shifting the Question

Answering "Why do I live?" is difficult. Answering "How am I alive?" is relatively easy. I intend to analyze the reasons for my survival thus far—the various natural accidents that have kept me alive. To state the conclusion first, there are two reasons: first, a "reprieve" has been granted due to an overabundance of resources; second, within that reprieve, I have unconsciously proven several things already.

Humans are not animals that walk immediately upon birth. Gorillas beat their chests, lions hunt, and salmon swim upstream. The difference for humans is that our life trajectory comes not just from DNA, but from the **system**. If you do not conform to the system, the system has no reason to let you live. If you do? You are qualified to survive.

If you were to dissect a skull, you would see the prefrontal cortex covering our reptilian-era decision-making organs. Human society is strikingly similar to this. Animalistic instincts—aggression, avoidance, and reproduction—provide the immediate energy, while reason tunes that energy to make cooperation possible. This is not a surprising fact. However, this "un-surprising" fact clearly reveals the balance of power and the mechanisms of survival of the fittest inherent in human society. Within this tightrope act, I can discern why I was allowed to live.

Let's look at why I am even telling this story. Simply put, I need it. But why? Most humans get along fine without such explanations; why do I think so complexly? Two reasons:

1. I did not feel safe in my home, the place that should be most comfortable. Experiences of conditional affection demand an explanation for attachment.
2. I did not adapt well to school. The experience of "transparently drifting"—not being explicitly bullied or harassed, but simply being an outsider—forces a choice between "I am the problem" and "Society is the problem." The moment I think "Society is the problem," I must analyze that society.

Humans often forget they are part of the **Great Apes** (gorillas, chimpanzees, bonobos, orangutans, and humans). We share high-level self-awareness (mirror test), lack of tails, tool use, and over 95% genetic similarity. Yet, aren't humans special? Darwin was mocked for generations for his monkey cartoons. Yuval Harari has repeatedly addressed this in *Sapiens* and *Homo Deus*:

> "Hence the existence of souls cannot be squared with the theory of evolution. Evolution means change, and is incapable of producing everlasting entities. From an evolutionary perspective, the closest thing we have to a human essence is our DNA, and the DNA molecule is the vehicle of mutation rather than the seat of eternity. This terrifies large numbers of people, who prefer to reject the theory of evolution rather than give up their souls."
> — **Yuval Noah Harari, *Homo Deus***

Yes, humans are special. But we are also part of the natural world. In primary school, we learn to distinguish "natural environments" from "human environments," but this is merely a normalization through repeated exposure to a fictional peace. In a state of nature, organisms with traits unsuitable for the environment die. Giraffes with short necks, fish without gills, oversized insects—all died. They starved, suffocated, or were eaten. Humans are no exception; there is only a reprieve. An individual who fails to adapt to the system dies. However, because the brain is a very expensive organ, the system had to watch for about 20 years to allow for full development. If you cannot prove by that age that you have adapted to the mainstream, you are coldly excluded and face social death. Only 40 years ago, this might have meant physical death as well.

Every animal has its own "claws." I will now explain the specific "human claws" one must prove to survive.

### II. Human Claws

#### R-Strategy: High Fecundity, Low Investment (The Cat)

Cats are animals where ovulation occurs only upon mating. Without mating, the heat cycle continues for days or weeks. This is why spaying/neutering is encouraged. It is an **R-strategy**: produce many offspring, spread them fast, and do not invest for long. In the face of an uncertain future, the strategy is to try many times so that at least a few survive.

#### K-Strategy: Low Fecundity, High Investment (The Human)

Humans ovulate periodically regardless of mating. This structure implies a strategy of careful ovulation and, if fertilized, spending vast amounts of time and resources to raise the offspring. While human females may experience increased libido during ovulation, it is expressed through subtle changes in language, expression, and behavior—increased sensory sensitivity, improved skin luster, pupil dilation. These signals operate within relationships and trust, testing the "possibility of stable investment."

> While a cat shouts, "Let's mate right now," a human ovulates while evaluating, "Can I raise a child and spend years with this person?"

#### The Nature of Human Claws

While R-strategy animals use visceral heat behaviors, **human claws are invisible tools: care, cooperation, and the system.** Success after reproduction is more important for humans. An infant cannot survive alone for years. Thus, humans maintain libido and bonding instincts long after ovulation and pregnancy. Sexual activity for pleasure rather than reproduction is a survival strategy predicated on long-term partnership and cooperative care.

In this process, humans use **systemic claws**: kinship, language, division of labor, tools, and norms. We survive within structures that maintain relationships and create rules. We chose to protect a few lives for a long time and take responsibility for them until the end. Long-term trust and social alliances are more important than short-term pleasure.

Cats stake their lives on mating; humans stake their lives on forming relationships and following rules within the regime. Human claws are invisible, yet they made us the apex predator.

#### The Reason Why They Let Me Live

People have let me live for two reasons: I am still within the **"Human Probationary Period,"** and there is a historical precedent that eugenics leads to totalitarianism—and totalitarianism lost to the Allies.

The "Probationary Period" isn't about being loved or cared for out of the goodness of people's hearts. It is a period for the system to weigh whether "you will be a benefit to us or not."

* **0–7 years:** Must learn language, walking, and basic survival skills.
* **7–12 years:** Train in basic learning, social norms, and cooperation.
* **13–19 years:** Must excel in one or two fields or at least meet the average.
If you fail these stages before age 19, the system classifies you as an investment failure. Even if it lets you live, it pushes you into the reserve labor force or the marginalized class. A "death penalty" of being treated as a non-person begins.

Totalitarianism justifies the sacrifice of the few for the regime. But if you kill one, why not two? Why not a hundred? Millions of "inferiors" died, but if eugenics actually produced a "superior" race, they should have won the war. They didn't. This proved that human survival and innovation are higher when we invest in **diversity itself**. Thus, modern states and corporations outwardly champion human rights and inclusion. It is economically and politically more efficient to cheaply "store the possibility of future utility" than to exclude.

However, this inclusion is only maintained under the premise of **abundance**. When food, energy, or safety becomes scarce, the cost of inclusion spikes. The system will first cut those with the lowest **Return on Investment (ROI)**. At that moment, I become an expenditure item rather than a stored possibility.

I realized: I am alive now because there was enough surplus that it wasn't worth the effort to kill me. If a crisis comes, exclusion—which is synonymous with death—awaits. Therefore, I must sharpen my human claws during this reprieve. I must master collaboration, language, and systemic adaptability to become an asset with investment value, not a useless surplus.

### III. The White-hat Hacker

But must we move simply "to survive"? No. Aiming only for survival is like choosing only one route in a game. Some games have a "non-violent route" where you reach the end without fighting; others involve constant combat to gain experience. Humans are the same. While we must sometimes press the "attack" button to survive day-to-day, a true survival strategy includes realizing why we are pressing that button and knowing when to stop.

I call these two modes of thinking **"Dostoevsky Mode"** and **"Turing Mode."**

* **Dostoevsky Mode (Top-down):** Starts with fundamental questions like "Why do I live?" or "Why do people suffer?" It digs into the human being from the bottom up through themes of emotion, belief, guilt, and internal pain. Writing this very post is an act of Dostoevsky Mode. It is philosophical and sometimes reaches painful, lethargic conclusions, but it touches the most intimate truths of being.
* **Turing Mode (Bottom-up):** Observes structures and builds upward. "Why does this system work this way?" "What algorithm makes people act like this?" It understands the big picture by dividing complex problems into small components—logic, conditions, and mechanical configurations over emotions.

#### Two Modes Coexist

Top-down and bottom-up, emotion and structure, questions and algorithms. These are not opposites but two axes required to understand the human whole.

* Dostoevsky Mode "carves away" the interior.
* Turing Mode "builds up" from the exterior.

Until now, I have written all my posts with "carving" questions. But that is not enough. Now, like Turing Mode, I must become a person who observes the system and "hacks" its conditions—a **White-hat Hacker**. Only then can I be someone who creates a *reason* to survive, not just someone who survives. Only then can the effort of those who let me live "because of future possibility" transform into real potential.

Perhaps the attempt to remain one's self while agonizing in the gap between these two modes is what creates true prose. I am currently sharpening myself. It is not just CSAT study to adapt to the regime, nor is it arming myself just to endure. I am refining my claws so that I do not remain an "ununderstandable" person, but someone who might one day be understood. These claws are for surviving through words, questions, rules, and relationships.

> "I approached economics not simply as a matter of making a living, but as a journey of life. Through this competition, I gained the belief that I can fulfill a social role even while walking an unproven path outside the designated road of college admissions."
> — **Kim Yeon-woo (2023 Hankyung Economics Competition Award Speech)**
