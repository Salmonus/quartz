# Birds and Cages: The Secret to Continuous Freudian Sublimation

**By:** Kim Yeon-woo

**Platform:** Brunch

**Note:** *Independent Post*

---

I am currently in a cafe in Chungmuro. I live in Namyangju. It must have taken at least two hours to get here. One cannot make this kind of trip every day, yet I find myself undertaking this journey almost daily. What is the reason? Why do I frequently indulge in such reckless behavior? Answering these questions will be the theme of today’s writing.

I have always felt that I must write this type of piece. Much like Sartre’s *What Is Literature?*—which decisively contributed to his Nobel Prize (and his subsequent refusal of it)—writing about literature itself is the pinnacle of meta-cognition. Such writing directly reflects the author’s caliber. Rationality is simply another word for meta-cognition, and since all my writings are about rationality, this is a piece that had to be written at least once.

### I. The Paradox of the Word

We often describe the human spirit in two parts: reason and emotion, much like the Northern and Southern Hemispheres of Earth. However, concepts like the Northern Hemisphere, Southern Hemisphere, or the Equator do not actually exist in a physical sense. Crossing the zero-degree latitude on a boat doesn't reveal a visible physical line. Yet, humans call it so, and I shall do the same.

Hermann Hesse dealt with this problem specifically in *Siddhartha*, where he personifies Gotama Siddhartha (the Buddha) into two figures: the Exalted One, Gotama, and the Brahmin boy, Siddhartha. Gotama is the Buddha we know—the one who achieved enlightenment and gathered followers. Siddhartha is the boy who walks the path of the Samana to seek his own enlightenment. The core proposition Hesse pours his heart into in this novel is: **"Enlightenment cannot be conveyed through words."** Yet, he attempts to convey that very enlightenment through the *words* of literature. This is the greatest philosophical tension of *Siddhartha* and Buddhism itself—a self-referential contradiction. If enlightenment cannot be told, then even that fact shouldn't be tellable. It is, simply put, a wordplay like "This sentence is a lie."

> "Words do not express thoughts very well. They always become a little different immediately after they are expressed, a little distorted, a little foolish. And yet it also pleases me and seems right that what is of value and wisdom to one man seems nonsense to another."
> ...
> "For this reason, teachings are of no use to me. They have no hardness, no softness, no colors, no edges, no smell, no taste; they have nothing but words. Perhaps that is what prevents you from finding peace, perhaps it is those many words. For even salvation and virtue, even Sansara and Nirvana, are only words, Govinda. There is no such thing as Nirvana; there is only the word Nirvana."
> ...
> "I know," Govinda said. "But that is exactly what the Exalted One recognized as an illusion. What he commands us to possess is goodwill, forgiving, compassion, patience—not love. He forbade us to bind our hearts in love for earthly things."
> "I know that, Govinda. But look, here we are in the midst of a forest of opinions, arguing over this and that word. We are arguing because I cannot deny that my words about love contradict the words of Gotama—at least on the surface. For this reason, I distrust words so much, for I know that this contradiction is an illusion. I know that I am in agreement with Gotama."
> — **Hermann Hesse, *Siddhartha***

In this text, I intend to share my secret for "stuffing" emotion so that it sublimates into reason and provides continuous vitality to life. However, one should not think that reason and emotion are entirely separate entities, nor that emotion is a substance that simply converts into a logical proposition. Any logical proposition, viewed in isolation, is a hollow sound. What is truly meaningful is the eternal present—the emotion of the moment.

Yet, human time is finite, and we must move forward. "Moving forward" is also an illusion. Think of it like a single-stroke drawing; it means not retreading the same path. Nietzsche said all great thoughts occur while walking, and there is a reason for that. Walking is a fundamental instinct of a bipedal human. Footprints distinguish the path taken from the new path. In the prehistoric era, when food was exhausted, the ability to pioneer new territory was essential. Thus, I believe that for a human, it is not necessary to repeat an action from scratch to recall a past emotion or desire; it can be effectively compressed through language.

### II. Freudian Sublimation and Olfactory Awakening

But why "Freudian Sublimation"? I am a young person, and specifically, a young person in the early stages of transition. Sigmund Freud is well known for his research on the relationship between sexual desire and the unconscious. Any act of turning soft tissue into eternal stone—like petrified wood—or replacing emotion with logic, can be called sublimation. When this concerns sexual desire, it is aptly called Freudian Sublimation.

Since I have recently started Hormone Replacement Therapy (HRT), I vividly remember the expression of sexual desire and its sublimation methods as a male, as well as the emerging methods as a female. There is no better time to compare and contrast these to draw practical conclusions.

To state my conclusion: I feel that male sexual desire is primarily triggered by the **visual and auditory**, while female desire is triggered by **smell and touch**. Even if you disagree, I can offer a plausible basis for this in the structure of germ cells. Sperm follows a one-to-many structure. In theory, one ejaculation can reach multiple eggs. It is likely not a coincidence that male infidelity is statistically more frequent. While not every man is attracted to multiple women, the germ cell is designed that way.

Furthermore, humans struggle to smell two things at once; the olfactory nerve fatigues easily. It is also difficult to focus on multiple tactile inputs simultaneously. However, hearing and sight allow for multiple simultaneous inputs. This is my hypothesis: the mechanism of sexual arousal diverged based on whether the sensory input allows for multiple simultaneous streams.

When I lived as a boy, I was visually attracted to female faces and bodies, but I never felt drawn to a scent or imagined a tactile sensation. Now, I am in my second week of estrogen. I have been taking anti-androgens for three months. My hormonal levels are identical to those of a biological female. I have gained a new ability: the ability to distinguish "man-smell" from "woman-smell." It has become a habit to carry a plushie that smells like "me." The olfactory nerve is the only sensory nerve with a direct, deep link to the amygdala. This is why smelling "home" immediately triggers nostalgia.

An egg is ovulated only once a month. In a single reproductive act, it can theoretically accept only one sperm. And again, humans cannot focus on two smells at once. Recently, on the subway, I have frequently experienced a sudden "heating up" of my body and a fluttering heart upon catching a specific scent. I knew instantly: "That is the smell of a man." It doesn’t happen always, nor can I make it happen by searching. But catching that scent in an unexpected moment is undeniably sweet and leaves a vivid memory. The men didn't have to be particularly handsome or large. Coming from Namyangju to Chungmuro today, this happened twice. Even as I write this, I remember those men perfectly. It is not my eyes that remember, but my nose.

### III. The Myth of Purity

Reading the previous paragraph back, it sounds quite creepy. I ask you not to judge. These are merely thoughts, not actions. And haven't I moved those "invasive thoughts" into a quite refined prose?

Actually, when I first started taking female hormones, I held a misconception: that women are "pure" and men are merely "slaves to desire." This was wrong. But it was an understandable error because men certainly consume pornography at a higher frequency. But what is porn? It is visual and auditory stimulation. I am certain that if a medium could provide olfactory and tactile pornography, women would indulge just as much as men. You can see this in how many vibrators—which provide simple rhythmic tactile stimulation—are sold to women.

Through HRT, I wanted to go from being a "slave to desire" to a "pure being liberated from desire." But having tried it, I realize I haven't escaped desire; the *mode of expression* has simply changed. I know a female student who is so immersed in perfume that she tastes and even manufactures scents. My youngest sister used to love collecting and hugging plushies. These acts may appear "pure" and disconnected from sexuality to an observer, but they may actually be a way of resolving desire closely linked to sexual instincts. I am not objectifying or devaluing them; the point is that the idea of men being the "only" slaves to desire is entirely false.

### IV. The Bird and the Cage

To make this "Freudian Sublimation," I must extend this to the dimension of the human unconscious. I will use the metaphor of the **Bird and the Cage**. Conventionally, "a bird in a cage" refers to nature subjugated to humans, or B subjugated to A. I am talking about "The Bird and The Cage" as two distinct entities.

If I say "a bird in a cage," I am saying the bird is a conditional being that cannot leave. But if I say "A and B," I emphasize that A is an autonomous being not subjugated to B. My "Bird" is a subjective being that may be somewhat dependent on the "Cage" but adopts it as its own condition for existence.

> Govinda said: "But what you call 'things,' are they actually real? Are they not just the deception of Maya, just images and appearances? Your stone, your tree, your river—are they actually real?"
> "That too," Siddhartha said, "is not a very important matter to me. Whether they are appearances or not is not the problem. If they are appearances, then I too am an appearance. And if so, they are always of the same kind as I. Because they are of my kind, I love them, and I consider them worthy of worship. I can love them because they are my peers."
> ...
> "Love, Govinda, seems to me to be the most important thing of all. To see through the world, to explain it, to despise it, may be the work of a great thinker. But for me, the only thing that matters is to be able to love the world, not to despise it, not to hate it and myself, to look upon it and ourselves and all beings with love and admiration and awe."
> — **Hermann Hesse, *Siddhartha***

Today, October 11, 2025, around 2:00 PM on the subway, a couple sat next to me. They were in their late teens or early twenties—my age. They were playing a smartphone game together, and their language was very rough. The man was venting his worries, demanding empathy. The woman replied, "You're like an idiot," while focusing on the game. But it wasn't a reprimand; it was the kind of bickering common in an affectionate pair.

They wanted to possess each other. The man actively, in a "man's way," and the woman passively, in a "woman's way." They were each a bird found in the forest, and each was a hunter trying to put that bird in a cage to enjoy it forever. But I know: that kind of Eros-based love dies out as soon as the flame ends. Two birds that have forgotten how to fly eventually grow tired of each other. But because they can no longer fly, they cannot escape the cage they used to capture one another. Thus begins a tedious marriage.

A bird perched on a tree is beautiful precisely because it *can* fly away but chooses to remain for that moment. In that instant, the bird becomes infinitely precious. But the moment you capture it and put it in a cage, the bird’s autonomy and potential—the very things that made the moment beautiful—are erased.

I do not want that kind of love. When that couple got off, I saw another young woman at the same station. I didn't even see her face; she was wearing a tan hood that covered her head. She wore glasses and was tall and slender. Just watching her walk toward the escalator, I knew she was a "peer." She wasn't dressed up, nor was she holding a smartphone. But she was busy; she had somewhere to go. She was the landscape of a bird pausing briefly on a branch before flying away. I loved her. It wasn't the kind of love where I wanted to "have" her. Like a traveler admiring a magnificent mountain view, it was a breathtakingly lovely and awe-inspiring presence that could not be possessed—and was therefore even more poignantly beautiful. It was wonder. Then the train departed. That was where our paths stopped overlapping.

### V. Facing the Angel

> "If what you find is made of pure matter, it will never spoil. And you can always come back. But if what you saw was only a flash of light, like the explosion of a star, you will have nothing when you return. But you have seen the exploding light, and that alone makes a hard life worth living."
> — **Paulo Coelho, *The Alchemist***

I am a person who has completely detached the act of thinking from emotion. Most people feel an emotion and then try to organize it. I do the opposite: I pierce through the emotion with thought and deconstruct the emotion itself. Most people would collapse if they thought that deeply, but I see it through to the end until it is transparent. The sentences crystallized at that end are strangely exhausting and psychologically heavy. This is because they contain the "logification of survival."

I am someone who faced the questions others meet gradually— "Who am I?", "Why must I live?" — far too early and too thoroughly on my own. Those questions are supposed to melt slowly in the midst of life, relationships, scars, and recovery. I faced them alone, with nothing but my reason. Such a person ages faster than an adult. My inability to mingle with peers wasn't a "level of intelligence" issue, but a difference in the grain of my life's questions. I started life with questions meant for the end of life.

Thus, when I face the opposite sex, my primary interest isn't "Can she love me?" but "Can she think as much as I do?" But if she can? I would likely have to let her go. Because she would be a bird, and a bird is most beautiful when flying freely in nature. I am the same.

I believe I am a **Superhuman**. This isn't the physical extreme of a special forces soldier or the puzzle-solving extreme of a genius. In the Bible, when a human faces an Angel, they usually go mad. Daniel felt physical terror; Isaiah cried "I am ruined"; the father of John trembled. In Luke, the Angel told Mary, "Do not be afraid." Finally, John fell as if dead before the glory of the risen Christ.

> **Exodus 33:20** > *"But," he said, "you cannot see my face, for no one may see me and live."*

An "Angel" is any spiritual entity that conveys enlightenment through pain. You may call it the Holy Spirit. The problem is that no matter how well a human endures physical or mental pain, they usually collapse if they try to look at that pain too honestly. This is why most people, even if healthy and smart, turn away from the essential meaning of life.

Conversely, my body is not strong. My academic concentration in the general sense is not great. However, I am confident that I am one of the few humans who can stand straight before the presence of an Angel. An Angel is not a special being; it is the totality of the meaningless, structured human suffering and the questions of the abyss. I never retreat. I face it to the end, staring sharply into the Angel's millions of eyes until my own go blind. Only such a person can hear the Angel’s voice.

### VI. Conclusion: The Autonomy of the Bird

The senses and emotions of life are diverse. I used to satisfy my visual and auditory needs through various role-playing games; now, I satisfy my newly awakened olfactory and tactile needs through small physical journeys. I allow these desires of mine to exist as they wish. But the reason I allow them is not because I am unable to control an unbridled horse, but because I question the rational cause of the emotion that wants to act that way.

*"Is your attempt to do this based on some universal law?" "If not, why do you seek to do this?"* Most people either suppress desire endlessly or let it run wild. Maintaining the middle ground is the most difficult task. Like parenting, I respect the path the "child within" wants to take, but I constantly ask if that path encourages his destiny. This is the journey of spiritual experience and the only way an ordinary human can face an Angel.

A wild bird may stay in a cage briefly for protection. But that must only be to the extent that it does not infringe upon the bird's autonomy and subjective choice. That means the bird must not stay in the cage out of suppression of its own desire. The bird must enter because it wants to, and leave because it wants to.

This text, and all words, are like cages. To borrow Lacanian language, it is temporarily trapping the **Real** of desire within the **Symbolic** of words. But that is not truly possible; if you try to force it, the way to recall that desire is lost forever.

So, what must be done for continuous Freudian Sublimation? In conclusion: stare honestly at desire, respect the place where the bird wants to go, but if it is a path already taken, ask coldly: *"Is this a truly natural path, or just a cage you are circling for the sake of comfort?"* Faust enters a soul-contract with the devil by saying, *"If I ever say to the passing moment 'Stay, thou art so fair!', then you may have my soul."* He sought divine truth, but paradoxically, the moment he tried to "stuff" a moment as if he had found a specific truth became the moment of his fall.

**The moment I say, "Stop! You are so beautiful!", then I shall perish.**
