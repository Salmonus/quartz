### The Strategy for Physics & Chemistry

I am a "Physics + Chemistry" student—a combination that is practically an endangered species in the wild. While high school Physics deals with discrete changes in nature (Classical Mechanics), Chemistry focuses on continuous changes (Chemical Reactions).

Since the science electives have a strict 30-minute time limit, the patterns are highly standardized. The percentile gap between those who know the established shortcuts (Skills) and those who don't is massive. In Mathematics, where skills are less emphasized due to the vast spectrum of problem types, the "validity of approach" is most important. Because I spent Year 2 focusing on that validity and neglected Science "Skills," my internal and mock grades were low. I don't regret it. But from now on, I must acquire and refine them.

#### Physics (Instructor: Hyun Jung-hoon)

* **Materials:** *Stimpack 01, 02* (Weekly), *Treadmill* (Concept/Past Papers).
* **Approach:** *Stimpack* is a weekly workbook with about 60 problems. Unlike a massive volume like *Zaisstory*, you can't just clear your mind and solve hundreds of problems to find a "feel." You have to trust the instructor’s "recipe." I must set aside my stubborn insistence on the "validity of approach" and experience the "Hey, it works!" moment by following the instructor's methods exactly.
* **Study Stance:** If Year 2 was about swinging a sword to build raw muscle, Year 3 is about perfecting the stance to finally prepare for real war. In war, preserving your life is more important than taking the enemy general's head. To ensure a stable standard score, one must be flawless up to question 19 rather than just obsessing over question 20.

#### Chemistry (Instructor: Kang Jun-ho)

* **Materials:** *Hwajaljuice* (Weekly), *Triple Point* (Concept), *Fast Track* (Half-Mock).
* **Approach:** I am stronger in Chemistry than Physics, so there are few problems I can't solve in *Hwajaljuice*. This is where I must perform a "Time Attack" under real exam conditions. If I've already cleared a path, the next time I take it, it must be the shortest possible route.
* **Fast Track:** This is a "Half-Mock" aimed at solving pages 1–3 (excluding killer problems) in 15–20 minutes. Honestly, 20 minutes is impossible for me right now. I expect to spend an hour checking every single shortcut. That’s okay. I will get faster—I *must* get faster.

#### The Definition of "Intensity" (치열함)

I shouldn't overuse the word "Intensity," so I will define it clearly:

1. **Do not lean** your back against the chair.
2. **Lower your body** humbly until you almost touch the problem; do not sit arrogantly upright. Endure the back pain.
3. **Delete all emotion.** No smiling, no frowning. Become a machine.
4. **The line of sight** must be perpendicular (90°) to the problem. No obtuse angles.
5. **The rhythm of the pen** should not be *tap... tap-tap?* but a relentless *tap-tap-tap-tap-tap!!!!!* In Math, you must charge forward even if you aren't sure.
6. **Handwriting** should be as precise as print. A clean solution must emerge even during the *tap*8!!!!!* process.
7. **Think of nothing** except the problem in front of you. Don't even think about what to study next.
8. **Read with extreme caution.** Your initial accuracy is your exam score.

This is what intensity looks like. Studying for the high-stakes weekly tests (*Review Tests*) is different from hitting a sandbag; it's a boxing match. The moment a time limit is introduced, the problems start attacking you. Therefore, I must live with that tension every day.

### Closing: Defining "One Hour"

I will end with a definition I always set around this time of year.

**What is my "One Hour"?**

Defining this makes me want to use time meticulously and makes planning enjoyable. In 9th grade, my one hour was the time it took to perfectly memorize 6 Hanja characters, including their radicals and stroke order. Wasting an hour meant giving up 6 new characters (6/h)—something I couldn't tolerate.

**Now, I define my one hour as 600 calories burned (600 kcal/h).**
Two days ago, I ran 4.2km in 28 minutes on the treadmill (*Treadmill*!), and it showed exactly 300 calories burned. I’ve gained 10kg since middle school, so weight loss is essential. If I feel like I can't study, I’d rather go to the gym and burn 600 calories. If I am sitting and studying, it is because I consider it as intense as running at 12km/h on a treadmill—and more valuable than that exercise.
