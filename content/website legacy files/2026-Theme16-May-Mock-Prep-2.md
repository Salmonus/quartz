### I. The Cliff at the End of the Highway

At a time when I should be sprinting toward the May Mock Exam, I find myself repeatedly doing things a student shouldn't do.

Despite the worst physical condition, I didn't waste time during the first two days. However, no matter how much I exercise daily or study to the point of exhaustion, falling asleep on time remains elusive. I survive on the treadmill until I am drenched in sweat and gasping for air—not to get healthy, but simply to pass out. (The effect is minimal.) It’s not that my head is filled with too many thoughts; rather, the moment I lie down to sleep, a sharp, physical chilling pain spreads across my chest. Sleeping face-down at school is no different, so my condition is barely sustained by numerous catnaps during class hours. Yesterday, after being released from school for the weekend, I went straight to the pharmacy and bought an over-the-counter sleep inducer. It’s different from a prescription sedative and makes me feel extremely fatigued, like having taken cold medicine. The problem is that the drowsiness lasted until morning, causing me to oversleep and miss Hyun Jung-hoon’s lecture.

I’ve grown at an unbelievable pace and am now standing right before the "Grade 1 / Perfect Score" threshold, yet I couldn't understand why I felt so depressed. This symptom was particularly severe during the winter break before 11th grade. Back then, I thought it was because of a lack of skill or constant comparison with others, so I saved time to grind through Math. Now, while my skills aren't "perfect," I no longer feel intimidated by any problem; I’ve come a long way and the end is in sight. So why this feeling?

I know that mothers suffer from postpartum depression not because they don't love their children. My own mother, who raised three children, criticized such people with contempt. Having a rich sensitivity can, at times, lead to self-destruction. Therefore, during major life transitions—like raising a newborn or preparing for the CSAT—one needs to intentionally keep themselves in a "less sensitive" state. Depression doesn't happen because you didn't take care of your body or mind, nor is it simply because "wounds" have accumulated. Taking care of oneself to "treat" depression often means diving deeper into that swamp and rummaging through it. Instead, one should redirect their interest and become less sensitive. Someone with Irritable Bowel Syndrome should immerse themselves in a topic more important than their gut, and a student like me, when mentally exhausted, should actually increase their workload. I saw this as the only solution.

So, I planned my day down to the minute, recording everything on a timer to fill every second. I intended to give myself "no room for other thoughts." During classes I didn't attend, I made my own planner and watched online lectures instead. When exhausted or overwhelmed by thought, I simply took notes on Kim Seung-ri’s EBS analysis lectures. During Night Study, I solved only the most mentally challenging Physics II problems. On the evening of April 30th (left), I even had to pull an all-nighter and substitute it with two hours of sleep in the morning. I studied while drinking coffee and taking vitamins. I restored my "Math sense" by handwriting every missed problem from Kang Ki-won’s assignments. I tried everything to return to my original rhythm. I endured all day until the point where my body actually hurt and walking became difficult, sometimes even giving up exercise to lie in bed—expecting sleep to come easily. It didn't. My body wasn't sleepy; it was just dizzy and weak. Every nerve cell in my body seemed to be screaming at me to stop studying for the CSAT. Finally, on Thursday and Friday, I decided to boldly investigate the cause of this phenomenon.

I thought alone and shared my thoughts with **ChatGPT**, securing logical consistency through dialogue. Gradually, the cause of the problem began to emerge.

### II. Nevertheless, the Journey Must Continue

I thought entering a domestic medical school would provide a "faster path." At that time, I didn't know that the field of **Neuromorphic Computing** already existed, or that an agency called **IARPA**, led by the US federal government, had already conducted large-scale research in 2017—only to succeed in uncovering a tiny fraction of a mouse’s visual cortex. I only knew up to the "Attention" paper or Transformers as the **State-of-the-Art (SOTA)**, and I thought the only possibility surpassing them was a faint question mark posed by LeCun’s "World Model." AI has progressed from Symbolism to Connectionism, creating an excessive Hype Cycle, but I believed an "AI Winter" was imminent and a hardware-level redesign was essential. Those were my concerns in 10th grade, and as a young student, I also craved the stable growth in skill that the institutional system provides. So, I filled out the track-change form for the Natural Science Track, and indeed, my ability to deconstruct complex structures, my problem-solving skills, and my overall study attitude improved greatly.

But now, I’ve seen the reality. Having looked at the world as my stage until 10th grade, I now poignantly understand why Korea fails to advance. The Natural Track students I know possess outstanding computational abilities, but they are **Concrete Thinkers** who accept the structures of the world and the institution without question. Of course, regardless of other reasons, the pure effort of burning oneself for a goal deserves praise. In that sense, I respected and tried to emulate the friends I met after 11th grade. Initially, the intimidation of CSAT Question #30 kept me at my desk. Watching my peers handle those problems effortlessly made me look at myself critically: "Does a person as poor at studying as me (a person with such a small vector magnitude) even have the right to worry about 'Direction'?"

I truly don't know. If I enter SNU or a medical school, I will once again meet the kids who got in by listening to their teachers for the "Susi" (Early Admission) track, or the "Power Jeongsi" (Regular Admission) kids whose names were always at the top of the Sidae Injae or Gangnam Daesung billards. And I will be lonely again, just as I am now in the Natural Track. I thought that loneliness stemmed from a lack of skill. It wasn't. It was the **existential isolation** of a "child who seeks deconstruction for the progress of the world by harboring fundamental questions about structure" standing among "children who never question from within the structure."

I believed that being "objectively smart" was what mattered. Whatever work you do, being objectively smart is important. So, I told myself: "Let’s strive for the CSAT and increase my absolute value." That was two years ago. Now, I know that thinking power is not determined by a single axis.

> "Is Teacher A above Teacher B? Teacher B says it’s right, so who are you to say it’s not?"
> "Teacher A is from Kyungpook National Univ... Teacher B is from SNU Med..."
> — *Overheard conversation between students regarding a Korean exam retake due to a question error.*

I will never welcome people who believe an individual's entire capacity can be determined by such vertical structures—"Up" and "Down"—onto my team. Of course, there are objective, institutionalized scores. They are certainly used as decisive reference materials for Korean university admissions. However, there have always been people in history who could not be judged by such standards: **Faraday, Ramanujan, Wittgenstein, Steve Jobs.** They were geniuses that the system could not grasp—individuals who rediscovered the meaning of structure from outside the boundaries and shook its very foundations. Such people should be accepted through **horizontal depth**, not interpreted through **vertical height**. Korean universities can never discover these people. The hierarchy of the elite dominance structure is too high and far away to find those who determine fate. Because fate resides in the lives of the 99%, not the 1%, and those who walk with them must be horizontally open.

When I say this, I expect the response: "You're just making excuses because you failed to win within the system." In fact, that is why I, an extreme extrovert, don't make friends and remain silent. So, I tried my best for the CSAT. They say if you don't like someone, it's because their flaws actually exist within you. They say what is not truly inside you cannot shake you. In short, I was "pierced." But I am not pierced anymore. I know what it means to do well on the CSAT. I know exactly what the journey toward a Grade 1 or a 100th percentile is. I know what is gained from others and what is lost from oneself in achieving that. I thought a domestic medical school would provide a "faster path." **That highway ended at a cliff.**

### III. Nevertheless, the Journey Must Continue

Just because I think this way doesn't mean I will drop my CSAT studies immediately. On the contrary, I will study even harder. There are two possibilities:

1. Abandoning the SNU/Medical school goal and applying to US universities.
2. Maintaining the original goal.

I’ve scheduled a consultation with Teacher Jo Kyung-ho next week. However, I know I must give my all to the CSAT. In case of option 1, **GPA becomes extremely important.** I have successfully ruined my GPA in the Natural Track, and the only way to explain a mangled GPA in the Korean curriculum is through the official KICE exams (June/September mocks and the CSAT). I must be able to claim: "My HAFS GPA is not good, but I am not a failure of the Korean educational system because I am in the **top 1% nationwide**." A 1% percentile is the cutoff for SNU’s Liberal Studies or most regional medical schools. That is the only way for US universities to understand my "lost two years." In case of option 2, it is simply important. Ultimately, the journey must continue.

However, if I choose option 1, my 2025 might become a bit tedious. I’ll explain the GPA with mock and CSAT scores, apply to test-optional colleges if I skip the SAT, and even if I don't take APs, variables like recommendation letters and essays remain. I don't write the letters, but I must write the essay. Since I’ve only studied based on CSAT English standards for two years, I will likely be "terrible" at English by international applicant standards. I’ll look into that next week.

Despite all these thoughts, I might end up preparing for Korean admissions again. Or, I might have to start from scratch because I can't get my parents' empathy despite the situation being okay. Or, things might flip, and I might turn my gaze across the sea again.

In 7th grade, I tried to read *Sapiens* because my English academy made me. I didn't understand everything, but some parts stayed in my heart forever, determining all my future behavioral guidelines:

> "The very clarity of this deal allows society to define common norms and values that regulate human behavior. Spiritual journeys are nothing like that. They usually take people in mysterious ways toward unknown destinations. The quest usually begins with some big question, such as who am I? What is the meaning of life? What is good? Whereas many people just accept the ready-made answers provided by the powers that be, spiritual seekers are not so easily satisfied. They are determined to follow the big question wherever it leads, and not just to places you know well or wish to visit. Thus for most people, academic studies are a deal rather than a spiritual journey, because they take us to a predetermined goal approved by our elders, governments and banks."

My Academic Journey is not following a **predetermined goal** set by society; it is the work of expanding that boundary, so I may not arrive at the **places I wish to visit**. Instead of the **ready-made answers** of current neural networks, I dreamed of structural improvements toward the fundamental question of "How to speak with a computer." Naturally, that can lead me to unknown places in mysterious ways. I started this knowing all of that.

> **Per varios casus, per tot discrimina rerum tendimus in Latium.** > *(Through various accidents, through so many perils of fortune, we make our way to Latium.)* > — *Aeneid Book I, line 204*

If human meaning can be reduced to structure, almost all previous attempts would lose their meaning. As Smith said in *The Matrix*.

However, a pure human choice can cause a crack in the system. That is why humans are valuable. It’s not because they are good at math or do well on the CSAT. The reason I fight is because **I chose to.** In that sense, the structures I design someday will also possess vitality.
