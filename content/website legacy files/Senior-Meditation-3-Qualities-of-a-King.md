# Senior Meditation (3): The Qualities of a King

**By:** Kim Yeon-woo

**Platform:** Brunch

**Note:** *This series focuses on lighter content not covered in "Senior Story."*

---

> "Reason thinks it would be able to fly much farther in free space, but in fact, it cannot fly at all without the resistance of air."
> — **Immanuel Kant, Preface to the *Critique of Pure Reason***

Contrary to popular belief, the most cited scholar in the world is not Newton or Einstein, but **Karl Marx**. While Newton’s papers built laboratories, Marx’s ideas built nations. People respect Newton, but they stand in awe of Marx. Deriving a formula from the natural world is a great achievement, but explaining *why* that formula is meaningful is an act of creating something from nothing. It is more than an achievement; it requires a moment of silence, not just applause.

Every person has something they "want to become." We spend our time imagining what "ought to be," only to live in regret of what we "could have done." We think a genie in a lamp can do anything, yet the genie is the most bound by its own power. Why is that?

It is because that is the **fate of a god**. A god is like the game board itself. From a pawn's perspective, becoming a more valuable piece or possessing the power to teleport seems like the key to victory—but the very desire to move forward is the privilege of a piece. Pieces move; the game board cannot. Similarly, the **King** moves only in a limited capacity. This is because the King defines the very purpose of chess. While the Queen is the most valuable piece in terms of utility, the King is the purpose, not the means. The King is the premise upon which chess exists.

Society constantly asks: *"What kind of piece are you?"* Most are pawns. A few are rooks, knights, or bishops. Very few reach the position of the queen. This means that the spots for "valuable pieces"—those that can move in any direction they desire—are limited. Most lives are spent moving one square at a time, blocked and unable to proceed. Individuals with incredible abilities produce amazing results, but they ultimately realize only instrumental values, never the end goal.

The goals of "wanting to make a lot of money" or "reaching a certain position in a field" are fine. In today's liberal society, formal egalitarianism justifies meritocracy in alliance with capital. These are practical goals for securing resources. However, some people live on a different internal frequency. They are deaf to stories about who earned how much or who became what, but they react instantly to questions like: *"Why must we live?", "Who set the rules of this world?",* or *"Are there subjects of moral privilege other than humans?"* It’s not a matter of good or bad; it is a difference in **sensitivity**. They were simply born more sensitive.

In the coordinate system of the current regime, such people may appear as "eccentrics," "lazy," "loners," or "idealists." It can't be helped. But among the few who appear lazy, there is one—perhaps not even one in an entire generation—who is the **Real Deal**. While others overtake pieces to race to the edge of the board, this being stands still, observing everyone. Are they a lazy pawn? Or are they the King? In the "Opening" of the game, no one knows. Everyone moves cautiously. But as the "Endgame" approaches, it becomes clear around whom all the pieces were moving. That "eccentric sluggard" remained in the first row not because they were lazy, but because they were the King. It wasn't about being tired; it was because **they were the center that explained everyone else's intentions, and thus, they could not move.**

**"The Qualities of a King"** is a concept from the manga *One Piece*. It refers to the conviction to lead people voluntarily, an unyielding will toward freedom, strong mental fortitude, and innate charisma. This is expressed as *Haoshoku Haki* (Conqueror's Haki), signifying the instinctive qualification of a leader who can shake the world. These four traits are not independent competencies but a consistent self-expression based on a single internal standard: **sensitivity**.

Some people are like radio telescopes—they can't hear the car horn next to them, but they detect the sound of planets moving. To the world, they seem dull, but they carry a massive problem that no one else has seen. This is an "ability above abilities"—it can overturn the board but cannot be explained by existing rules. Thus, simply possessing it requires immense mental strength. Yet, it guarantees true freedom. The "Pirate King" is defined as the freest being on the sea. It is not someone with the power to teleport, but someone who, despite physical limitations, can **decide for themselves where they are headed.** That is true freedom. One who possesses the unyielding will to endure existential solitude is the one with the qualities of a king.

Still, one cannot find the treasure alone. One must communicate and lead. Not in the style of Napoleon, but in the sense that the King defines the goal (like in chess), making even those with superior abilities (the Queen, the Knight) compelled to join.

A person with the qualities of a king **must possess the ability to communicate.** This is because they aren't the ones who will be doing the heavy moving. The best archer can rarely be the best at swordsmanship, spearmanship, and horsemanship simultaneously. Communication is not just about being eloquent. It is the ability to perceive the game board one belongs to and sense the necessity of the "Me" that faces it. The King moves only one square in any direction, but he provides the criteria for decision-making for pieces with much higher destructive power. Being a valuable piece is the talent of an excellent citizen, but **questioning why that power must exist** is the quality of a king.

Those who discover a new standard of ethics that will prevail globally will burn with the greed to draw, compose music, develop technology, and write papers based on those visionary principles. However, the reason most ideologies are first presented through **writing** is that it is the mirror through which thought passes most transparently. Every painting, song, or technology is first "thought" through language. Therefore, if a King must move "just one square," it should be the act of presenting his thoughts through writing. Writing is the only **meta-technology**—the technology capable of "self-editing thought." One should attempt other technologies if possible, but one must also know how to trust and delegate to others. The King protects; the Queen attacks. The "value" of the pieces may differ, but they are equal in that they are all indispensable components of the game. In this sense, the King and the Soldier are equal—and that is true humility. It is not about mindlessly lowering or raising oneself, but acknowledging that "everyone is a member of the world."

Throughout my life, I have often heard that I am "stubborn" or "too ambitious." I admit it. I am a young man with great ambition. But that ambition is not a vow to "climb to the top of society's ladder" or "live a successful life since I was born." Rather, it is closer to the question: **"Who built society's ladder?"** and **"What does it mean to live well?"** That is a much greater greed than the former. But for the right person, it is the only path. I am not inflating my ego to say "I will be the King." Rather, I am saying: *"I am this kind of person. A King must perform this role. Therefore, if one person must be the King, it is this specific type of person."*

In terms of my life, we are still only in the **"Opening."** It is a period of basic development, making moves just to pass the turn without fully understanding who I am or who the opponent is. In such a situation, harboring a massive awareness of the problem and questioning the rules of the game is, in a way, incredibly painful. *Can I solve it? Can I pull it off?* No single piece, not even the Queen, can dominate the game alone, yet I spend all day seized by the anxiety that I must do it all. This sometimes prevents me from doing even the things I am supposed to do. What should I do?

**I must believe.**

> "A revolution needs the right person in the right place at the right time."

This quote reflects Yuval Harari’s insight into the uncertainty and randomness of history. He cites the moment in 1989 when Nicolae Ceaușescu heard the crowd jeer for the first time during a speech. The television broadcast cut off, Ceaușescu panicked, and things spiraled out of control. It was the decisive moment when years of suppressed anger exploded because someone had the courage to take the first action. Harari argues that revolutions or historical shifts aren't always caused by "structural factors" or "grand ideologies," but sometimes because a specific person happened to be in that spot at that moment. History is often the product of dramatic coincidence.

If my thinking is correct, at the necessary moment, the necessary person will be in the necessary place. The reformer **Martin Luther** would not have succeeded in his revolution without the Elector of Saxony, who staged a fake kidnapping to hide him in a castle after he was excommunicated. Luther did not invent the printing press. But when Luther was active, Gutenberg’s press was already there. That was, literally, a **"God’s Move"** (*Shin-ui-hansu*). Luther moved "just one square" to write the 95 Theses, but it was the role of the other pieces to distribute them across Europe. A King does not fight on the front lines. He is not a subject for prisoner exchange. But it is the King who starts the war and declares it. One with the qualities of a king does not need to do everything. They simply need to **move one square**, believing that the revolution will succeed.

Right now, by any standard, that **"one square"** is the CSAT (*Suneung*). The six months I spent organizing creative activities, learning how to promote myself, and training my critical thinking in seminars sent me to HAFS. While not every student there was authentic, the friends who became the "pillars" of my high school life were people I met only because I was a HAFS student.

It is the same now. If I spend the remaining five months categorizing types, learning how to solve problems within the time limit, and identifying exam elements while fully enjoying the process, I will be able to enter the right **Institution**. Not every top scorer looks at the world the way I do, but the people who will form the foundation of my starting line in society will be there because I am in that institution.

Whenever I feel distanced from the CSAT, I will think of the precious connections from my high school years. And I will imagine how much richer my university life will be if I meet just five more people like them. For a revolution to succeed, the right person just needs to be in the right place at the right time.

I will make that move. That one square.
