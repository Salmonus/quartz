### II. Feedback: What Went Wrong?

**1. Science Electives (Gwatam): Absolute Lack of Practice**
Of course, one could argue that my energy was depleted since these are the last subjects taken. However, that fact remains the same on the actual CSAT day. Even if I were to look at the paper again tomorrow morning with a clear mind, I don't think I could do much more. Effectively, I only started studying Science this past winter break. I am at the stage where I’ve just finished confirming concepts and am gradually becoming able to solve the most typical, basic problems. I never evaluated myself as being "good" at Science yet.

Once you reach a level where you can run through a mock exam without much burden, your rate of improvement accelerates. For Science in particular, the ability to manage time by problem type is essential. Solving N-material by unit was not enough to train my "exam operation" skills. Only by consistently experiencing novel problems that break existing frameworks—through weekly workbooks and daily 30-minute timed practice—can I achieve my best score even in the worst condition. I will set aside the March score for a moment and find meaning in the fact that I’ve reached a stage where I *can* practice this way. I should take confidence in having at least established a foothold where I can pursue improvement alongside other subjects, rather than spending almost the entire week only on Science under the pretext of "stabilization."

**2. Korean: Lack of Stamina and Routine**
I simply have nothing to say. I barely practiced, and the score reflects exactly what I expected. Solving one *Batang* mock exam during the break was the extent of my preparation.

Another variable in Korean is the physical toll it takes. There is a fundamental difference between someone who is habituated to running through a Korean exam paper every morning and someone who is not. I realized that a senior must establish a morning Korean routine not just to "study Korean," but to simulate the brutal physical exhaustion they will face at the start of the day. I’ve learned that the Math and Science problems you solve *after* your mind has become dizzy are your true skills. Even if I’m sleepy, I shouldn't think, "I'll sleep a bit and study with a clear mind." Instead, I must hold onto my consciousness, imagining how I would react if that exam paper were in front of me at that very moment. My skills will improve quickly if I do this.

**3. Mathematics: Misconceptions and Accuracy**
I missed questions **20, 21, 27, 29, and 30**. The only one I didn't solve was 29. Unlike question 21, where I clearly know my flawed logic, questions 20, 27, and 30 resulted in different answers despite me having a firm solution of my own. This indicates the presence of **misconceptions**. Misconceptions can only be corrected one by one as I increase the volume of problem-solving. This can be resolved simply by staying on top of *Ass* (Assignments) and doing well on Review Tests. I will continue to do this steadily throughout my daily routine.

### III. The CSAT Starts Now

Literally, CSAT study starts now.

Learning concepts as mere knowledge or predicting "what the CSAT will be like" while saying things anyone could say is not CSAT study. CSAT study is a journey of persuading yourself at your lowest moments to find a reason to overcome—or even squeezing out a reason when none exists. The internal strength and solidity of life gained while enduring the middle of this competition are incomparable to those who haven't experienced it. Validating cases that no one else sees, grasping structures, and finding ways to move ahead is great learning, but it is also a precious experience in itself. As I said in **Theme 2**, the journey is the reward.

When we are young, we exist in an environment where whatever we do is treated as something great by adults. People raised in such a loving environment continue to take pride in their work as adults, confident that they are doing something special that not just anyone can do.

However, when you lose in a significant competition, you can feel incredibly small, and the values you believe in can feel small along with you. It feels like everyone is pointing fingers at you, and an impulse arises to blame all external factors—talent, environment, or wealth. In such a moment, the will of a pure person is tested, and the world they believe in begins to reorganize itself around that defeat. If that person truly has the ability to put those values into practice, they will recall the moments of "uncertain victory" and be grateful for the opportunity to re-examine their shortcomings. Such a moment cannot be called a "defeat," and such a person is not a "loser." How I choose to utilize my March Mock score from this point on is entirely up to me.

Realizing that something was seriously wrong and switching from the International Track to the Natural Track to take the CSAT was the right choice for growth. It is self-evident that if I don't do well in what I'm doing now, there will be no next step. I must strive constantly and do what I can and what I must.

> *"It is not my sword that pierces you, but your past."* > — ***The Count of Monte Cristo***

My current pain is not because of the March Mock or the CSAT, but because of my past, in which I lived sloppily. If I fail the CSAT, the obstacle to my life won't be the exam itself, but likely my life attitude—and unless I correct that, there will be no other way. I am grateful that I have been able to find a breakthrough at a relatively young age.
