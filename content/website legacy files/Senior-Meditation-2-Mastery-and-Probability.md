# Senior Meditation (2): The End of Mastery is a Game of Probability

**By:** Kim Yeon-woo

**Platform:** Brunch

**TL;DR:** Mastery is ultimately about managing probabilities.

---

### I. The Statistical Nature of Excellence

The end of mastery is a game of probability.
Whether it is sports, philosophy, mathematics, aerospace engineering, artificial intelligence, specialized high school admissions, or college entrance exams (Early or Regular)—everything eventually becomes a battle of percentages.

* When everyone can perform the basic movements to finish a race, how do you execute high-difficulty movements consecutively to shave off time or maximize points?
* When everyone has mastered existing works and reached the edge of "speakable" certainty, what percentage of "unspeakable" uncertainty will you secure?
* In Game Theory, can one reach a state of "Perfect Equilibrium" rather than just a compromise of a Nash Equilibrium?
* Once you have the minimum configuration to take off with lift, how should you structure it to minimize the probability of an accident?
* Which loss function should you use to achieve maximum accuracy with minimum epochs, avoiding overfitting and modeling reality as accurately as possible?
* How many paragraphs and what materials should compose a self-introduction letter to secure maximum points in an interview within the shortest time?
* Which types of problems and how much learning are required to secure a Grade 1 GPA within 50 minutes with the least effort?
* When anyone can solve the "easy 4-point" problems, what exam elements and patterns must be internalized to reach the 100th percentile?

---

### II. Floor, Ceiling, and the 0.1% Increments

The end of mastery is a game of probability. When everyone has crossed the "floor," who will determine the "ceiling"?

Just as all heat cannot be converted into work, not all work is converted into utility. A game with a 100% probability—where you either always win or always lose—is a useless game to even think about.

Even the "bottom 100%" dream of being the "top 100%." Both start at 0%.

* The **bottom 100%** turns away from the reality of 0% and only dreams of a 100% outcome.
* The **top 100%** acknowledges the reality of 0% first and collects even 0.1% every single day.

Solving one workbook does not make you a 100th percentile student. In the first place, a "fixed 100 score" does not exist. If it does, it belongs only to the person who wrote the exam. However, every time you solve a single problem, the probability goes up. Internalizing **"already tested"** elements sets your floor; grasping **"untested"** elements sets your ceiling.

> If you raise your probability by just 1% a day, in 100 days, you reach a 100% percentile.

A day where you feel full of confidence that you’ll do well isn’t necessarily a day of good study. Rather, a day where you feel self-reproach, asking *"Why am I so bad at this?"* is a day well-spent. It means you have confirmed that many "untested elements" exist. It means your probability has increased by that much.

---

### III. The Ticket to the Game

However, **emotions are not a probability.** **Health is not a probability.** Rather, emotions (mental health) and physical health are the "tickets"—the two axes that make the "probability game" possible in the first place.

* Connect with people fighting the same battle.
* Manage your emotions with appropriate music.
* Eat delicious food and get enough sleep.

Finally, be grateful that you are still in this game. There are those who didn't even have an entrance ticket, and those who were forced to exit.
