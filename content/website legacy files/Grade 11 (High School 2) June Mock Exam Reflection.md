### Analysis of the Results

Regardless of my physical condition on the day of the exam, the subjects I have consistently invested time in provide clear answers and yield results. For **Korean**, if I don't keep refining my skills, I end up getting questions wrong even when I feel like I know the material.

For **Mathematics**, I must approach it not with the intent of "mathematical analysis," but with the singular goal of "finding the answer." However, since they provide 100 minutes, I manage to solve everything. Using *reductio ad absurdum* (proof by contradiction) through the given options is a good secondary tactic, operating under the assumption that a correct answer must exist. If solved correctly, it is impossible to run out of time.

The reason I missed two questions in **Chemistry** was that I lacked time—I solved stoichiometric equations using unknown variables rather than relative quantities. Furthermore, I hadn't solved enough problems in the "Moles and Particles" unit before the exam. When I practiced a Chemistry paper at 2 AM the night before, even while making mistakes and taking the long way around due to sleepiness, I still had 10 minutes left on the final question. I believe the only way to master a subject is to spend time with it and think deeply until you become "familiar" with it. The proof is that I can get a Grade 1 (Top Tier) in **English** even when I'm half-asleep.

### The "Study-Athlete" Mindset

To be honest, while solving the Math problems today, my mind was wandering. A thousand different thoughts crossed my mind, my stomach ached, and every imaginable mental distraction attacked me. Yet, I still calculated the exponents/logarithms and found the trigonometric ratios.

I realized that the CSAT (Suneung) is "just" because it allows ample time; one must set their "baseline" to a perfect score even in poor condition so that, at worst, they only miss one or two questions. Therefore, a state of mind plagued by intrusive thoughts is actually the best environment to prepare for the CSAT. The experience of resisting those thoughts to solve even one more problem will be of immense help on the actual exam day.

Even when the brain isn't functioning at its peak, the subconscious must know the way, and the hands must remember the process. Relying solely on conscious memory is equivalent to leaving your score to luck. This is why I failed Chemistry but did well in English and Math. Ultimately, it can be compared to building muscle: you must exercise consistently even when you don't feel well. It’s not about "thinking deeply" to solve every problem; there are problems where the eyes and hands analyze the data and the answer simply appears, and then there are problems where you must move your eyes more sharply and your hands more busily with the intent to "find" the answer.

I've realized that I must approach this with the mindset of a **"Study-Athlete,"** much like how professional athletes undergo physical training. This does not mean I stop thinking.

### Status and Values

I value "values" deeply. I believe that "status"—which is merely a tool—loses all direction if it is not persuaded by those values. However, today I failed to protect what I loved and wished to defend. This happened because I lacked the "status" I had previously overlooked in favor of my values, precisely when I needed it most. Thus, there are cases where the acquisition of status must come first. Unless I want to experience this emptiness and helplessness again, I have decided that I must also be intent on acquiring status. But I must not be consumed by it. That is why it is difficult.

### The Weight of Solitude

When I reflect like this, everything becomes abstract. Sometimes I wonder if it would be more helpful to use this time to solve one more concrete problem. However, when I put too much pressure on my brain, "inexperienced logic" tends to dominate my inner self, which can stall my entire day and ruin my studies.

While I could spend time with friends, there are too many variables there. For the sake of stability, I conclude that controlling myself is the best path. The only evidence that justifies this isolation is found at the end of the day—either when I have pushed myself to the limit using the right methods without a shred of doubt, or when I have found a small spark of enlightenment or hope after enduring alongside desperate thoughts.

Sometimes, I want to live life as a joke. I want to abandon these attempts to be different or to live based on values and just live for my own status like everyone else. But I would rather die than do that. The world doesn't change just because one more mediocre intellectual or wealthy person is added to it. To live a life where breathing actually feels meaningful, I must carry these desperate thoughts in my heavy head and push myself to the brink of death. Only then, I feel, will my next mock exam scores improve.
