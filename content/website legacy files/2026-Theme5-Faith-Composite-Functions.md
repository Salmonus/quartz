### I. Sleep Issues During the Holidays

The gym was closed during the holidays, so I couldn't exercise at all. I could have worked out outside, but the continuous snow made the roads icy and difficult to run on. While there are parks and open spaces around Daechi-dong, I lacked the physical strength and will to engage in high-intensity exercise while everyone's eyes were on me.

For me, skipping exercise is equivalent to skipping sleep. There hasn't been a single day during this holiday that I've slept properly.

It’s not just a matter of mood. Like a physical injury or illness, a tangible, chilling pain spreads from the center of my chest. It is a psychological issue, but it doesn't seem like something that can be solved through psychiatric counseling. It is a problem of the core beliefs and life attitudes I have built since the early stages of my ego formation.

*I cannot solve this. There is no one to help me...* I cross out names of family and acquaintances one by one. Those who cannot help me are enemies.

The next morning, I wake up with the (negative) lingering scent of the previous night, my eyes swollen and difficult to open. Naturally, I am drowsy and lethargic, my mind filled only with thoughts that hinder my studies. Without stacking *Guron-san* and energy drinks on my desk, I can barely even write out a planner.

This isn't something I can just ignore; it’s been like this, to varying degrees, since middle school. The reason I am finally finding the will to resolve this now, as a high school senior, is because my workload has increased to the point where I cannot complete my tasks without reducing emotional consumption. I must reduce it. Now that I am nearly an adult, that is my responsibility.

### II. The Solution to Sleep Issues

* **Previous Approach:** It can't be helped. There are many problems, but I must immerse myself in study and forget them all. Don't think about things that are meaningless to think about. It is a blessing to be able to escape despair through study. It is happiness to be able to do my job from my position. If it gets too hard, I'll just solve *this* and die.
* **Problem with Previous Approach:** I cannot regulate my emotions during times when I am not studying or when I am trying to fall asleep.

**==> Below are the thoughts I had during my spare time over the past week:**

One must not fear people. Others are not enemies; they are colleagues who can help one another. The reason one fears people is likely because of an experience of loss, or the perception of loss. If I think I've lost something, it's not because someone actually took it from me, but because my expectations were too high. People are not gods. Everyone else shares my limitations. One should not have high expectations of others from the start. Don't expect others to cling to you, and conversely, don't think you can cling to them. Show disciplined behavior.

Then, who can help me?

People are not gods, but God is God. I decided to believe in God so that I could sleep.

Praying to God is not about whining like a child to their mother about being tired or wanting something. It is about being able to do the work I do, believing in its value, and being grateful to the Value itself that led to the result. That prayer does not reach God; rather, I, the one praying, hear it. And then, as if receiving help from an absolute being, I will be able to quietly achieve many things on the path I pursue, in a way that looks like a miracle to others. I feel this is what spiritual value is.

Regardless of denomination or sect, all gods teach certain values. Even if the process of explaining those values seems less persuasive from the perspective of modern science, there is meaning in the fact that they have created **Ready-Made Answers** on how humans should act to make the world a better place when assuming an Absolute Being. I could even create my own God—everyone must do this eventually. But for now, let’s start by accepting. Before building my own custom PC, I'll buy a pre-built one out of desperation.

> **Multum enim valet deprecatio iusti operans.** > *(The prayer of a righteous person is powerful and effective.)* > — *Epistula Iacobi (James), 5:16*

I cannot just pass by Latin that I don't know. From now on, I should review Latin and Hanja like this.

* **enim (conj):** For, truly, in fact.
* **valet (valeo, valere):** To be strong, to have value, to be effective.
* **deprecatio, -onis:** Entreaty, plea, prayer.
* **iusti (iustus, -a, -um):** Righteous, just.
* **operans (operor, operari):** To work, to act, to have an effect.

**==> "The working prayer of a righteous person is very effective."**

I don't know if I am "righteous," but a righteous person's prayer will surely bring clear results for the reasons mentioned above. Being righteous means acting based on righteous values. When those righteous values are carved onto a stone tablet and commanded to be followed, that is a religion. Today, the world has advanced, so I can write them on a blog instead of a stone tablet.

Until I possess internal solidity, I have decided to serve Christ. Not for any other reason, but because He allows me to review my Latin. Confucianism allows me to review Hanja, but it provides no absolute being to lean on, so it doesn't help me sleep. Buddhism requires learning Sanskrit, and similarly, lacks an object of reliance.

I don't know if this will solve my sleep issues. What matters is that I must keep trying. As a student, I cannot just stand by when my emotions are out of control. It is better to pray than to spend the dawn in misery. If it reduces my pain by even 1%, it is more than worth it.

Solving this way made me uncertain if I could get the 2026 composite function problem right. > **"Understand the problem perfectly, then look at the board."** > — *Kang Ki-won*

Understanding a problem perfectly means I never have to return to the problem statement while solving it. In the case of 2023 #30, one shouldn't just memorize a specific scenario and solution that fits only that problem. Instead, one must practice fully extracting information from condition (A), condition (B), and function  separately. Only then can one smoothly solve high-difficulty inference problems with many variables, such as finding the number of all ordered pairs when  and  are integers less than or equal to 25.

Similar logic applies to 2025 #30, where functions with periodicity and symmetry are composed. You must verify every case arising from each condition to avoid using proof by contradiction (*reductio ad absurdum*). Also, you won't be intimidated by problems asking for the sum of all possible cases compared to problems where the function shape is fixed. Ultimately, pursuing a 100 on CSAT Math means developing the eye to analyze every condition individually. I intend to study all subjects that way.

God already knows everything about a problem before seeing it. But a human realizing God's will only knows the final destination of values; the process must be endured by the self. An excellent human does not delude themselves into thinking they know a shortcut. They consider every existing case and do not make arbitrary guesses or assumptions. They know that this is the fastest shortcut. I must practice obsessively like that. That is how it differs from GPA prep.

Only when you understand the problem perfectly and can see the "blank space" will your resolution speed increase.
