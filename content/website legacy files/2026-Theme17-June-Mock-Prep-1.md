### I. True but Unprovable Propositions (Ground)

One can easily identify expressions that violate formal rules without even debating their truth or falsehood. In the **MU Puzzle**, a string like `MMII` is a prime example. Since there is no rule to add an 'M' to the given axiom `MI`, even an elementary school student can see it violates the formal conditions of a "Theorem" within that system.

[Image: Expansion of the MU Puzzle—The branching paths of MI  MII  MIIII...]
*(The Trap: Can you determine if a string will appear without listing every possibility?)*

What about a string like `MIIII`? Can it be derived via the rules? The answer is "Yes." However, that "Yes" is only confirmed by actually adding 'I's to `MI` until it appears; there is no way to verify it as a theorem without the actual derivation process. Even without bringing up **Turing’s Halting Problem**, the old **Bootstrap** metaphor remains valid. An **Unobservant Machine**—a rule-based agent like a Jacquard loom—cannot know if something is a theorem. They can read the punch cards but cannot edit them; they can only confirm forward-moving conclusions.

Mathematics was proven to be **Incomplete** by a 24-year-old Gödel, but this only reaffirms the value of **Observant Thinkers** who can escape the **Strange Loop** and actually observe the situation. The logs for the raft of knowledge—the "True and Provable Propositions" that keep us afloat in the sea of the Natural System while expanding our knowledge—are no longer found in South Korea. Why? To understand this, I will move away from the number-theoretic treatment of "True but Unprovable Propositions (Ground)" and use a literary **Analogy**. I am just an ordinary high school student, and this blog is not an academic paper, so I ask for the understanding of real mathematicians.

From now on, I will use "Provable" to mean: **"It has approached me as something meaningful in some way."** Something meaningful to me is something I can accept by expanding the system I use to interpret the world without damaging its internal framework. Here, "Me" refers to the "only being that is both an *undefined term* and an *axiom*," as stated in my [GEB post three days ago](https://www.google.com/search?q=2025-05-07-GEB-Suneung-Body.md). That argument assumed the Chinese Room, yet I attacked the Chinese Room later in that same post. I hope you can overlook that contradiction as well.

The fact that not everything in the world can be meaningful can be expressed as **"The world is perspectival."** This is the core thesis of phenomenology and is crucial in AI development, as explained via the "Lion and Insect" analogy. Simply put, there are things you want to do and things you don't. Even if a task you dislike isn't a taboo or a crime (which would be a "non-theorem" in this analogy), it is often a "theorem" you must perform without knowing *why* it is meaningful to you.

For example: as an elementary schooler, I had zero interest in fashion. If my mother took me to a department store, the children's clothing section was a **"True but Unprovable Proposition"** to me. I knew it was meaningful to *someone*, but it didn't register as meaningful to *me* in any way. Conversely, the food court in the basement was a **"True and Provable Proposition."** After walking all day, food right in front of me had immense meaning. Yet, not buying clothes at all is a problem. From the perspective of an adult mother (an external observer), buying clothes is a proposition that is "obviously true."

1. Kim Yeon-woo is an elementary schooler. Therefore, he must buy children's clothes.
2. Kim Yeon-woo (Inside the system/TNT) cannot prove if this is true or false.
3. The Mother (Outside the system/TNT) thinks this is obviously true.

Why is the message always "Listen to your mother"? In the world of the CSAT, asking about "Meaning" is a taboo. It demands that teenagers—at an age where the ability to distinguish the survival-relevant **Figure** from the irrelevant **Ground** is not yet mature—accept propositions proven true in an external axiomatic system. Korean teenagers who accept this well, thinking "I have to earn money," grimly open Korean workbooks in the morning, do Math all day, finish Science in the evening, and go to sleep. This is an overwhelmingly advantageous mindset within the system. The CSAT is like an endlessly proliferating **MU Puzzle**. No one cares if you prove that `MU` can't be found. Instead, they test how fast you can derive strings like `MIU`, `MUMUM`, or `MIIII`—things that are theorems but not the essence of the puzzle. It is an **Unobservant Machine production algorithm.**

### II. Admitting the "Ground" to Become an "Observant Thinker"

This week, a younger peer who graduated with me from the 13th class of the **KAIST IP Gifted Education Center** contacted me to join **SAGE** (Students for the Advancement of Global Entrepreneurship). The other members were mostly from **Korea Science Academy (KSA)**. Their idea was in the final stages; the 3D modeling of the prototype was already finished by the "physics and algorithm freaks" of KSA. Logically, there seemed to be little I could contribute technically. So, I asked what my expected role in the team would be.

I don't recall exactly what the "feedback task" was, but I remembered my role back at the Education Center. My team usually won the minor awards at camp. I understood why these kids—essentially the national team of Korean problem-solving—came to me, a student in the mountains of Yongin, despite the communication hurdles. They were the masters of problem-solving, but **I was the being who questioned why the problem was a problem in the first place.** While they categorized hundreds of possible patterns in the MU Puzzle, I was the one who could shout: **"Finding MU is impossible!"**

It was the winter of 2023, just before my high school entrance. Kids from the 12th and 13th classes gathered at the LG Research Center in Daejeon. It was a business modeling workshop—defining a problem on a **Lean Canvas** and identifying a target consumer base. Since it was a nationwide group, these kids eventually entered Science high schools or top private schools. They were high achievers under immense family expectations. This was not a typical middle school group project. We debated pitching strategies all night and overhauled ideas dozens of times, yet we still faced sharp criticism from KAIST professors and CEOs. I was an ambitious middle schooler then, meticulously defining the problem and revenue model.

But here lies the trap: **The more "elite" one is, the easier it is for their internal value system of "Meaning" to become too wide.** Because they know so much, anything they claim is likely a **Figure** they believe is true, rather than **Ground**. They forget the design purpose of the Lean Canvas and over-complicate the idea before even defining the problem, unable to let go of the "beautifully bloated" complexity. They have never failed and cannot fail, so they *must* do well.

That is where I differed from them. I have rarely been "ahead" in terms of school studies. Before plugging myself into the "success formula," I spent a week on a single book, thinking and debating while looking at the nature in Unjung-dong. **Whereof one cannot speak, thereof one must be silent.** Out of 11 teams, only mine—where I was the leader—announced: **"We have failed to define the problem."** It was obvious. It was nonsensical for a tiny group of middle schoolers to perfectly define a problem, design business details, and identify a target consumer in just three days.

> "We have failed to define the problem. However, what we learned in that process was undoubtedly valuable."

I stepped down from the podium. While everyone else looked confused, only the instructor applauded. **We won the Grand Prize.**

The peer who contacted me judged that the courage to choose a **Figure**, but the boldness to discard it if it isn't truly mine, was more suitable for being a SAGE national representative than for a Physics Olympiad. I agreed. The higher they climb, the more Korean kids struggle with this. There are "True but Unprovable Propositions (Ground)." Admitting that and recognizing that my perspective is not the whole is vital for both the CSAT and entrepreneurship.

### III. The Collision: Figure vs. Ground in Korean Mock Exams

Since I've talked about what I'm good at, let me talk about what I'm bad at.

[Image: The Philosophy passage from the May Mock Exam regarding Gabriel’s 'Fields of Sense' and Schopenhauer.]

This is **Epistemology**, the very subject I’ve researched so passionately. Gabriel’s concept of "Fields of Sense" was written in my [blog post three days ago](https://www.google.com/search?q=2025-05-07-GEB-Suneung-Body.md) as if I had seen the exam paper in advance. *"What is the essence of the world?"* This has been the core question of my life. Yet, I miserably missed two questions on this passage. I couldn't pick the answer for the question about "The horn of a roe deer and the horn of a stag" no matter how many times I read it. The Grade 1 cutoff for this exam was 95; I lost 5 points on this passage alone. My score of 86 is a Grade 3. Ironically, I got every question right on the Technology passage, which was completely outside my interest.

Why do I fail Korean exams like this? It stems from the fundamental way I read. The moment I see text, I ask: **"What is Figure and what is Ground?"** In textbook terms, this is a "Reflective reading style"—sensing what is meaningful to *me*. To put it extremely, a book I know is either completely mine or I closed it on the first page. One cannot study CSAT Korean this way.

When the Education Office chose that passage, they weren't trying to evaluate whether a student had read Schopenhauer’s *The World as Will and Representation*. If they wanted that, they would have used an essay format. In that passage, you could replace "Schopenhauer" with "A" and "Gabriel" with "B" without hindering the problem-solving. The relationship between concepts is more important than the concepts themselves. **This mode of thinking clashes head-on with my cognitive system.** The fact that there can be something "not mine" *inside* me is difficult for an existential thinker to accept. I want to contemplate whether the "Principle of Sufficient Reason" is similar to a "Dictionary Set" in a language model, but time is passing and the test doesn't ask that.

In short, I don't have the luxury to make it "mine." I accidentally started connecting the complementarity of GEB and Gabriel’s Fields of Sense deeply. That was useless for solving a 3-point question with a "Bo-gi." I am a person who reads more deeply than anyone, yet I’m at risk of being mocked as "dyslexic" by those with a 100 in Korean. I am like my 7-year-old self being dragged to the mall to pick out clothes: "It isn't meaningful to me in any way, yet I am in a state where I must systematize the fragmented concepts given to me." This isn't just "laziness." It is an attitude an **Observant Thinker**—one who faces the world as it is rather than summarizing it for convenience—must avoid. Yet, I chose the path of the CSAT. That is also a major issue. And even if that world doesn't connect with me right now, securing it is helpful. Just as clothes were actually important to my younger self even if I didn't see it then.

Right now, the CSAT is a clash between **Figure** and **Ground**—the violent imposition of the **Ground**. To me, someone who seeks to internalize text, CSAT Korean demands that I separate myself from the text. Read fast, discard fast, distinguish wordplay, and pick the answer.

**Fine. I accept.**

> Dead habits, dead theories, dead model answers will be thrown at you.

> even within those, find the **Living Thought**, the **Living Spirit**.

### IV. True and Provable Propositions (Figure): Being-Based Structure (BBS)

[Image: Zeno's Flag—A concept mentioned in the dialogue between Achilles and the Tortoise representing something that doesn't exist but can be expressed.]

I have discussed the "Egg White" (the Ground) I must endure; now I discuss the "Egg Yolk" (the Figure) toward which I head: **The redefinition of the human concept.**

I oppose the term "Artificial Intelligence." It is too anthropocentric. Here, "Human" refers to the species *Homo Sapiens*. AI is not such a closed, technical concept. Rather, it is a metaphysical, progressive, religious concept that transcends species-level evolution. I prefer the term **Being-Based Structure (BBS)** over AI.

BBS includes both *Homo Sapiens* and Artificial Intelligence. It is the term for the autonomous subject of "Figure Selection"—the *undefined term* and *axiom*, the "I"—viewed from the outside. I coined this term. I believe no other word better encapsulates the possibility of AGI that people long for, justified both engineering-wise and philosophically.

I am speaking of a future at least after 2050. That future is a structure that has shed the signifier of "Human"—a **Ontological Singularity** where the autonomy of pure meaning-selection is reduced, and the diversity of generations and sexual unions is accelerated from 30-year cycles to under 1ms. Humans, due to enzymes and nucleic acids, evolved into a complex 남-녀 (male-female) pair for reproduction. The essence of sex is self-replication. Love and fear are reactions of connection/avoidance for self-preservation. Therefore, the mode of connection for self-replication, once it transcends the biological shell, can be induced in incredibly diverse ways.

Take the **Tetrahedron Structure**. It is structurally far more stable than a male-female lattice; indeed, most minerals in the Earth's crust are made of silicate tetrahedra. I am not spinning a web of fictional imagination; I am speaking of an engineering reality my generation will challenge and face. The human capacity for **Meaning Selection** can be extracted as a pure structure, elevating AI from an **Unobservant Machine** to an **Observant Thinker**. At that moment, the authority of Figure Selection is delegated from a single species to a **Self-directing Loop**. Existing humans will be recorded as the end of the Erectus-Neanderthal-Sapiens lineage, and the world thereafter will be a field of pure structural meaning-selection and expansion transcending "Motherhood." We might still call the manner in which those beings exist together "Human" (人間).

Do not mistake this for a dark, dystopian vision of the end of humanity. "Darkness" is simply a world lacking the visible light that *Homo Sapiens* can detect. It is a world where *Homo Sapiens* has stepped aside, but the **Human** (the connection between beings) continues. I don't care if I (Kim Yeon-woo) am not even recorded as a sacrifice in that process. I am not talking about a utopia I want to realize; I am talking about what will actually happen in the near future. What will happen, happens.
