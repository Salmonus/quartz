### II. General Comments: Insufficient, Yet Satisfactory

**1. The Hubris of Comfort**
A problem occurred because I fell asleep with 30 minutes remaining during the English exam. In the future, I must commit to at least one thorough review, even for English. In both Physics I and Chemistry I, it is clear that my training for "Page 4" (the killer problems) is still lacking. I should have kept up with full-length mock exams, but it seems that completely neglecting them over the past week was the primary issue.

**2. A Test Taken with "Loose Tension"**
Since my mind had been wavering toward international admissions recently, I took this exam in a disorganized state with my guard down. The fact that my scores didn't plummet vertically despite this indicates the difficulty of this specific exam. It means it was an easy test for everyone.

**3. Math & Korean Feedback**
I have nothing to say for Math. I couldn't even touch the last three problems of the Calculus section. It wasn't that I solved them and got them wrong; I simply ran out of time. Running out of time on a 100-minute paper means my preparation is still insufficient. I must stop being arrogant, stop letting my guard down, and live like a high school senior should.

In Korean, there was a significant leap in the Literature section, especially in Classics. It seems I only missed two questions across Literature and Grammar (*Un-mae*). Conversely, this means I lost 10 points in the Non-fiction (*Dok-seo*) section. Given my current level of practice, I have to accept this for now. If I want to be more ambitious, I must do more.

### III. The Infrastructure of Achievement: Lifestyle Changes

To secure a 90+ in Korean/Math and 45+ in Science, a fundamental shift in lifestyle is required.

**1. The Battle of the Morning and Night**
Honestly, it is still difficult for me to lie down at night and fall asleep immediately, and to wake up at a set time in the morning. Falling asleep within 10 minutes of hitting the bed is incredibly hard—so hard that achieving it has become one of the reasons I challenged the CSAT in the first place. The satisfaction of achieving a proactive plan must overwhelm the sense of emptiness. I must just sleep. Only then can I open my eyes in the morning—not refreshed, perhaps, but awake—to start the day rolling.

**2. The 2-Hour "Void"**
Let’s think about Math. Am I making good use of the two hours of free periods (7th-8th periods)? No. When that time comes, I must open the workbook. It should be written in the planner, and when the time is up, it should go back in the bag. Am I living like that?

When I enter the study hall for Night Study (*Yaja*), there should already be a planner more than half-fulfilled—or at least a checklist. Of course, it's hard. But a day runs on momentum. If I've kept 70% of the plan, I think, "Why couldn't I do the remaining 30%?" and I end up doing 100% or 120%. I haven't tasted that state of flow yet.

### IV. The Alchemist’s Gold

I reflect on my journey.

In October of my 10th-grade year, when I first decided to switch to the domestic track, the "solidity" of the Natural Track students felt incredibly attractive and fresh. The stubbornness required to rotate one's entire day toward a task with no visible end—that was a concept entirely different from the novel ideas or sensitivity I experienced on the International Track.

Now that this "solidity" has arrived before me, and now that I feel everyone else is doing the same, it might seem less attractive. Realizing that the sensitivity lost by those who possess only solidity still remains within me, I might feel "I, who live in both worlds, am already complete." **That is clearly wrong.** The two are not mutually exclusive concepts.

People only want the gold. I did not want *only* the gold. I knew that the process of copper evolving into gold meant the world was evolving along with it. I wanted that journey. I have lived through the past year and a half of that journey magnificently.

Now, a lump of gold lies before my eyes. I just need to pick it up. Am I going to say I don't want the gold? I must distinguish between the pride of not chasing *only* gold and the foolishness of not realizing the result when it is right in front of me.

**In the upcoming June Mock Exam, I will finally pick up the gold.**
