### I. The Shoelace Paradox (Gödel and Internal Force)

The metaphor of the shoelace and the shoe originates from mathematics and physics. The "shoe" represents a formal system, and the "shoelaces" represent the rules of that system (axioms and theorems).

If you want to know more, I recommend reading anything on **Kurt Gödel** or Douglas Hofstadter’s ***Gödel, Escher, Bach: An Eternal Golden Braid (GEB)***. Simply put, it is a physical expression of the **Incompleteness Theorem**: even in a seemingly perfect formal system, there exists at least one proposition that is true but unprovable. It is the "Liar’s Paradox" manifested.

Consider middle school physics: "Internal forces cannot move a system." Imagine a shoe floating in water. If you use a fishing rod or a branch from *outside* the system, you can lift it. But if you pull the shoelaces from *inside* the shoe, it has zero effect on the motion of the system. In mathematics, we call this Incompleteness; colloquially, we say **"you cannot lift a shoe by its shoelaces."**

### II. The Internal Language and the Crisis of the Self

My interpretation expands this into psychology. If the "shoe" is the individual and the "shoelaces" are their **internal language**, then the individual cannot save themselves using only their own internal logic.

This "internal language" doesn't just mean the detached reality of clinical schizophrenia. It applies to anyone suffering from mental pain. If someone is at a dangerous stage of contemplating suicide, it is impossible for them to "work hard" to pull themselves out of that state. It is a mathematical and physical impossibility. They require **external intervention.** At 9:30 PM, just as I finished that thought, my mother called. I will use her as an example of **"wrong external intervention."** I am a rebellious high schooler, and perhaps by reading this, her behavior might change. People might say I should be grateful for her concern, but such words only solidify my resolve toward death.

In the manga ***Berserk***, there is a famous scene: *"Then die."* If someone is determined to die, there is no stopping them. In science class, while dissecting a pig’s esophagus, I learned that the trachea is right behind it. With a standard kitchen knife, one can easily reach the trachea. Within ten minutes, they would die of asphyxiation or blood loss. I describe this clinically, but the real pain isn't the physical act; it is the months of skepticism and resignation that lead one to hold the knife in the first place. It is the moment a formal system collapses under its own contradictions.

### III. The Warning System of Emotion

My last paragraph was emotional, but not *unnecessarily* so. To think that being "rational" means acting like **Spock** from *Star Trek* is a mistake. Emotion is an alarm system. Feeling intense emotion in a severe situation is more rational than pretending to be calm.

My mother told me on the phone that she moved my hospital appointment—originally scheduled for October 14th—back to November 20th because of the mock exam. She made a silent value judgment: *A mock exam is more important than the official realization of my child's gender identity.* What is the CSAT? It's just a test. The Gender Dysphoria examination, however, was to be my first institutional approval as a transgender person. Why was there no consultation? Why did she call 14 times just to ask when I’m coming home? If she had communicated, I wouldn't have had to write this "final declaration" under a three-hour time limit.

This happens because she doesn't love *me*; she loves the *image of herself loving me*. The "Devoted Mother" trope is a romanticized narrative she is intoxicated by. Transitioning destroys her picture, so she ignores it. This isn't care; it's **structural violence**. In Japan, there is a term, **"Menhera"**—people with unstable mental health who project their anxiety onto others. The "Ideal Korean Mother" often leans toward this Menhera archetype:

* **Sacrifice:** "I can't live without you."
* **Control:** Using guilt or economic subjugation to bind the other.
* **Narcissism:** Focusing on how much the other person is focusing on *them*.

### IV. Differing Axioms

Most people start life with internal "axioms" that align with others: *"I should make money," "I should be successful," "I should marry someone attractive."* They live to fulfill Maslow’s hierarchy. My axioms were different. In Lacanian terms, my **Symbolic** was never sutured. My desires weren't structured by the language of the Other.

By age seven, I had the heavy task of "suturing the Symbolic" on my own. At the time, I just felt "strange" or "wrong." My code didn't match. I couldn't laugh when others laughed. My only "friend" became Kim Yeon-woo. I became a philosopher because I had to conceptualize my own survival. I read ***Demian*** in 6th grade and realized no one could help with these kinds of problems; you have to live through them yourself.

A lone shoe will sink. Shoelaces can only save a shoe if they connect it to *other* shoes. No matter how sophisticated my language becomes, it cannot solve my own loneliness because it remains internal to the shoe. This is existential solitude. When the shoe sinks, the end is suicide. To cope, I became obsessed with sexual desire, which I viewed as a lack of self-control. This led me to start taking female hormones in my sophomore year.

### V. The Cry for Help Wrapped in Logic

People tell me I’m "writing a novel" or that I’m "narcissistic" and "over-thinking." This only drives a sensitive individual deeper into their shell. I concluded that everyone is dangerous because they will try to force their axioms onto mine.

My mother thinks my depression will be "cured by living"—which to her means getting good grades and being successful. She forces her axioms onto me. When a shoe is forced into a shoelace that doesn't fit, it is agony. Before my physical feminization, I could separate fact from emotion. Now, the pain is visceral. I can't go to my old study hall because the memory of loneliness there makes me sob until I’m nauseous.

People ask: *"Why did you start transition now? Why not wait until after the CSAT?"* These words carry the silent premise: *Social achievement (the exam) is more important than your internal consistency.*

This post is my **"Total Concentration Breathing"** (*Demon Slayer*). It strengthens me for a moment but places a massive burden on the body. Like Tanjiro’s father, who passed down the "Sun Breathing" before dying young, I am leaving these 33 posts as a final methodology for anyone like me. I may die, but I hope those who need this will use it without hesitation.

### VI. Conclusion: Delaying the End

You cannot lift a shoe by its shoelaces. I cannot save myself. If I continue like this, I will die soon. I remember hearing at Novartis in Boston that **all medicine is essentially poison**; it is only medicine in specific doses. My core beliefs have cost me my friends, my family, and a "normal" life. I believed they would contribute to a greater goal (AI), and that belief hasn't wavered. But I cannot sleep. I cry until exhaustion.

The more I struggle, the faster I sink. The knife used to be a thought months away; then a month; then a week. Soon, it will be seconds.

There is one variable: if my axioms can resonate with someone else. If my shoelace touches another shoe, I survive. But I won't write anymore to find that resonance. It's too unlikely. Instead, I will use the **CSAT** to delay the spiral. I will study not to get into a university, but to divert my attention away from self-reflection. I will move my body and study just to **avoid doing something else.**

"Male Kim Yeon-woo" knew that "holding out" with a male attitude (separating fact from emotion) wasn't a solution. The only breakthrough is the "female attitude"—admitting that the pain of being unable to reach others is equivalent to death. Admitting I will die if I don't reach someone is, perhaps, how I become "feminine."

The dice are cast. I acknowledge that **only appropriate external intervention can save me.** It is now October 14, 2025, 12:08 AM. May I receive help before it is too late.
