### I. March Mock Exam Prep Status

It feels unpleasant to have one's unpreparedness pointed out. Conversely, the parts that *are* prepared reveal themselves naturally.

When people ask about my **Korean** studies, I feel unpleasant. Doing well in Korean means managing one's morning condition perfectly, and while I have the will to resolve my sleep issues, I fail time and again.

When people ask if my **Physics** studies are going well, it feels even more unpleasant. I lack sufficient problem-solving practice. While I’m better than I was in January, I still can't touch Page 4. An unimaginable pile of untouched *Stimpack* supplementary problems is staring me down.

When people ask about my **Chemistry** scores, my mood improves. Although the gap between my Chemistry and Physics scores is small, Chemistry is different. Unlike Physics, where I’m building from the ground up, in Chemistry, I can solve missed problems simply by fixing a specific misconception or approach. I can reach Page 4 within 30 minutes.

In **Math**, I am almost overconfident. According to Kang Ki-won’s *Ass* (Assignments), I still make minor mistakes, and there are problems I don't know. However, unlike *Stimpack* or *Hwajaljuice*, which are full of roadblocks, I solve the *Ass* faster than the expected time. I'm not at the CSAT-ready level yet, but the path forward looks better than Science.

I have never "prepared" for **English**, yet I consistently score 100. When I face an "insurmountable wall" in Math or other subjects, I think of my English. It gives me the perspective that I am capable of reaching that level.

The problem isn't Korean, Physics, or Chemistry. The real problem is that my time investment for each subject is **not proportional** to the "unpleasantness" I feel.

> Confidence does not come from wrapping your strong points in thicker armor; it comes from reinforcing your weak points, even if only with leather.

Fear makes me wake up and sit at my desk, but it also prevents me from thinking clearly once I'm there. As students, we constantly seek validation—that we are okay, that we will do well, that we are better than others. We spend most of our time reinforcing what we are already good at, while intentionally turning a blind eye to the minor flaws that could become an Achilles' heel on exam day. We live intensely on the surface, but looking deeper, we are living quite sloppily.

We construct favorable scenarios that could never happen in reality and live trapped in our own worlds. we perform for an invisible crowd and find satisfaction in imagining unheard applause. The only real spectator of that "show" is our future self on the day of the CSAT, and it is doubtful whether that version of ourselves will clap with satisfaction for the person we are today.

**Fear belongs to the prey; confidence belongs to the hunter.** Every student has been the prey at some point. The process of converting anxiety and will into certainty and plans is what "studying" is. Those who do not truly study remain the prey. One must be able to self-edit not just the order of study while sitting down, but every weakness in their life related to the exam.

For instance, I wake up late. That alone lowers my Korean score. I also go to the bathroom frequently during the hours I should be testing on CSAT day. That is a weakness. Anything that breaks immersion is a weakness. Taking a smartphone into the bathroom, for example, is an act a student should never do. Failing to fall asleep when it's time to sleep is also a weakness, as it is directly linked to waking up late. "I can't sleep" is no excuse. If the thoughts keeping you awake are not more important than the CSAT, it is better to sleep.

I am reminded of the crystal shop owner in *The Alchemist*. Students want to do well on the CSAT, but at the decisive moment, they act as if they don't. When faced with their own listed weaknesses, they suddenly think, *"How could I...?"* and perform an act that turns a whole day's investment into bubbles, denying their dream in an instant—even when there's only one more stone to break to find the emerald. The difference in minor habits is the difference in faith, and it ultimately demotes a person from the position of a hunter to that of prey.

I am almost finished with my CSAT preparation. Whether you fight naked or fully prepared, the result can still be death. Even if you survive in a "mostly prepared" state, it was just luck, and that same attitude will eventually bring worse results.

The basis of preparation is confidence. It is about reducing "uncomfortable and unpleasant experiences." It is about discovering and mending the weaknesses that would be embarrassing if others found out. Only then, when the 2026 CSAT paper finally appears before my eyes, will it not be the most uncomfortable and unpleasant experience of my life, but a festival for the prepared—a moment of ecstasy where dreams are realized. I can do this.

### II. The Owl of Minerva

The "Owl of Minerva" signifies that philosophy can evaluate and judge an era only after it has passed, but it cannot predict the future or allow us to prepare for it actively.

Economic hardship is pain. Being unable to dress, eat, or live well is painful in itself, but a greater pain comes from comparison with others. The deficiency in human relationships adds a chilling ache to the heart. Poverty and deficiency are not unrelated. Lacking money makes one stingy and even alienates family relationships.

**Even if lonely, even if poor, let us at least keep our age straight.**

Physically, I am 18, but mentally, age does not exist. My spirit looks out at the world from a much wider perspective, standing on the shoulders of giants. However, because one can only intervene in the present while alive, physical age must be considered. The period during which one can exercise creativity with a fresh body and mind is until age 30. Turing developed the Turing machine in his early 20s while being persecuted as a minority, forming the basis for computers and AI. Einstein's publication of the Theory of Relativity while working at a patent office is too famous to mention—he was also in his early 20s.

It is shameful that I, who am at a similar age and challenging myself based on their achievements, cannot even reach their feet.

Thinking big and projecting oneself onto others who face extreme situations or possess extraordinary talent helps in living the present. It doesn't matter if they are fictional characters. To be able to say that I am a transcendent person who separates physical age from spirit—accepting the mind as a bundle of ego based on materialism and determinism, yet clearly perceiving my role—I must have first overcome the weaknesses listed in Section I.

> "Philosophy, as the thought of the world, does not appear until reality has completed its formative process, and made itself ready. ...When philosophy paints its grey in grey, then has a shape of life grown old. By philosophy's grey in grey it cannot be rejuvenated but only understood. The owl of Minerva spreads its wings only with the falling of the dusk."

This means that only when the day has passed and the dusk of evening falls does the wisdom of philosophy begin to reflect on that day. Henry Ford is revered more than any economist. It means that acting in reality is more meaningful than talking about theories of "this is that" or "that is this." We must participate in reality and act rather than just talk.

> "Oh, Samana, you are clever," said the Exalted One. "You know how to speak cleverly, my friend. Be on your guard against being too clever!"
> — *Siddhartha*
