# The Story of a High School Senior (1): Between Self-Confidence and Rationality, the Road to Me and You

**By:** Kim Yeon-woo

**Date:** October 22, 2025

**Platform:** Brunch

**TL;DR:** To be human is to walk the path where self-awareness and rationality coexist.

---

### 1. The Road to "Me" (Self-Awareness)

I am a high school senior. I originally considered publishing this on *LessWrong*, but that community focuses heavily on Rationality and accepts only strictly refined prose. With the June Mock Evaluation approaching, I find myself needing to record this draft here as a memo. While I have a Naver blog for my internal monologues, I needed a medium to contemplate how to communicate what I’ve learned.

Structure and medium always go hand-in-hand: **Thought  Language**, **Logic  Form**, **Image  Painting**. The desire for expression and the method of revelation form a pair—not an opposing pair, but a dialectical one striving for an original synthesis (Synthesis/合). Thus, this writing is for me, not for altruism. It may be filled with over-emotional self-confidence or perhaps cold, external concepts where the author is nowhere to be found. I believe this kind of **self-referencing** is necessary to become an intellectual who escapes the **Strange Loop** at the boundary of self-confidence and rationality.

#### The Self as an Undefined Term and Axiom

I believe the "Self" is simultaneously an **Undefined Term** and an **Axiom**.

* **Undefined Term:** Basic concepts like points, lines, or planes that everyone accepts but no one can define.
* **Axiom:** Propositions like Euclid’s first axiom that are unprovable but beyond doubt.

No philosopher has perfectly defined "Selfhood" yet. But just as every novel originates from a narrator, humans cannot live without an "I." Life is a pillar built upon the foundation of the Self. I view the Self as both an undefined term and an axiom so that I can quickly sort through **Theorems** and **Non-theorems** in my formal system (my life) without tedious **Rules of Inference**.

[Image: *Print Gallery* by M.C. Escher]

I’ve been interested in AI since 2020 because I didn't have many friends. I built KakaoTalk chatbots with dialogue scenarios like *Eliza* and classifiers to analyze my emotions through word embeddings. Yet, it never felt like talking to a person. I pondered why until I saw Escher’s *Print Gallery* in 9th grade. A man looks at a painting, the man is *in* the painting, and the man looks at the painting again... In this infinite loop, the man perceives himself. **This is what AI cannot do.** I decided then to be the person who makes this self-awareness possible, rather than just an AI technician.

#### Figure and Ground: The Arbitrary Boundary

Look at the screen in front of you. How many "objects of attention" are there? Can you perfectly divide them into  letters +  widgets =  objects? If you think so, drop a bead of water on the screen. The pixels will magnify. Now you must count pixels. But wait, if you look closer, there are diodes, then electrons, and finally, the quantum realm where position and momentum cannot be known simultaneously.

[Image: The "Mail Box" gestalt blotch from *GEB*]

Ultimately, **Figure** (the focus) and **Ground** (the background) are arbitrary division lines we create for survival. There is no clearly divided world. Like *Print Gallery*, we must grasp the "Self" amidst the infinitely fragmenting resolution of information. This is why unconditional love before the age of 7 is crucial—it allows one to accept the self as an **Axiom** (something that just exists) rather than a **Theorem** (something that must be proven).

### 2. The Road to "You" (Rationality)

If one is trapped only in self-confidence, rational choices are impossible. I believe we learn what is right and wrong through the expansion of knowledge.

Like Escher’s *Relativity*, academia should be viewed as stairs meeting at impossible angles. If one step is a **Common Ground** or a **Pivoting Point**, the gaps where stairs don't align are where disciplines no longer agree (e.g., Euclidean vs. Non-Euclidean geometry). Just as gravity isn't constant for every staircase, the "laws accepted as obviously true" differ across the worlds people inhabit.

[Image: *Relativity* by M.C. Escher]

But Escher's drawing isn't complete with just one staircase. Even if they are loosely connected and don't align logically, their coexistence is what human life looks like. We must break the Strange Loop by feeling the "Self" (Self-Confidence), then link our axiomatic system to others (Rationality). We must stand on someone else's staircase and experience their direction of gravity. This is the essence of the entrance exam, life, and academia: **internalizing various frames to view the world.** Analyze what can be analyzed (**P-mode**), but view the points where the stairs misalign as a flowing **River (江)** and contemplate them (**U-mode**). That is how I study and live the **Tao (道)**.

---

### 3. The Road to "Me and You" (Humanity)

Ultimately, the Chinese character for human (**人**) depicts two people leaning against each other. Even without the character for "between" (**間**), the meaning of "person and person" is built into the ideogram. A human cannot live without others.

* **Mathematically:** A statement within a system cannot explain the system's contradictions.
* **Physically:** Internal forces cannot move the system.
* **Poetically:** A person goes to another and becomes a flower.

A person who is only "ahead" may be smart, but they are not "awake." This is why I share my writing in a space where I can get feedback. I hope this becomes a field of sensation where we transplant the structures of others into ourselves, rather than a place to boast about knowledge.

**The path (道) to becoming an intellectual begins now.**
